# Curated translation candidates: Mesopotamian texts beyond the flood stories

Compiled 2026-10-16, after the flood corpus (`סיפורי-המבול-המסופוטמיים/`) was finished.
It is based on two surveys, both kept in `curation-surveys/`:
- **`relevance-survey--2026-10-16.md`**: about 60 Mesopotamian texts that scholarship connects with the Torah. Each entry has the specific parallels, the key studies and how well accepted the link is.
- **`availability-survey--2026-10-16.md`**: what can actually be obtained, checked against eBL (all 300 chapters), ORACC, ETCSL, CDLI, SEAL and public-domain or open-access books. It gives line counts, a measured preservation figure, licences, and the access mechanisms that work.

## How the list is ordered

**Relevance comes first**: how much a word-by-word reader of the Torah gains from a careful Hebrew translation with an apparatus (guidelines §1).

**Update (2026-10-19): the user's interest is wider than the Bible.** In the user's words, it is "the soul of the ancient": how the Mesopotamians thought, felt, prayed and lived (guidelines §1, second purpose). The Bible ordering below stays, but from the next round a text also ranks high when it is a strong window onto the ancient mind, even with no Torah parallel. Tier 3 ("to expand horizons") is therefore **not** a low tier for this user; texts such as Ludlul, the Theodicy, the Descent of Ištar, the city laments, the love lyrics, the proverbs and the letters are full candidates.
Within that, the list prefers what the user asked for:
- texts in **good condition**;
- **medium or large** texts, or several short texts that make one corpus together.

Short texts appear only as part of a corpus, or as "quick wins" at the end.

Where the commentary stands matters too. The current work is **Gen 1** (the fifth day: the *tanninim*). **Gen 8:20–11:26** has no commentary yet. The legal and ritual blocks are drafted but thin.

Legend: **Lines** = edition lines; **Cond.** = condition, as measured in the availability survey; **Base** = the base text we would translate (guidelines §3, rank 1).

## Tier 1: translate these (high relevance, good base text, good condition)

### 1. Enūma eliš, the Babylonian Epic of Creation (Tablets I–VII)
- **Lines:** 1,098, Akkadian (SB).
- **Cond.:** excellent. Tablets I, IV, VI and VII are ≥ 90% intact; V is the weakest.
- **Base:** eBL `L/1/2` (Heinrich), with 23–75 manuscripts per tablet and English on every line. King 1902 and Langdon 1923 are public domain, for history.
- **Torah:** Gen 1 almost word by word:
  - *tehom* / Tiāmat (a cognate, not a loan);
  - the sky made from half of Tiāmat, with a bar so that "her waters should not escape" ↔ the *raqiaʿ* (1:6–8);
  - Tiāmat's eleven monsters ↔ the *created* tanninim (1:21, the unit now being edited);
  - the stars, months and moon phases (V) ↔ 1:14–18;
  - man made from Qingu's blood to bear the gods' toil ↔ 1:26–28;
  - the gods' rest ↔ 2:1–3;
  - naming as an act of rule;
  - the bricks of Esagil and its "raised head" ↔ 11:3–4;
  - Marduk's bow set in heaven ↔ 9:13;
  - the divine warrior at the sea ↔ Ex 15.
- **Why:** it is the main external control for Gen 1, the text the commentary is working on now.

### 2. Creation and Eden companions (a small corpus with Enūma eliš)
- **Adapa** (eBL `L/1/7`: MB 78 + SB 97 lines; MB good). The bread and water of life refused, the garment, and wisdom without immortality ↔ Gen 2:17, 3:4–5, 3:21–22.
- **Enki and Ninmaḫ** (ETCSL 1.1.2, 142 lines; 31% damaged). Man formed from clay to free the gods from toil ↔ Gen 2:7, 2:15. The disabled people the gods made, each given a role ↔ Ex 4:11, Lev 21. *The Ex 4:11 link appears unpublished.*
- **Song of the Hoe** (ETCSL 5.5.4, 110 lines; excellent). Heaven separated from earth, and humans sprouting from the ground ↔ Gen 2:7, 3:19.
- **Enki and Ninḫursaĝ** (ETCSL 1.1.1, 305 lines; 21% damaged).
  - Dilmun, the pure land ↔ Eden.
  - Water brought up from the earth ↔ 2:6.
  - The forbidden plants eaten, and the curse ↔ 2:17, 3:6.
  - Nin-ti, "lady of the rib / of life" ↔ 2:21–22, 3:20 (debated).
- **The bilingual "Creation of the World by Marduk"** (CT 13 35–38, about 40 lines; good). It opens with a chain of "not yet": "no reed had come forth…" ↔ 2:4b–5. Base text: King 1902 (public domain) and the literature; check eBL and CDLI for a newer edition.
- **KAR 4** (about 70 lines; SEAL has a 191-line entry that needs checking). Humans grown from the blood of slain gods, and set to work the fields ↔ 1:26–28, 2:15.
- **Grain and Sheep (Lahar and Ašnan)**, the cosmogonic prologue (ETCSL 5.3.2, 193 lines; very good). Primeval humans "ate grass like sheep" with no bread and no clothing. Then grain and the ewe were given ↔ Gen 2–4, 9:3. This bears directly on the commentary's reading of Gen 2–4 as culture history.
- **Why:** the Gen 1–3 block is the commentary's centre. These texts together are about 1,000 lines, mostly well preserved.

### 3. Esarhaddon's Succession Treaty (SAA 2 6), with the Tayinat copy
- **Lines:** 691, Akkadian (NA, 672 BCE).
- **Cond.:** excellent (4% of words in brackets).
- **Base:** ORACC `saao/saa02` Q009186 (CC BY-SA 3.0); the English translation is in the HTML view. The Tayinat copy is P500551.
- **Torah:**
  - Deut 13: denouncing the prophet, the dreamer and the family member, and the rule not to add or take away (13:1).
  - Deut 6:5: "love".
  - Deut 28:26–35: the sequence of curses (Sîn → leprosy, Šamaš → blindness, the bride taken, the corpse for the birds).
  - Deut 28:23: bronze sky and iron earth, in reverse order.
  - Deut 28:53–57: cannibalism.
  - Deut 29:13–14: the oath binding future generations.
  - Lev 26:19.
- **Why:** these are among the most widely accepted lexical and structural parallels to the Torah, and they control *word order*, which is exactly what the commentary asks about.

### 4. The Laws of Hammurabi (prologue, about 282 laws, epilogue)
- **Lines:** 3,641 composite lines (about 1,400 verse-lines of text), Akkadian (OB).
- **Cond.:** excellent.
- **Base:** CDLI P464358, a composite with a normalization and English on every line, reusable with attribution. Check against the Louvre stele (CDLI P249253) and SEAL 7520 (prologue and epilogue, Streck).
- **Torah:** the whole Covenant Code (Ex 21–23), and much of Deut 19–25:
  - the goring ox, §250–252;
  - talion, §196–201;
  - deposit and the oath "before the god", §120–126;
  - herding liability, §261–267 ↔ Ex 22:9–12, Gen 31:38–40;
  - the river ordeal, §132 ↔ Num 5;
  - the unsolved murder, §23–24 ↔ Deut 21;
  - debt slavery, §117 ↔ Ex 21:2;
  - incest, §154–158 ↔ Lev 18;
  - the surrogate slave-wife, §144–147 ↔ Gen 16;
  - the prologue and epilogue (justice, the stele, blessings and curses) ↔ the covenant frame.
- **Why:** hundreds of exact contact points. The Torah's deviations (no class tiers, the ox stoned, no vicarious punishment) are exactly the "strange word" the commentary looks for.

### 5. Gilgameš, Standard Babylonian Tablets I–X and XII, with the OB Tablets II–III (XI is done)
- **Lines:** about 2,350 (SB) + 364 (OB II–III), Akkadian.
- **Cond.:** good overall. I and VI are good; IV is poor (59% of words in brackets); X is 34% and XII 30%. OB II is very good.
- **Base:** eBL `L/1/4` (George, 2026). The Hebrew must match the existing Tablet XI in the flood corpus.
- **Torah:**
  - Enkidu formed from clay (I) ↔ Gen 2:7.
  - His humanization by Šamḫat (sex, bread, beer, clothing; the animals flee him): "you have become like a god" ↔ Gen 2:20, 3:5–7, 3:21, 9:2.
  - Gilgameš, two-thirds divine, oppressing Uruk, who wants "to make a name" ↔ Gen 6:1–4, 11:4.
  - The seven years of chaff and the stored grain (VI) ↔ Gen 41.
  - Enkidu's curse and blessing of the harlot (VII) ↔ the curse speeches of Gen 3.
  - The quest for life, and Siduri ↔ Gen 3:22.
  - The guardian of the cedar forest ↔ the cherubim (3:24).
  - The raising of Enkidu's ghost (XII) ↔ Deut 18:11.
- **Why:** it is the ANE template for the passage from animal life to culture, which is central to the commentary's reading of Gen 2–4. It is also the best-known text in the corpus, and it completes the epic whose Tablet XI we already have.

### 6. The other law collections: Eshnunna, Middle Assyrian Laws A, Lipit-Ištar, Ur-Namma
- **Laws of Eshnunna** (CDLI P480696, 195 lines; good; normalization + English). §53 is nearly verbatim **Ex 21:35** ("they shall divide the price of the live ox and the dead"), the closest verbal parallel between biblical and cuneiform law. §54–57 has the goring ox with warning, and the vicious dog; §12–13 the thief by day and by night ↔ Ex 22:1–2.
- **Middle Assyrian Laws A** (ORACC `tcma`, P281779; 828 tablet lines; good; English by de Ridder 2021; CC BY-SA). Their Torah parallels:
  - §8, the woman's hand cut off ↔ Deut 25:11–12;
  - rape in the city vs in the field ↔ Deut 22:23–27;
  - the unbetrothed virgin ↔ Deut 22:28–29, Ex 22:15–16;
  - levirate-type marriage ↔ Deut 25:5–10, Gen 38;
  - veiling ↔ Gen 24:65, 38:14;
  - witchcraft ↔ Ex 22:17;
  - counted blows ↔ Deut 25:3.
- **Lipit-Ištar** (CDLI P464355, 503 lines; good) and **Ur-Namma** (CDLI P432130, 456 lines; fair). These are Sumerian, with prologues and epilogues; compensation for bodily injury ↔ Ex 21:18–27.
- **Why:** together with item 4 they make a full "cuneiform law" corpus of about 2,000 lines, which is the comparative background of the whole legal Torah.

## Tier 2: strong candidates for a later round

### 7. Gudea Cylinders A and B (building of the Eninnu temple)
- **Lines:** about 1,300, Sumerian (c. 2125 BCE).
- **Cond.:** very good.
- **Base:** ORACC `etcsri` Q000377 (CC BY-SA) and ETCSL 2.1.7.
- **Torah:** Ex 25–40:
  - the plan revealed in a dream ↔ *tavnit*, 25:9, 40;
  - the people's purity and harmony during the building;
  - materials from distant lands, and the skilled craftsmen (Bezalel);
  - the god's entry into the house ↔ 40:34–35;
  - the seven-day dedication, with social equality ↔ Lev 8–9, Lev 25.

  Hurowitz 1985 and 1992 is the standard comparison.
- **Why:** the Tabernacle block has about 40 commentary units, many of them stubs.

### 8. Maqlû (I–VIII + Ritual Tablet), and Šurpu II–III
- **Maqlû:** 1,595 lines, Akkadian (SB); very good. Base: eBL `Mag/1/1` (Abusch) and ORACC `cmawro/maqlu`.
  - *Kaššāptu* is the cognate of `מְכַשֵּׁפָה` ↔ Ex 22:17 and Deut 18:10.
- **Šurpu:** the only legal full edition found is Zimmern 1901 (public domain, old).
  - Its list of sins ↔ Lev 5:1–4 (the unwitting oath, touching impurity), Lev 19, the Decalogue.
- Šurpu is more relevant, but its base text is weak. Look for Reiner 1958 in a library.

### 9. Etana, and Nergal and Ereškigal
- **Etana:** eBL `L/1/9`, 585 lines (OB, MA, SB); fair.
  - The barren king and the "plant of birth" ↔ the barren matriarchs and Reuben's mandrakes (Gen 30:14–16).
  - The eagle's ascent ↔ Gen 11:4 and Deut 32:11.
- **Nergal and Ereškigal:** eBL `L/1/6`, 87 + 333 lines; fair. The MB version has German only.
  - The "long stairway of heaven" (*simmiltu*) ↔ Gen 28:12, 17.

### 10. Sumerian culture and debate texts
- **Enki and the World Order** (465 lines; 32% damaged). The world is organized and domains are allotted ↔ Gen 1, Deut 32:8.
- **The debate poems**: Bird and Fish (191; ↔ the fifth day!), Hoe and Plough (201; very good), Summer and Winter (295; ↔ Gen 4:3–5?).
- **The Farmer's Instructions** (111; excellent) ↔ Gen 3:17–19, 9:20.
- **Enmerkar and the Lord of Aratta** (641; fair). The "spell of Nudimmud": the one language and its confusion ↔ Gen 11:1–9 (debated); the heavy mouth ↔ Ex 4:10.
- **Why:** these are the ANE's own culture-history myths, which matter especially for this commentary's thesis. Enmerkar is for Gen 11, a block that has no commentary yet.

### 11. Assyrian prophecy and royal inscriptions
- **SAA 9 Assyrian Prophecies** (ORACC; 11 texts; SAA 9 1 is 214 lines). "Fear not" ↔ Gen 15:1, 26:24, Deut 31:6–8; prophecy laws ↔ Deut 13, 18.
- **Sennacherib's annals**, RINAP 3 no. 22 (502 lines; excellent). Primarily for Kings and Isaiah; for the Torah, conquest rhetoric ↔ Deut 2–3, 20.

### 12. City laments and the Curse of Agade
- **Lament for Ur** (ETCSL 2.2.2, 442 lines; excellent).
- **Lament for Sumer and Ur** (2.2.3, 524; good).
- **Curse of Agade** (2.1.5, 321; very good).
- **Torah:** Lev 26:31–33; Deut 28:49–68 ("a nation of fierce countenance"); 29:21–27 ("why has the LORD done this?"). Primarily, Lamentations.

### 13. Justice and wisdom
- **The Great Prayer to Šamaš** (eBL `L/3/4`, 200 lines; very good). Two sets of weights ↔ Lev 19:35–36 and Deut 25:13–16; bribery ↔ Ex 23:8; interest ↔ Ex 22:24.
- **Advice to a Prince** (eBL `L/2/5`, 55; excellent) ↔ Deut 17:14–20, the law of the king (under-studied).
- **Counsels of Wisdom** (159; good).
- **The full Instructions of Šuruppag** (ETCSL 5.6.1, 288; fair), already partly in the flood corpus. "Do not move a boundary" ↔ Deut 19:14, 27:17.

## Tier 3: to expand horizons (low Torah relevance, high literary value)
- **Ludlul bēl nēmeqi** I–V (eBL, 534 lines), the **Babylonian Theodicy** (288), the Sumerian and OB **"Man and his God"**: for Job, and for Deut 28's diseases.
- **The Descent of Ištar** (eBL, 159) and **Inana's Descent** (ETCSL, 440; good): the netherworld ↔ *sheʾol*.
- **Anzû** (694), **Poor Man of Nippur** (161), **Cuthaean Legend** (345), **Dialogue of Pessimism** (93), **Erra I** (191; Tablets II–V are not in eBL), **Lugalbanda II** (422; very good), **Bilgameš and Aga** (117; very good), **Agušaya** (SEAL), the **OB love lyrics** (ORACC AkkLove; for the Song of Songs).

## Quick wins (short but very relevant; add to the corpus they fit)
- **The Sargon Birth Legend** (about 30 lines; NA) ↔ Ex 2:1–10: a basket of rushes sealed with *kupru* (bitumen), cast on the river, drawn out. It is not in eBL or SEAL. King 1907 (public domain) has the text; Lewis 1980 is ©. It fits the flood corpus's echo control (*kupru* / `כֹּפֶר`, and `תֵּבָה`).
- **The Edict of Ammiṣaduqa** (about 200 lines; OB) ↔ Lev 25 (*andurāru* / `דְּרוֹר`), Deut 15. Its availability is not yet verified.
- **The akītu ritual, day 5** (*kuppuru* with a ram's carcass; the ritual agents banished until the festival ends) ↔ Lev 16. Thureau-Dangin 1921 is the edition; its copyright status needs checking.
- **The OB herding contract** YBC 5944 ↔ Gen 31:38–40, Ex 22:9–12.
- **The "120 years" lifespan texts / the Ballad of Early Rulers** ↔ Gen 6:3, Deut 34:7. No open edition was found (see `סיפורי-המבול-המסופוטמיים/6-אזכורי-המבול/project.md`).

## Methodological "negative" set (parallels that largely fail)
These are worth a short treatment to show *why* they fail, which suits the commentary's demand for rigour. The relevance survey §3 has the details:
- the Nuzi wife-sister and teraphim contracts;
- the Chedorlaomer texts;
- Dumuzi and Enkimdu ↔ Cain and Abel;
- the "rib" pun in Enki and Ninḫursaĝ.

## Out of scope (not Mesopotamian), but among the most important comparanda
These are listed in the relevance survey §4:
- the Ugaritic Baal Cycle (Gen 1:21 *tanninim*, Leviathan, Deut 32:8–9);
- the Deir ʿAllā Balaam text (Num 22–24);
- Sefire and Tell Fekheriye (Fekheriye is half Akkadian; Lev 26:26);
- Hittite rituals (Lev 16) and Hittite treaties;
- the Ebla scapegoat texts;
- Egyptian texts for Gen 37–50 and Ex 1–14.

If the folder's scope ever widens, the Baal Cycle and Deir ʿAllā come first.

**Update (2026-10-21):** the rest of the world is now surveyed in [`curated-translation-candidates--world.md`](curated-translation-candidates--world.md) (ten regional surveys in `curation-surveys/world-survey--*`). The Baal Cycle is its first recommendation; Deir ʿAllā is in its Tier 1 #6.
