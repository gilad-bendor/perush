# Project: Gilgameš SB Tablet XI – Ūta-napišti's Flood Story → Hebrew

Read `../../translation-guidelines.md` first, then `../corpus.md` and the shared `../renderings.md`. This file holds only what is specific to this text.

## Scope
- **Text:** Poem of Gilgameš, Standard Babylonian ("Sîn-lēqi-unninni") version, **Tablet XI, lines 1–208**. That is Gilgameš's question (1–7), Ūta-napišti's account of the flood (8–206), and the transition back to Gilgameš's quest (207–208).
- Lines 209–328 (the sleep test, the plant of rejuvenation, the return to Uruk) are not part of the flood story and are out of scope (decided 2026-10-16; can be added later).
- **Parallel texts** to cite in the apparatus, not to translate here:
  - Atrahasis OB III (Wasserman 2020 / SEAL 1517). Gilg. XI is largely a reworking of it: XI 197 even names the hero "Atra-ḫasīs".
  - The Sumerian Flood Story (ETCSL 1.7.4).
  - Berossus fr. 7 (DFHG).
  - Each is now a project of its own in this corpus (`../corpus.md`). Lines shared with Atra-ḫasīs III, the Ark Tablet or Ugarit get identical Hebrew there.

- XI 24 *uqur bīta bini eleppa* "demolish the house, build a boat" ↔ Gen 6:14 `עֲשֵׂה לְךָ תֵּבַת`
- XI 55, 66–67 *kupru* bitumen, poured outside and inside ↔ `וְכָפַרְתָּ אֹתָהּ מִבַּיִת וּמִחוּץ בַּכֹּפֶר` (6:14) [cognate]
- XI 57–63: dimensions; six decks, seven parts, nine compartments ↔ 6:15–16 measurements; `תַּחְתִּיִּם שְׁנִיִּם וּשְׁלִשִׁים` [divergence]
- XI 81–86: loading family, kin, craftsmen, animals ↔ 6:18–21; 7:1–3
- XI 89, 94: "go into the boat and seal your hatch" / "I sealed my hatch" ↔ 7:16 `וַיִּסְגֹּר יְהוָה בַּעֲדוֹ` [divergence: who shuts the door]
- XI 115–126: the gods terrified; Bēlet-ilī's regret (XI 119–120: "the past has turned to clay, because I spoke evil in the assembly of the gods") ↔ 6:6–7 `וַיִּנָּחֶם … וַיִּתְעַצֵּב אֶל לִבּוֹ` [divergence]
- XI 128–131: six days and seven nights; on the seventh day the storm subsides ↔ 7:4, 7:10–12, 8:1–2
- XI 135: "all the people had turned to clay" ↔ 7:21–23 `וַיִּגְוַע כָּל בָּשָׂר` … `וַיִּמַח אֶת כָּל הַיְקוּם`
- XI 137, 142–146: a vent opened, sunlight on his face; the boat grounds on Mount Nimuš ↔ 8:4 `הָרֵי אֲרָרָט`; 8:6 `וַיִּפְתַּח נֹחַ אֶת חַלּוֹן הַתֵּבָה`
- XI 147–156: dove, swallow, raven ↔ 8:6–12: raven, dove ×3 [divergence: order and number]
- XI 157–163: the sacrifice; "the gods smelled the sweet savor" ↔ 8:20–21 `וַיָּרַח יְהוָה אֶת רֵיחַ הַנִּיחֹחַ` [equivalent / near-verbal]
- XI 164–167: lapis "flies" as a remembrance ↔ 9:12–17 the bow as a sign of remembrance [divergence]
- XI 183–195: "on him who commits a sin, inflict his crime!" – lesser punishments (lion, wolf, famine, Erra) instead of a flood ↔ 8:21–22 `לֹא אֹסִף עוֹד` [divergence]
- XI 199–206: the blessing; the survivor and his wife become like gods ↔ 9:1 `וַיְבָרֶךְ אֱלֹהִים אֶת נֹחַ וְאֶת בָּנָיו` [divergence: immortality vs. mortality]

**Corrections after drafting (2026-10-16):**
- "Poured outside and inside" (XI 66–67) is not quite what Gilg. XI says. It pours *kupru* "into the kiln" (66) and tips *iṭṭû* "inside" (67, *ana libbi*: inside the kiln or the boat, unclear). It is the **Ark Tablet** (Ark 18–19: *kidātiša … qirbiša*) that has "outside … inside", the real match for `מִבַּיִת וּמִחוּץ`.
- XI 135 is "all *tenēšētu* (people)", not *awīlūtu*.
- XI 164–167: the "flies" are lapis beads, lifted by Bēlet-ilī. The motif of a remembrance object is shared with 9:12–17 [equivalent]; the content differs [divergence].
- XI 183–195: Ea's alternatives (lion, wolf, famine, Erra) are offered for the *future* (George: precatives), which brings the passage closer to 8:21–22 and to Ezek 14:21 (`חֶרֶב וְרָעָב וְחַיָּה רָעָה וָדֶבֶר`).

## Decisions (user, 2026-10-16)
1. **Niqqud:** partial (guidelines default). The translation lines are unvocalized, in plene spelling. Niqqud only for names at their first occurrence, archaic or ambiguous words (`אֹמַר`, `שָׁאר`, `לָמוּשׁ`, `כָּלָא`, `נָח`, `עוּץ`…), and Hebrew cognates the reader must notice (`כֹּפֶר`, `חֵמָר`, `פְּתִיל`). Bible quotations in the apparatus keep full niqqud (coordinator note, 2026-10-16).
2. **Uruk:** `אוּרוּךְ`, with a note that it is `אֶרֶךְ` (בראשית י:י). (Uruk does not occur in ll. 1–208.)

4. **Scope:** lines 1–208.

## Line numbering
- eBL/George throughout. Two special cases: **74a** (one manuscript only, NinNA4a = T1, nothing translatable), and **132–133a / 133b**. George moves the line break: all manuscripts divide after *ḫayyalti*, but the metre requires the division after *tâmtu*. So the edition prints "132–133a" (*ša imtaḫṣu kīma ḫayyalti inūḫ tâmtu*) and "133b". We follow it, and the notes mention Streck's objection.
- Brackets follow George's translation and the ATF, not only the normalization line. eBL's normalization omits brackets where every manuscript is broken (e.g. l. 81 *mimma īšû*).

## Translation decisions (this session)
1. **Tense.** Akkadian presents in narrative (the storm, 100–107, 129; the raven, 156) → Hebrew participles, so the durative is visible. Preterites/perfects → Hebrew past. Statives → adjectives or passive participles (`מֻשְׁבָּע` 19; `רבוצים` 116).
2. **Repetitions** are identical to the letter: 1/8, 36–37/177–178/181–182, 46–47/88/91, 48/97, 130/147, 143–146, 148/151/154, 149/152, 150/153, 161–163, 188/190/192/194, 189/191, 193/195, 205/206.
3. **Echo control, decisions that cost something:**
   - `חולל` for *šakānu abūba* (not `הביא`, which would echo 6:17).
   - `קָרֵה` for *ṣullulu* (not `כסה`, which would echo `מִכְסֵה`, 8:13).
   - `גופה` for *lānu* (not `קומתה`, which would echo 6:15).
   - `סיפונים` for *ruggubu* (not `קומות` / `תחתיים`).
   - `נשענה` for *ītemid* (142) and `נָח` only for *inūḫ* (133a), so the real cognate נוח stands alone.
   - `הרפתה` for *ittaraq* (131; not `שככה`, which would echo 8:1).
   - `נהפך לטיט` (not `שב לטיט`, which would echo 3:19).
   - `מקום עמידה` for *manzāzu* (not `מנוח`, 8:9).
   - `שפל המים` for *qarūru* (not `קלו` / `חסרו` / `יבשת`).
   - `מנחה` for *surqinnu* (not `קטורת`, which the OB *qutrinnu* needs).
   - `סלים` for *sussullu* (never `תבות`).
   - `פתח־אוויר` for *nappašu* (shared; not `חלון` / `צהר`).
4. **Hidden cognates surfaced:** `כֹּפֶר` (55, 66), `זרע` (27, 84), `נפש` (26–27, 175), `מדד`/`מידות` (29), `פתח` (137), `נָח` (133a), `כָּלָא` (133b), `לקח` (206), `מנה לכליון` (171), `נמלך` (170, 184), `קרקע` (41), `פתיל` (54), `צחק` (165), `ציפורים` (44), `עורב` (154).
5. **Ištar in 117** = "the goddess" (George, Wasserman), with a footnote.
6. **Crux 149/152** (*īpiram-ma*): `*חיפשה מזון*?` (George's tentative *epēru*), with the alternatives listed in the apparatus.
7. **l. 129:** the eBL normalization follows ms. J1 (*šāru abūbu meḫû*). Most manuscripts (and OB III iv 25) have *šāru rādu meḫû abūbu* "wind, rain, storm, flood". We translate the edition and note the variant.
8. **l. 126:** the edition follows ms. T1 (*ina nurub nissati*). Most manuscripts read *ilū ašru ašbi ina bikīti*. Noted.

## Lessons for the guidelines
- **Bible quotations: check them by script, and normalize Unicode first (NFD/NFC).** Hand-typed niqqud often has a different combining-mark order from the `תנך-מנוקד` files, so a plain substring test fails even when the text is identical. My checker (session scratchpad `chk.py`/`chk2.py`) splits quotations on "…" and also checks that the cited verse matches. It is worth adding to `tools/`.
- **eBL normalization lines don't show every restoration.** When all manuscripts are broken at the same place, the `%n` line may still show the word unbracketed (XI 81). Use the eBL translation's brackets and the ATF.
- **eBL lemmatization slips:** XI 160 *attabak* is lemmatized as *bakû* "weep" (it is *tabāku*); 6 *aḫī* as *aḫu* "brother" (it is *aḫu* II "arm"). Don't trust the gloss without parsing.
- **The eBL composite sometimes follows a minority manuscript** (XI 126 ms T1; XI 129 ms J1). Wasserman's apparatus shows this quickly; record it.
- **Niqqud workflow:** draft unvocalized and in plene spelling from the start. Stripping niqqud from vocalized drafts leaves defective spelling (`דבר`, `מדותיך`, `הגבור`) and also deletes the maqaf (U+05BE lies inside the niqqud range).
- **Modern-Hebrew false friends** of good biblical cognates: `סבל` "carry" reads as "suffer"; `נמלך` is fine; `בקש נפש` means "seek to kill". Check every cognate rendering for its modern sense.
- **Echo control produced real finds** that the standard English translations hide: *kalû* / `כלא` (XI 133b ↔ 8:2) and *nâḫu* / `נח` (133a ↔ 8:4). A systematic check of every verb in the Genesis 8 scene against the Akkadian lexicon is worth doing for each version.
- Tchernichovsky 1924 is a useful negative model for echo control: it uses `מנוח לכף רגלה`, `ריח הניחוח`, `העולה` where the Akkadian has none of them.
