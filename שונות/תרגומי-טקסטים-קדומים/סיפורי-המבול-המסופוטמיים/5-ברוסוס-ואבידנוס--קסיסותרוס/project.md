# Project: Berossus and Abydenus — the Hellenistic Babylonian flood story (Xisouthros / Seisithros) → Hebrew

Read `../../translation-guidelines.md` first, then `../corpus.md` and the shared `../renderings.md`. This file holds only what is specific to this text.

## Scope
Berossus, priest of Bel at Babylon, wrote the *Babyloniaka* in Greek for Antiochus I (c. 290–278 BCE). The work is lost. It survives in excerpts by Alexander Polyhistor (1st cent. BCE), which reach us through (a) George Syncellus (Greek, c. 800 CE) and (b) Eusebius's *Chronicle*, whose Greek is lost but which survives in an Armenian translation (we have it only in the Latin version printed by Müller). Abydenus (2nd cent. CE?) retold the same material in Ionic Greek; he reaches us through Syncellus and Eusebius (Greek *Praep. Ev.*; Armenian *Chronicle*).

- **Checked against the scan** of Müller's page (`../../sources/Berossus/Mueller-FHG-II/`, PDF pp. 565–567 = FHG II pp. 500–502) for fr. 7 and fr. 6. **Findings:**

## The Septuagint layer (new source)
Berossus wrote in Greek in the early 3rd century BCE; the Pentateuch was translated into Greek in Alexandria in the same century. Where their vocabulary coincides, the parallels index records a separate layer: **Berossus ↔ LXX ↔ Hebrew**. Text: Rahlfs 1935, from Greek Wikisource, saved in `../../sources/LXX-Rahlfs-1935--el-wikisource/` (README there; status licence-unclear, so only the README should be committed). Main coincidences found: κατακλυσμός (= מַבּוּל throughout), ὁ κατακλυσμὸς ἐγένετο (7:6, 17), βρώματα (6:21), μῆκος / πλάτος (6:15), ἄσφαλτος (6:14 for כֹּפֶר; 11:3, 14:10 for חֵמָר), ὄρνεα (6:20; 9:2, 10), εὑρίσκω in the bird scene (8:9), καθίζω (8:4, the ark "sat"), κοπάζω (Abydenus; LXX 8:1, 8, 11), ἰδεῖν εἰ (8:7–8 ~ Abydenus εἴ κου … ἴδοιεν), Χαλδαῖοι (11:28, 31), Ἐρυθρὰ θάλασσα, Ἡλίου πόλις (41:45), Ἀρμενία (Isa 37:38). Divergences: the LXX's κιβωτός (none of Berossus's three ship-words), θυσιαστήριον (Berossus βωμός), ἀπέστειλεν / ἐξαπέστειλεν (Berossus ἀφίημι, Abydenus μεθίημι; ἐξαφίημι occurs in neither), περιστερά / κόραξ (Berossus and Abydenus name no species).

## Decisions
1. **Ship-words.** πλοῖον = `סְפִינָה` (shared rendering); σκάφος = `כְּלִי־שַׁיִט` (Berossus uses it only in the command and the building, §§2–3); ναῦς = `אֳנִיָּה` (once, B7.4.5). Latin *navis* = `סְפִינָה`, *navigium* = `כְּלִי־שַׁיִט`. Never `תֵּבָה`: the LXX's κιβωτός, which renders `תֵּבָה`, appears only in Syncellus's own heading (fr. 1: "about Noah and the ark") [divergence]. λάρναξ (the Deucalion "chest") does not occur in these fragments.
2. **κατακλυσμός = `מַבּוּל`** [equivalent; LXX identical: κατακλυσμός renders `מַבּוּל` throughout Gen 6–11]. Latin *diluvium* = `מַבּוּל` too.
3. **ἄσφαλτος = `כֹּפֶר`** (B7.7.2). Hebrew has no neutral word for bitumen; the LXX renders `כֹּפֶר` (6:14) and `חֵמָר` (11:3; 14:10) both as ἄσφαλτος, and the Babylonian original behind Berossus is *kupru* (Gilg. XI 55, 66). Corpus consistency decides for `כֹּפֶר`, with a note.
4. **θεοί = `אֵלִים`, θεός = `הָאֵל`** (shared: never `אֱלֹהִים`). Κρόνος = `קְרוֹנוֹס`, noted as Ea/Enki.
5. **ἄνθρωποι = `בְּנֵי הָאָדָם`** (mankind, the destroyed and the taught). ἀνήρ occurs once (B8.2, δίκαιος ἀνήρ = `אִישׁ צַדִּיק`).
6. **Names of the kings** are transcribed from the Greek (fr. 6 and Abydenus), with the cuneiform names in a note, not substituted. Χαλδαῖοι = `כַּשְׂדִּים` (biblical name of the same people; LXX Χαλδαῖοι for `כַּשְׂדִּים`, 11:28, 31). Βαβυλών = `בָּבֶל`. Ἐρυθρὰ θάλασσα = `הַיָּם הָאָדֹם` (literal; here the Persian Gulf; note the LXX's use of the same phrase for `יַם סוּף`).
7. **Units of time and length:** σάρος / νῆρος / σῶσσος = `סָרוֹס / נֵרוֹס / סוֹסוֹס` (Akk. *šār* 3600, *nēr* 600, *šūš* 60); στάδιον = `רִיס` (the Mishnaic Hebrew stadion, c. 185 m; note at first occurrence).
8. **The two speech-modes.** Polyhistor's excerpt is in indirect speech (accusative + infinitive) throughout, "he says that …". Hebrew can't sustain that; we render as narrative past and let the frame ("כָּךְ … נִכְתָּב") carry the reported status, as all translators do. Where Greek switches to a finite verb (Εἶπέ τε αὐτοῖς, B7.6.1), the note says so.
9. **Abydenus's historic presents** (προσημαίνει, κελεύει, κομίζονται, ἀφανίζουσι) are kept as Hebrew present; his imperfects as past.
10. **κυβερνήτης = `הַקַּבַּרְנִיט`**: the Mishnaic Hebrew loan of this very Greek word; noted as the boatman (Gilg. XI 95 Puzur-Enlil).

## Cruxes (decided; details in the apparatus)
- B7.1.1 Ἀρδάτου: corrupt for Ὠτιάρτου (fr. 6) = Ubār-Tutu. Kept as written, `אַרְדָּטֵס`, with a note.
- B7.2.2 whether the date (15 Daisios) is the date of the dream or of the destruction: syntactically ambiguous; Abydenus and the Armenian make it the flood's date. We follow them.
- B7.2.3 διὰ γραμμάτων πάντων ἀρχὰς καὶ μέσα καὶ τελευτάς: "the beginnings, middles and ends of all (things), in writing" (Greek) vs. "all the books, first, middle and last" (Armenian). We follow the Greek.
- B7.2.9 εὐξάμενον (MSS, "having prayed") vs. εὐξόμενον (Scaliger, "to pray"): we read the future with Scaliger and the Armenian (*orandum*).
- B7.3.2 5 stadia (Syncellus) vs. 15 (Müller's note "quindecim, Eus.") — but Müller's own Latin of the Armenian prints *quinque* ("five"). Recorded; text: five.
- B7.4.8 διελθόντα "passing through" makes poor sense with "seams"; read διελόντα "having pulled apart" (the usual correction; cf. Armenian *tecto partim effracto*). Rendered `פָּרַם` "unstitched", marked `?`.
- B7.6.4 πέριξ "round about" vs. πεζῇ "on foot" (Müller's correction from the Armenian *pedestri itinere*). We follow Müller.
- B1.3.1 ζῷον ἄφρενον: unintelligible (ἄφρων "senseless"? Voss: ἄρρεν ὄν "being male"; Armenian *immanem beluam* "a monstrous beast"). Rendered `יְצוּר (?)`.
- B6.5 τὸν μυσαρὸν Ὠάννην "the loathsome Oannes"? Müller conjectures δεύτερον "the second"; rendered with `?` and both options.

## Lessons for the guidelines
1. **Check digital Greek against the page images, always.** DFHG silently dropped a full printed line (homoeoteleuton καὶ π… → καὶ π…) in the most important sentence for the comparison (the animals). A clean-looking Unicode text is not a verified text. Recommend: whoever maintains `sources/Berossus/` corrects `Berossus-fr7--Xisouthros-flood--Greek.md` and notes it in its README; scan page = PDF p. 566 (FHG II p. 501), left column.
2. **For Greek texts, add an LXX layer** to the parallels index (Berossus ↔ LXX ↔ Hebrew). It separates three different things: a Greek word the LXX also uses (lexical identity), a link that exists only via the Hebrew (e.g. "walk with the gods" ↔ `הִתְהַלֵּךְ אֶת` where the LXX says εὐηρέστησεν), and a deliberate LXX avoidance (βωμός vs θυσιαστήριον). Rahlfs is now in `sources/LXX-Rahlfs-1935--el-wikisource/` (add to guidelines §4; licence-unclear → `.gitignore`).
3. **Echo control in Greek.** Where the LXX equates a Greek word with the Genesis Hebrew word, that equation is evidence for choosing the Hebrew cognate-like rendering (ὄρνεα → `עוֹפוֹת`, ἄσφαλτος → `כֹּפֶר`, κοπάζω → `שָׁכַךְ`). Where it is only a shared root (διαφθείρω / καταφθείρω), prefer a neutral Hebrew word and record the link.
4. **Translations of translations.** The Armenian Eusebius, known only in Latin, sometimes stands closer to Genesis than the Greek (*sedes qua pes subsisteret*; *tectum*; *filios*; *efficienda*). A Christian translator knew Genesis. Such "parallels" are evidence for reception, not for Berossus: tag them, but say so.
5. **Partial niqqud and ktiv male.** Stripping niqqud from Hebrew drafted with full niqqud leaves defective spelling (`חקים`, `שלשת`, `ספר` for `סיפר`, `האדם` for `הָאָדֹם`!). Draft unvocalized in ktiv male from the start and vocalize names afterwards. A name-vocalizing pass must not hit homographs (`סיפר` the verb vs `סִיפַּר` the city).
6. **Bible quotations: check mechanically.** NFD-normalize both sides; the check caught pausal `הַמָּיִם` (ח:א) and a quotation spanning two verses.
