# Parallels index: the Mesopotamian flood stories and Genesis

This is the corpus's main product for the commentary (`corpus.md`; guidelines §7.3).
It merges the parallels indexes of all six projects into one table, ordered by Genesis verse: Genesis 1–11 first, then the rest of Genesis, then the rest of the Bible.
Each row points to the line in the project's `translation.rtl.md` (and its `annotated.rtl.md`, where the full apparatus is).
The Hebrew rendering is ours, as it stands in the translation. Bible quotations are vocalized and checked with `tools/check_bible_quotes.py`.

## Key findings for the commentary

The commentary asks of each word in Genesis: *why exactly this word?* These are the places where the Mesopotamian evidence answers, or fails to answer, that question. The rows cited are in the table below, under the Genesis verse given.

1. **`עֲצֵי גֹפֶר` (6:14): no Mesopotamian version names a wood, and none has a word like `גֹפֶר`.** OB Atr. III i 22, Gilg. XI 24 and Ark 4 say only "build a boat". Where a material is given it is rope (Ark 10–17), reeds (Nippur J r. 7′) or wood implied by an axe (Ugarit I₂ 3–4). The wood type is Genesis's own detail, and so is the word. Nothing in the sources supports or contradicts the commentary's link to `גָּפְרִית`, so the reading has to stand on Hebrew evidence alone. (Rows under 6:14.)
2. **`קִנִּים` (6:14): the concept is attested, the word is not.** No Akkadian flood text has *qinnu* "nest". The boat is divided inside: "nine" parts in Gilg. XI 63, and cabins (*ḫinnū*) "above and below" in Ark 17, which also get bitumen (Ark 20). *ḫinnu* only sounds like `קֵן` and is not related to it. So the noun "nests" for the compartments is Genesis's choice, and the commentary's reading of it (`קִנְיָן`) is not checked by any source. (6:14, Gilg. XI 63, Ark 15–17.)
3. **`כֹּפֶר` (6:14) is the strongest lexical link in the corpus, and the standard translations hide it.** *kupru* is in OB Atr. III i 33, ii 13, ii 51, Gilg. XI 55, 66, Ark 21, 31–32, and through the LXX's ἄσφαλτος in Berossus B7.7.2. But **`מִבַּיִת וּמִחוּץ` is attested only in the Ark Tablet** (Ark 18–19). There the substance is *iṭṭû* (`חֵמָר`), not *kupru*, and the word for "inside" is *qerbu*, cognate of `קֶרֶב`, not "house". Gilg. XI 66–67 has "into the kiln … inside". So the pair inside/outside is inherited, but `מִבַּיִת` is Genesis's word. In OB the bitumen also seals the door from inside (ii 51), which is the hero's act (finding 6). (6:14.)
4. **`צֹהַר` (6:16): Genesis plans at the building stage a source of light that every Mesopotamian version either forbids or opens only at the end.** OB Atr. III i 30 commands the opposite: "let Šamaš (the sun) not see inside". The boat is roofed "like the Apsû", above and below (i 29–31). The light comes in only after the storm: Ūta-napišti opens a vent and "daylight fell on my cheek" (Gilg. XI 137). Ziusudra drills a hole and the sun-god Utu himself enters the boat (Sum. D 6–8). In Ugarit I₂ 4–10, Ea orders a window "at the top" when the flood ends, and then a bird is sent. In all three, the opening is tied to the sun or to the sun-god. No version has a word cognate with `צֹהַר`. The commentary reads `צֹהַר` as noon light, "the supreme leader". The evidence fits the light sense, and it shows that the deviation lies in the *timing*: the light is part of the plan from the start. (6:15–16; 8:6–12.)
5. **`תַּחְתִּיִּם שְׁנִיִּם וּשְׁלִשִׁים` (6:16): the decks are counted in the late versions, but the number three is Genesis's alone.** Gilg. XI 61–62 has six decks and seven divisions (and nine parts, 63). BM 38917 rev. 7′–9′ has "to six, to seven, to eight". The Ark Tablet has two levels ("above and below", 17), and OB Atr. III is roofed "above and below" (i 31). No version has three, and none names the levels by ordinals as Genesis does. (6:15–16.)
6. **Who shuts the door: Genesis departs from every Mesopotamian version.** Wherever the scene survives, the hero closes or seals it: OB Atr. III ii 51–52, Ark 60, U r. 3′, Gilg. XI 94, with the boatman as "the sealer" (95). In W 6′ the god *orders* the hero to shut it. Only in Genesis does God do it himself: `וַיִּסְגֹּר יְהוָה בַּעֲדוֹ` (7:16).
7. **The hidden cognates, which the usual translations ("ceased", "rested", "stopped up") hide:**
   - *sekēru* N = `וַיִּסָּכְרוּ` (8:2), same stem: Atr. II B 10.
   - *kalû* = `וַיִּכָּלֵא` (8:2): Gilg. XI 133b.
   - *nâḫu* = `וַתָּנַח` (8:4): Gilg. XI 133a. The subject is the sea, not the boat.
   - *kupru* = `כֹּפֶר`, and *qerbu* = `קֶרֶב` for the inside of the boat (Gen 6:14): Ark 18–19.
   - *šanā* "two each" = `שְׁנַיִם שְׁנַיִם` (7:9): Ark 52.
   - *zēr napšāti* = `זֶרַע` (7:3): Gilg. XI 27, 84.
   - *warḫu* = `יָרֵחַ`, the lunar date (7:11): Atr. II H 28.
   - *ṭēmu* + *šanû* = `וַיְשַׁנּוֹ אֶת טַעְמוֹ` (1 Sam 21:14): Atr. III iii 25.

   (8:1–5; 6:14; 7:2–15; rest of the Bible.)
8. **Most of Genesis's "unique" details turn up in one minor recension each, and never in Gilgameš XI:**
   - "inside and out": Ark 18–19;
   - "two by two": Ark 52 (the reading is uncertain);
   - the animals *sent* to the hero (`יָבֹאוּ אֵלֶיךָ`, 6:20): W 10′ (partly restored);
   - the "bird of heaven" in the loading list: Nippur J r. 11′, OB ii 35 (restored);
   - a window made and then a bird sent: Ugarit I₂;
   - "no more flood": z v 13′;
   - a new name for the hero: z v 17′;
   - three bird sendings with a sign on the second: Berossus B7.4.2–6.

   Genesis looks less like a reworking of Gilgameš than like a selection from the whole range of versions.
9. **Where Genesis departs from all the Mesopotamian versions:**
   - one God decides, with no divine assembly (6:5–13);
   - the same God destroys and saves, with no dissenting god under oath (6:13; Atr. II H 9–18);
   - the cause is moral evil (`חָמָס`, `רָעַת הָאָדָם`), not noise, and the Atra-ḫasīs tradition says the opposite: the "evil deed" is the god's (Atr. II I 5);
   - the regret comes before the flood and is God's, not the mother goddess's after it (6:6);
   - the raven goes first (8:7);
   - there are burnt offerings on an altar, and no hungry gods swarm "like flies" (8:20–21);
   - the blessing is `פְּרוּ וּרְבוּ` (9:1), where Atra-ḫasīs limits birth (Atr. III vii 1–9);
   - there is a covenant with a sign;
   - Noah dies (9:29), while every Mesopotamian survivor whose end is preserved becomes immortal.
10. **The seven days are used in reverse.** In Genesis seven days are the waiting time *before* the flood (7:4, 10), and the rain lasts forty days. In OB Atr. III i 37, iv 24 and Sum. D 3, seven days and nights are the length of the flood itself; Gilg. XI 128 has six days and seven nights. Genesis keeps the number but moves it. (7:4–12.)
11. **The dates.** The Mesopotamian versions tie the flood to the moon's disappearance (Atr. II H 28; Atr. III ii 39; Ark 50; Ugarit I₂ 1). Genesis gives a calendar date in the second month (7:11), and Berossus gives 15 Daisios, which is also the second month (B7.2.2). (7:11.)
12. **The shape.** The Mesopotamian boat is round (Ark 7, W 2′, probably OB i 28) or a cube (Gilg. XI 29–30, 58–59), 1 *ikû* in area and up to 120 cubits high. Only Berossus has a long ship (5 × 2 stadia, B7.3.2), closest to Genesis's 300 × 50 × 30. (6:15.)
13. **The smell of the sacrifice is the closest narrative parallel, with different roots.** `וַיָּרַח יְהוָה אֶת רֵיחַ הַנִּיחֹחַ` (8:21) ↔ "the gods smelled the (sweet) smell" (Gilg. XI 161–162). In OB Atr. III v 34 the verb is restored. There is no cognate of `נִיחֹחַ`, and our translation avoids `ניחוח` on purpose. (8:20–22.)
14. **Before the flood, the structure of Genesis 5 has close parallels.** There are ten kings with Xisouthros tenth (Berossus B4–B6; one King List manuscript), and the count varies from eight to ten within Mesopotamia. The seventh king is Enmeduranki of the sun city (SKL 26), who corresponds to Enoch, seventh, 365 years. The flood hero's fate, "taken" to live with the gods (Berossus B7.4.13; Gilg. XI 205–206 `לקחוני`), is in Genesis the fate of Enoch (5:24), not of Noah. (Gen 5.)
15. **The first verses of the flood story follow the epic's opening.** Mankind multiplies and, after a fixed span, the god reacts: 600, then 1,200, then 3 × 3,600 years in Atra-ḫasīs (I 352–353; II A 1–2, E 63) against `מֵאָה וְעֶשְׂרִים שָׁנָה` right after `כִּי הֵחֵל הָאָדָם לָרֹב` (6:1–3). Atra-ḫasīs also has "seven and seven … males … females … two by two" (cf. `שִׁבְעָה שִׁבְעָה זָכָר וּנְקֵבָה`, 7:3; `שְׁנַיִם שְׁנַיִם`, 7:9) at the *creation* of mankind (Atr. I 256א–ז, NA only), not at the boat. (6:1–4; 7:2–3.)

## How to read the table

**Tags** (guidelines §6.4):
- [cognate]: same root, same or similar meaning.
- [equivalent]: same concept, different root.
- [divergence]: the motif is shared, the details differ.
- [absent]: in one text and not in the other.

**(restored)** after the line number means that the parallel rests, in whole or in part, on a word restored by the editor. Such a parallel is only as good as the restoration.

**LXX** notes (Berossus rows): "=" the Greek word is the Septuagint's word for the Genesis Hebrew; "≠" it is not.
