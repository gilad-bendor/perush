# Project: The Sumerian Flood Story ("Eridu Genesis"; Ziusudra) → Hebrew

Read `../../translation-guidelines.md` first, then `../corpus.md` and the shared `../renderings.md`. This file holds only what is specific to this text.

## Scope
- **Text:** the Sumerian Flood Story, ETCSL 1.7.4 = ORACC epsd2/literary Q000357 ("Eridu Genesis" is Jacobsen's 1981 name). The whole surviving text: 89 composite lines in five segments (A–E), about 89 of an original ~300 lines.
- **Plus** the Ur manuscript P346146 (UET 6/1 61+503+691+701; Peterson, *JCS* 70, 2018): 32 line-stubs, translated as a separate section (see Decisions §3).
- Content: (lost beginning) → a god's speech about restoring mankind and having cities built (A 1–10) → An, Enlil, Enki and Ninḫursaĝa create the "black-headed" people and the animals (A 11–14) → kingship descends from heaven; five cities are given to five gods (B) → (lost: the reason for the flood) → the gods' oath; Inana laments; Enki deliberates; Ziusudra, the pious king; the warning through the wall (C) → (lost: building and loading the boat) → seven days and seven nights of flood; Utu's light enters the boat; the sacrifice (D) → the oath "by the life of heaven and earth"; the animals come out; Ziusudra prostrates himself before An and Enlil, receives "life like a god", and is settled in Dilmun "where the sun rises" (E) → (lost end).

## Base text and manuscripts
- **Base text (rank 1):** ETCSL c.1.7.4 (Black, Cunningham, Zólyomi et al., revised 2006; composite after Civil 1969 and Jacobsen 1981). `../../sources/ETCSL/1.7.4--Flood-Story/` (`transliteration.md`, `translation.md`, `c.1.7.4.xml`). The XML is authoritative.
- **Lexical/morphological data (rank 2):**
  - ORACC Q000357 CDL JSON: per-word ePSD2 lemma, sense, base and **morphological analysis** (e.g. `mu.n:~;eš.a,bi.a`), extracted with a scratch script (`python3 -I`) from `../../sources/ORACC/epsd2-literary--Q000357--Flood-Story/Q000357.cdl.json`. The `morph` field is the best available guide to ḫamṭu/marû and to the prefix chain; it was used for every line.
  - ETCSL's own lemmatisation (`<w lemma label>` in `../../sources/ETCSL/_bulk/etcsl-unzipped/etcsl/transliterations/c.1.7.4.xml`), and ETCSL corpus-wide searches for parallels of rare words (`kab₂ dug₄`, `na de₅`, `ĝizzal ak`, `zi an-na`).
  - ePSD2 glossary (`../../sources/ORACC/_bulk/epsd2-literary.zip` → `gloss-sux.json`), read in place with `python3 -I`.
- **Manuscripts:**
  - **N** = CBS 10673 (+10867), Nippur, OB. Six columns of ~50 lines; only the lower third survives. The only manuscript of the ETCSL composite. Hand copy Poebel 1914 (PBS 5 no. 1); first edition Poebel 1914 (PBS 4/1, pp. 9–70).
  - **Ur** = P346146 (UET 6/1 61+…), OB, Ur. ORACC transliteration only (after Peterson 2018); not lemmatised; no correlation to the composite given.
  - Not available: MS 3026 (Schøyen; Volk, unpublished); CT 46, 5 (bilingual; Civil 1969: 138 suggests it may belong to the gap in col. iii); STVC 87 B (content similarity only).
- **Comparison translations (rank 3–5), consulted only after drafting each line:** ETCSL English (`translation.md`); Civil's numbering and short Spanish renderings of C 18–23, D 1–2, D 9–11 quoted by Jiménez Zamudio 2002 (`../../sources/Comparative-studies/Jimenez-Zamudio-2002--…/`); Poebel 1914 (historical: "Ziugiddu", Šamaš); paraphrase in Hammerly-Dupuy 1968. Civil 1969, Jacobsen 1981/1987, Kramer 1983, Römer 1993 (TUAT), Chen 2013 and Peterson 2018 are **not** in hand (`../../sources/_records-only--Sumerian-flood-secondary/`).

How it was established:
- Civil = ETCSL + offset is **verified** for C (Jiménez Zamudio quotes C 18–23 as Civil 152–157) and D (D 1–2 = 201–202, D 9–11 = 209–211). A 1 = 37 follows from the 36 lost lines; E 1 = 251 from D 17 = 217 + 33.
- B: ETCSL's manuscript list says "84–100", but the gap counts (A 14 = 50, + 32 lost → B 1 = 83; B 18 = 100, + 34 → C 1 = 135, verified) require **B 1 = 83**. Either B 1 (a mere trace) is unnumbered in Civil, or the list is off by one. Treat B as 83/84–100 (±1).
- The six columns of 50 lines (300 in all) fit every figure: obverse columns preserve their **ends** (i 37–50, ii 83–100, iii 135–150), reverse columns their **beginnings** (iv 151–162, v 201–217, vi 251–262). Poebel 1914 confirms that C 16 is the last line of col. 3 and C 17 the first of col. 4.
- Jacobsen 1981 (and TUAT, which follows him) has a different numbering; not available here.

## Decisions
1. **Aspect.** ORACC's `morph` analysis decides ḫamṭu vs marû. Narrative ḫamṭu → Hebrew past. Narrative marû (D 1, D 2, D 11, E 3, E 7, E 8) → Hebrew participle (present), which keeps the imperfective/plural/solemn-present colour without inventing a nuance; each case is noted. Cohortative *ga-* → Hebrew cohortative (`אָשִׁיבָה`); precative *ḫe₂-* → jussive.
2. **`kab dug₄-ga`** (B 10, B 16, C 22, D 2): a recurring term of unknown exact meaning. ETCSL lemmatises *kab₂* "gauge", and *kab₂ dug₄* elsewhere in the corpus means "to measure, to check" (ETCSL 4.06.1: water measured with a 1-sila jug; 2.4.2.01: Šulgi "measuring" his strength). Rendered **`הַחֲלָקוֹת הַמְּדוּדוֹת?`** ("the measured plots"), identical in all four lines, with `?`. ETCSL leaves it untranslated (`......`); others render "cult centres" (Kramer) or "capitals/settlements" (Civil, via Jiménez Zamudio). Open.
3. **The Ur manuscript is a separate section, not an apparatus layer.** Reasons: (a) its 32 lines are mostly one- or two-word stubs and overlap the Nippur composite in wording only in the city ordinals (Ur r 2′–4′, 7′ ↔ B 11–15); (b) its sequence differs (Enlil, then "shepherdship", i.e. the appointment of the first king Alulim, per Peterson), so it is a different recension, and an apparatus layer would imply a line-alignment that we cannot verify without Peterson 2018; (c) its obverse ("no snake, no lion, no dog, [no wolf]") belongs to a part of the story that N has lost entirely, so there is nothing for an apparatus to hang on. Cross-references are given in the apparatus at B 11–16.
4. **Names.** Shared forms (`../renderings.md`). Sumerian city names follow the shared forms with a first-occurrence note: Eridug → `אֶרִידוּ`, Larag → `לָרַךְ`, Zimbir → `סִיפַּר`, Šuruppag → `שׁוּרוּפַּךְ`. Sumerian-only names: see `renderings.md` (project) and the proposals below.
5. **`iz-zi-da`** ("side-wall") → `קִיר הַצַּד`; **`e₂-ĝar₈`** (C 17; the Sumerian source of Akk. *igāru*) → `קִיר`. The wall speech C 19–21 is marked as parallel to Atra-ḫasīs III i 20–21 and Gilg. XI 21–22, and C 20–21 as **sharing a formula with the Instructions of Šuruppag 9–11** (father to Ziusudra).
6. **`niĝ₂-gilim`** (A 13, E 3, E 10): "small creature(s)" (ePSD2 "rodent"; ETCSL "small animal"; ETCSL translation simply "animals"). Rendered `הַיְּצוּרִים הַקְּטַנִּים`, not `שֶׁרֶץ`/`רֶמֶשׂ` (echo control: these are Genesis's words; the parallel is tagged instead).
7. **`edin`** (A 14) "steppe" → `עֲרָבָה`, **not** `עֵדֶן`; the much-discussed etymological link (Sum. *eden* → Akk. *edinu* → Heb. `עֵדֶן`?) is put in the apparatus only.
8. **`dim₂`** "to fashion" → `עִצֵּב`, not `יָצַר`/`עָשָׂה` (both Genesis creation verbs). The noun `niĝ₂-dim₂-dim₂-ma` → `יְצוּרַי` (the ordinary Hebrew noun; root echo noted).
9. **`ud-ba` / `ud-bi-a`** → `בָּעֵת הַהִיא`, not `בַּיָּמִים הָהֵם` (which would pre-empt Gen 6:4; parallel noted).
10. **`zi`** "life, breath": `חַיִּים` (E 1–2 oath "by the life of heaven/earth"; E 8 `zi da-ri₂` = `חַיֵּי נֶצַח`, not `חַיֵּי עוֹלָם`, to avoid pre-empting Gen 3:22 `לְעֹלָם`).

## Open questions
- C 12 (`an saĝ NIĜIN mu-un-dim₂-dim₂`): what Ziusudra "fashions" (an idol? Poebel/Kramer). Left as `…`.
- C 15 (`ma-mu₂ nu-me-a`): "something that was not a dream" (ETCSL) vs "in dreams that had not been before" (Poebel). Matters for the comparison with Atra-ḫasīs III i 11–14 / Gilg. XI 197 / Berossus (revelation in a dream).
- D 8 `ba-an-kur₉-re-en`: the `-en` ending (1st/2nd sg.) is anomalous for Utu as subject.
- E 1–2: the addressees of "you (pl.) shall swear by the life of heaven, by the life of earth" (the gods? the animals?) and the meaning of `za-e-da … la₂`.
- Get Civil 1969, Jacobsen 1981, Peterson 2018 and Chen 2013 (see `_records-only--Sumerian-flood-secondary/`) to check the cruxes above.

## Lessons for the guidelines
- **ORACC CDL JSON carries a `morph` field** (e.g. `ha.Vmma.i.n:~`, `V:~;e.eš`) for Sumerian lemmatised texts. It decides ḫamṭu/marû and the prefix chain, and is the Sumerian equivalent of "check the stem" (§5 step 5.2). The existing `tools/cdl2md.py` does not print it; a reusable extractor should (field names: `f.form, f.cf, f.gw, f.sense, f.base, f.morph`).
- **ETCSL's TEI XML is lemmatised too** (`<w form lemma pos label>`), and the bulk corpus allows a concordance of any rare word in seconds. That is how `kab₂ dug₄` "to measure" and the Šuruppag formula in C 20–21 were found. Recommend a small `etcsl_concord.py` in `tools/`. (This session's throw-away versions, not copied into `tools/` because the brief forbids writing outside the project folder: `cdl.py` (ORACC CDL → form | lemma | sense | morph) and `etcsl_lem.py` (ETCSL XML → per-line lemmas) in the session scratchpad `sumtr/`.)
- **Map line numbers by gap arithmetic and check against any quoted line.** ETCSL's "approx. N lines missing" plus one verified anchor per column gave Civil's numbering for the whole text and exposed an off-by-one in ETCSL's own manuscript list (B "84–100").
- **Tablet physics helps:** on a tablet whose lower part survives, obverse columns keep their ends and reverse columns their beginnings; a "segment" boundary may be a mere column turn with no gap (here C 16/17).
- **Sumerian marû in narrative** → Hebrew participle is a workable default that shows the aspect without over-interpreting (see Decisions §1). Generalize to other Sumerian projects.
- **Sumerian formulas recur across genres** (wall speech ↔ Instructions of Šuruppag; flood epoch formula ↔ King List). Before drafting a Sumerian line, search ETCSL for its formula.
