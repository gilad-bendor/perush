# Project: the Laws of Ur-Namma (prologue, laws, epilogue) → Hebrew

Corpus: `../corpus.md`, project 6. Read `../../translation-guidelines.md` (§5 Sumerian rules), `../corpus.md`, `../renderings.md` (**§5, §6.1, §6.3** and the merged notes), LH's `renderings.md` §1–§4, and the Parts of LE, MAL and **Lipit-Ištar** (`../5-חוקי-ליפית-אישתר/project.md`, Parts T5–T6: the Sumerian legal Hebrew) first.

## Scope
- **CDLI P432130 composite** (after Civil 2011 and Wilcke 2014): prologue 1–181, laws 181.a–474 and the additional sections b001–b038, c001–c032, d001–d043, e001–e029, f001–f008 (Civil's laws, chiefly from the Schøyen tablet MS 2064), epilogue g009–g028′: **628 labelled lines** (many `[...]`). Sumerian, Ur III (Ur-Namma or Šulgi, 21st c. BCE), OB copies (Nippur Ni 3191, Sippar Si 277, Ur, MS 2064 …).

## Base edition and sources
- **Rank 1:** CDLI P432130 + exemplars linked to it (`../../sources/CDLI/P432130--Laws-of-Ur-Namma-composite/`), with CDLI's variant notes.
- **Rank 2:** ePSD2 (`../../sources/ORACC/epsd2-royal--Q000947--Laws-of-Ur-Namma/`: lemmas + morph for the prologue and §1–§11 of the older RIME composite; the bulk zips), ETCSL for formulas, the LH/LE/MAL/LI Hebrew (`../laws_lookup.py`).
- **Rank 3:** CDLI's English. **Not obtained:** Civil 2011 (CUSAS 17), Roth 1997, Wilcke 2014, Finkelstein 1969, Frayne 1997.

## Line numbering and § concordance
- CDLI labels (`### 181.a`, `### 127a`, `### b002`, `### g028'`).
- **The composite has no § marks.** Laws are headed `## §N (first–last): heading` with **Roth 1997's number** where the law is one of Roth's §1–§37 (mapped by content: ePSD2 Q000947 labels §1–§11; the rest from the standard order, from memory, to be confirmed: §1 homicide, §2 robbery, §3 detention, §4–5 slave marriages, §6 the young man's betrothed deflowered, §7 she follows another man, §8 the slave-woman deflowered, §9–11 divorce / the widow, §13–14 accusations and the River ordeal, §15 the son-in-law, §17 the escaped slave, §18–22 bodily injuries, §25–26 the slave-woman and her mistress, §27–28 the witness, §29–31 fields; the order after §22 is uncertain), and **`## חוק (first–last): heading`** for laws without a Roth number (Civil's additions; cite by composite line). The concordance goes in the translator's Part section; uncertain mappings are open decisions. `laws_verify.py UN` checks that each heading's `(first–last)` equals its entries.

## Parallels to watch (preliminary)
- Prologue: the standard measures (*ba-ri₂-ga*, *ban₂*, *sila₃*, the shekel and mina weights) ↔ Lev 19:35–36; Deut 25:13–16; freedom (*ama-ar-gi₄* `דרור`) for Umma, Marad … ↔ Lev 25:10; **"the orphan was not delivered to the rich man, the widow to the powerful man, the man of one shekel to the man of one mina, the man of one sheep to the man of one ox"** ↔ Exod 22:20–23; Deut 10:18; 24:17; 27:19; Lev 19:15; "I removed evil, violence and the cry for justice" ↔ Gen 18:20–21; Exod 22:22.
- Laws: §1 "if a man commits a homicide, that man shall be killed" ↔ Exod 21:12; Gen 9:6; §2 robbery; §3 detention (↔ Exod 21:16); §4–5 slave marriages (↔ Exod 21:4); §6–8 the betrothed and the slave-woman deflowered (↔ Deut 22:23–29; Lev 19:20–22); §9–11 divorce payments; §13–14 the River ordeal (↔ Num 5); §15 the son-in-law; §17 the escaped slave returned (↔ Deut 23:16–17); **§18–22 and the new injury laws (bone, nose, skull, eye, tooth): money for limbs ↔ Exod 21:18–27, 23–25** (`עין תחת עין` – `תחת` reserved; the Torah's talion vs UN's silver); the slave striking a free man (head half-shaved, paraded) and the free man beating a slave (↔ Exod 21:20–21, 26–27); the man's daughter struck and miscarrying (↔ Exod 21:22–23: "if she died, that man shall be killed"); the slave-woman struck ("a slave for a slave", ↔ `תחת`); the slave-woman who curses / strikes her mistress (salt in her mouth; ↔ Gen 16:4–6; Prov 30:23); the false witness (↔ Exod 23:1; Deut 19:16–21); the fields (↔ Exod 22:4–5); the hired ox (↔ Exod 22:13–14); daughters inherit when there is no son (↔ Num 27:8); the deposit of barley in another's house broken into (↔ Exod 22:6–8); the wife left behind six months (↔ Deut 24:5?); the ox lost from the pen (↔ Exod 22:9–12; Gen 31:39).
- Epilogue: the curse on whoever erases the inscription (↔ Deut 27–28; LI and LH epilogues).

### Decisions (translator)
1. **The Akkadian-equivalent rule (corpus §6.3) and LI's Sumerian Hebrew** applied to every formula (`renderings.md` §1): *tukum-bi* `אם`; *lu₂* `איש`; *-bi* `ההוא`; *i₃-la₂-e* `ישקול`; *i₃-gaz-e* `יהרג`; *ib₂-su-su* `יחליף`; *ra* `מחץ`; *ama-ar-gi₄ … ĝar* `דרורו יושם`; *ur₂-ra-na … nu₂* `בחיקה שכב`; *nam-erim₂ ku₅* `נשבע שבועה`; *id₂* `הנהר`; *zalag* (*ubbubu*) `זיכך` (LH §2); *dam nitadam* `אשתו הבחירה`; *tak₄* `עזב`; *mussa-tur* `חתן`; *niĝ₂-mussa* `שי`; *saĝ-rig₇* `שלוחים`; *šeš-gal* `האח הגדול`; *ibila* `יורש`; *da-gi₄-a* (*bābtu*) `רובע`; *nin* of a slave-woman (*bēltu*) `גבירה` (LH §146); *a-zu* `רופא`; *maš* `תוספת`; *ur₅-ra* `חוב`; the measures and LH's craftsmen (§274), sābītu (§111), *ša libbiša* `את אשר בבטנה`, *qinnazu* `שוט`.
2. **Words that would echo a key verse got their own Hebrew** (each grepped against LH/LE/MAL/LI `>` lines): *niĝ₂-a₂-ĝar* `בתרמית` (not `במרמה` Gen 34:13, `בערמה` Exod 21:14; `פתה` kept for Exod 22:15 – LE *ina nulāni* is also `בתרמית?`); *ĝešbu₂* `*בקטטה*` (LH *risbatum*; `אגרוף` kept for Exod 21:18); *zir* `ריסק` / *ḫaš* `שבר` (Lev 24:20 `שֶׁבֶר`); *šub* (eye) `השיל` (LH; `הפיל` Exod 21:27); *saĝ saĝ-še₃ … gub* `עבד לעבד יעמיד` (`תחת` reserved; LI's `עבד כעבד` is *-gin₇*); *aš₂ du₁₁* `מארה אמרה` (`קלל` Exod 21:17); *sa₆* (heal) `החלים` (`רפא` reserved, Exod 21:19); *ur-maḫ … šu sur₅* `אריה *שסעו*` (`טרף` reserved); *ennuŋ* `בכלא` (`משמר` contains שמר); *šuku* `חוֹק (המחיה)` (Gen 47:22; `קצבה` = LH *aldû*); *egir* `ירושה` and *a-ga* `*אשר מאחוריה*` (`נחלה` kept for Num 27:8); *ga … gu₇* `חלב האכיל` (`הניק` = Exod 2:7–9's verb, noted).
3. **The *a/e₂ nu-gi₄-a* idiom** (§6, §8): rendered by the composite's spelling – *a* lines `אשר *לא הובאה*` / `*והביאה*` (asterisked; Roth "deflowered", CDLI "brought into a household", ePSD2 *a* "water"), *e₂* lines (ex03, adopted by the composite in §8) `אשר הביתה לא הובאה` / `והביתה הביאה`. No `טרם` (not a "not yet" construction), no `בתולה` (no *batultu*), no `מאורשה` (reserved): the comparison with Deut 22:23 `מְאֹרָשָׂה` is made in the apparatus.
4. **216 *munus-bi lu₂ i₃-gaz-e*** = `האשה ההיא / *(בידי) איש* תֵּהָרֵג` – keeps *lu₂* and LH's feminine `תֵּהָרֵג` (the verifier's `יהרג` formula); *lu₂* is probably the impersonal agent ("one shall kill her"), CDLI "by the man (her fiancé)".
5. ***ḫamṭu* in an apodosis** (284 *i₃-na-šum₂*, c019/e027/e028 *i₃-gaz*, c020 *ba-ab-tum₃*): rendered by the legal sense (future: `יתן`, `יהרג`, `יטול`) and noted – **a deviation from LI T5's Decision 4** (304′ kept past), because here the ḫamṭu forms are regular apodoses of whole law groups (MS 2064 writes *i₃-gaz* where the older tablets write *i₃-gaz-e*), not a resultative.

### Open decisions for the user
1. **`בתרמית`** for *niĝ₂-a₂-ĝar* (§6, §8, §29) – alternatives `במרמה` (Gen 34:13, Dinah), `בערמה` (Exod 21:14), Roth's legal sense "in violation of the rights of another" (`שלא כדין`).
2. **§6/§8 *a nu-gi₄-a* / *a bi₂-in-gi₄***: `*לא הובאה*` / `*והביאה*` (CDLI's "brought into the house") vs Roth's "virgin … deflowered" (`*בתולה* … *ובעלה*`); the ePSD2 lemma *a* "water" supports an idiom for intercourse.
3. **`*בקטטה*`** for *ĝešbu₂* (LH *risbatum*) vs `באגרוף` (Exod 21:18; CDLI "fistfight").
4. **216 `*(בידי) איש* תֵּהָרֵג`** vs `יהרגוה` (needs a verifier exception) or CDLI's "by the man (her fiancé)".
5. **193 `לא תצא`** (the slave-woman, CDLI/Roth) vs `לא יצא` (the freed slave stays); LI 345′ renders the same string causatively.
6. **Decision 5** (ḫamṭu apodoses as future) vs LI's past.
7. **342 `יִמָּחֵץ`**: who is flogged (the free man, CDLI) is not marked in the Sumerian.
8. **`העודף`** for *niĝ₂-diri* (literal) vs `ההיטל` (CDLI "levy"); e018 *lugal niĝ₂-diri* `*מלך העודף*`.
9. **`חוֹק (המחיה)`** for *šuku* (Gen 47:22) vs `מנת המזון`.
10. **The concordance** (table above): §12?, §16, §21, §22?, §23?, §24?, §33?, §34–§37? need Roth 1997 / Civil 2011 to confirm.
11. **`עבד לעבד יעמיד`** (402; *-še₃*) vs LI's `עבד כעבד` (*-gin₇*) – kept apart because the Sumerian case differs.

### Lessons
- **A prologue whose king is "I" but whose verbs carry the 3rd-person *‑n‑*:** decide the person once for the whole text (here 1st, from the narrative frame) and apply it to every identical verb line; otherwise identical Sumerian lines (163 = 165 = 168 = 171) end up with different Hebrew.
- **The older ePSD2 lemmatization (Q000947) is a second edition, not only a lexicon**: it preserves the old readings (*ug₅* "killed" Namḫani, Kiur, *maš-gan₂-bi*) that every older translation (and the reader's memory) follows. Record each difference in **כתבי יד**, follow the rank-1 composite.
- **The prologue's Torah contacts are clusters, not single verses**: orphan–widow–stranger (122, 162–171) ↔ Exod 22:20–23 with the cry (179) ↔ 22:22; measures (135–149) ↔ Lev 19:35–36 / Deut 25:13–16 and, as a whole sequence (justice → measures), Ezek 45:9–12. Index each verse, but say in the comment which ones belong together.
- **Look for two words of the same field before fixing one**: copper and bronze side by side forced a choice that a curse list (one metal) did not.
