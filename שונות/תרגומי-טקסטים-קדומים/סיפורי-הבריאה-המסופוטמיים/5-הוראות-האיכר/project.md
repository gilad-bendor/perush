# Project 5: The Farmer's Instructions (ETCSL 5.6.3) → Hebrew

Round 3, run 1 (2026-10-21), creation corpus (`../corpus.md`). Siglum in the corpus parallels index: **FI**.

## Scope
An OB Sumerian school text (copies c. 1800 BCE from Nippur, Ur and elsewhere; the composition perhaps Ur III): an old farmer instructs his son through the agricultural year, from the irrigation of the field to the winnowing, and the last lines name it as the instructions of Ninurta, Enlil's faithful farmer. The whole composite: 111 lines (1–111).

## Decisions
1. **Imperatives and the ethical dative.** Almost every line is an order. *-ab* / *-i₃* imperatives → Hebrew imperative; *ḫa-ra-* / *ḫe₂-* / *ḫu-mu-ra-* jussives → third-person future + `לך` (the Sumerian *-ra-* "for you", kept every time: the text's voice is a father speaking to a son, and the work is done "for you"); *na-* with *marû* → `אַל` + future (36, 37, 39, 74, 79, 80, 86); *nu-* → `לֹא`. One exception: 34 *na-du-un* is a warning ("(otherwise) it will not go"), rendered `לא` (open decision 11).
2. **Recurring frames** kept identical (`renderings.md`, "Formulas"): *-ed-a-zu-ne* `כשאתה עומד ל־` (2, 41, 48, 74, 100); *ud še … -a* `ביום שהשעורה …` (67, 69, 71, 72, 102, 107); *ud še … -zu(-ne)* `ביום שאתה …` (89, 98, 104); *sizkur₂ … dug₄-ga-ab* `את תפילת … – אמור!` (65, 103; 108 `את התפילה …`; 88 jussive `יאמרו לך`); *a dug₄-ga-ab* `השקה!` (68–70, 72; 71 `אל תשקה!`); *tukum-bi* `אם` (52, 54). 41 = 48 (identical Sumerian, identical Hebrew). *ud* "day" kept as `ביום` (not "when"), so that the reader sees the word.
3. **Reduplication** of a verbal base is doubled in jussives and imperatives (`יכסה ויכסה`, `יסובב ויסובב`, `קצור וקצור`, `ישר וישר`), as in S&W; not in prohibitions (80) or in participles (79, 82).
4. **The shared formula (111)** is copied byte for byte from S&W 317 (`אִכָּרוֹ הַנֶּאֱמָן שֶׁל אֶנְלִיל`; checked by script), and *za₃-mi₂-zu dug₃-ga-am₃* follows §9.5 (`תְּהִלָּתְךָ – טוֹבָה הִיא!`).
5. **The gated words** (§9.2–9.6) occur only with their licence: `חרש` with *uru₄* / *apin*; `זרע` with *numun* (49, 52, 68); `קצר` with *gur₁₀* (74, 75, 78); `לקט` with *de₅* (79, 82); `נח` / `הניח` with *ne-ḫa* (87 `הָנַח`, 96 `תנוח`); `עפר` with *saḫar* (61–62); `עבד` with *kiĝ₂*; `טוב` with *dug₃* (18, 111). Avoided: `תניח` (root נוח) in 74 → `תתן`; `בראשך` / `בראשה` (`ברא`) → `לראשך` / `לראשה` (20, 34); `לשבת` → `להושיב` (100); `קר` for *te-en* → `מצטנן` (108); `שִׁדָּפוֹן` / `יֵרָקוֹן` for *samana* → transliterated `סָמָנָה` (71).
6. **Two Sumerian verbs for "instruct" and "glean" are one verb, *de₅*.** *na … de₅* (1, 110) = `הוֹרָה` / `הוֹרָאָה` (**2026-10-23 harmonization: `נתן עצה` / `העצה … שנתן`, as in every other corpus**; §9.3, ↔ Isa 28:26 `יוֹרֶנּוּ`, Prov 4:4 `וַיֹּרֵנִי`); *de₅* alone (79, 82) = `לָקַט` [cognate via *laqātu*]. The apparatus says so at each.
7. **Measures** (the brief): *giĝ₄* `שֶׁקֶל` (51, 90), *ma-na* `מָנֶה` (9), *sila₃* `קַב` (73); *ban₂* `בָּאן`, *gur* `גּוּר`, *iku* `אִיקוּ`, *nindan* `נִינְדָּן` transliterated, values in the notes; *šu-si* `אצבע`. *{urud}giĝ₄* "axe" (9, 19) is another word in the same sign: `קרדום`.
8. **Splits by sense, recorded:** *la₂* `מתח` (10, a net) / `תלה` (16–17, 99, 104) / `שקל` (90, Akk. *šaqālu*) – as in H&P; *sal* `דק` (tools, 9, 44) / `צר` (a "mouth", 11, 67); *a₂* `זרוע` / `כוח` / `עת` (§9.3); *dirig* `עודף` (23, 25) / `עלה על` (67); *šub* `השליך` (43–44) / `נפל` (50, intransitive); *ĝiš* `עץ` (27, 32) / `מקל` (86, S&W *ĝiš ra*); *ba₉-ra₂* `פתח` (the threshing floor, 97) / `דש` (grain, 98, 101; §9.3) / *ĝiš-ba₉-ra₂* `מורג`; *ki* `הארץ` / `מקום`.
9. **New terms** (no precedent in any corpus): *{urud}giĝ₄* `קַרְדֹּם` (1 Sam 13:20–21); *bulug* `מַרְצֵעַ`; *šum-me* `מַשּׂוֹר` (Isa 10:15); *mud* (handle) `קַת`; *niĝ₂-gul* `מַקֶּבֶת` (Judg 4:21); *u₂-tag* `פטיש?`; *usu* `אוֹן` (Isa 40:29: `כֹּחַ` stays for *a₂*); *dur* `עֲבוֹת` (Job 39:10; `קשר` stays for *keše₂*); *ub* `זווית` (`פינה` is H&P's *a₂-ur₂*); *dam* (plough part, "spouse") `מוֹט` (Jer 27:2); *niĝ₂-kud* `טְרִיז`; *eme-apin* `לשון־המחרשה` (the `אֵת` only in the apparatus); *le-um* `לוח` [cognate via *lēʾu*]; *ki-saḫar* `מקום־העפר`; *lag* `רֶגֶב` (as H&P *lagab*); *bir₅* `אַרְבֶּה` [cognate via *erbû*]; *lillan* `מְלִילָה` (Deut 23:26; `אָבִיב` is S&W's *šu-nim*); *samana* `סָמָנָה`; *ku-dun* `רֶוַח` (= Akk. *nēmelu*, wisdom corpus); *kid₇* `גָּדַע`; *mar-gid₂-da* `עֲגָלָה` (Isa 28:27–28); *mur* `מִסְפּוֹא`; *nir* `זָרָה` (Ruth 3:2); *dabin* `סֹלֶת` (proverbs); *ka-gu₇* `מאכל פה`; *u₂-saga₁₁* `אגודות העשב`; *ki-ĝar* `*מגרש*`; the three plough types and *gag-dag₂*, *ka-šu₂*, *ab-ki-iz*, *ba-an-du₈*, *suḫub₂* transliterated.
10. **Echo control:** no Hebrew word was chosen for a parallel. `שָׂם` (49) is the reserved rendering of *ĝar* (and Isa 28:25 has `וְשָׂם`); `השווה` (9) is H&P's *sa₂* (and Isa 28:25 has `שִׁוָּה`); `עֲגָלָה`, `מוֹרַג`, `גֹּרֶן`, `זָרָה`, `אֲלֻמָּה` are the plain Hebrew words. Rejected because they would import a verse the tablet does not have: `שִׁדָּפוֹן` / `יֵרָקוֹן` (71), `אֵת` (53), `אָבִיב` (70), `שָׁמַר` (6: `משמר עשה` follows the corpus's *en-nu-uĝ₃*), `תּוֹרָה` (110: `הוֹרָאָה`), `מוּסָר` (24: `עֲבוֹת`, so that Prov 1:8 is not echoed), `זְרוֹעַ נְטוּיָה` (21 is only `זרוע חזקה`, literal).

### Where this text departs from Summer and Winter or Hoe and Plough (with the reason)
- *gur* (92 `אורח שובך`) = `שוב` "return" (laments' *gur* = `שוב`); S&W 32, 91 has *gur* = `הקיף` "turn around". Split by sense.
- *{ĝiš}mud* = `קַת` (a handle: 17, 34); H&P 86 has `גזע` (stumps in the field). Split by sense (ETCSL lemma "stump (handle)").
- *us₂* = `צד` (46, 51: a measure, "side, length"), `סמוך` (61); *ki us₂* = `הודבק אל הארץ` (102) follows S&W.
- *ḫa-za* = `החזיק` (Inana's Descent), not `אחז`: `אחז` is S&W's *dab₅* (absent here), and *ḫa-za* is probably a loan of Akk. *aḫāzu* (the cognate of `אחז`) – open decision 13.
- *tu-lu* = `רָפָה` (Enki and Ninmaḫ 23); S&W has `רפה` for another word (*ḫu-nu*) – open decision 12.
- Everything else shared with S&W / H&P is followed: *a-šag₄* `שדה`, *gana₂* `חלקה`, *kislaḫ* `בּוּר`, *ab-sin₂* `תלם`, *a-tur₃* `תלם־המים`, *ki duru₅* `מקום לח`, *apin* `מחרשה`, *al* `מעדר`, *šudul* `עול`, *gud* `שור`, *zu₂* `שן`, *zar* `אלומה`, *garadin* `גדיש`, *de₅* `לקט`, *gur₁₀* `קצר`, *ĝiš ra* `במקל חבט`, *ur₃* `גרר`, *ze₂* `תלש`, *kud* `נחתך`, *si-il* `פילח`, *tag* `נגע`, *šub* `השליך`, *ĝar* `שָׂם`, *sig₁₀* `שָׁת`, *gu₂* `שפה`, *gid₂* `האריך`, *dub₂* `ניער`, *du₈* `פרש`, *gu₇* `האכיל`, *nu₂* `שכב` / `השכיב`, *sumur* `כעס`, *mi₂ dug₄* `טיפח`, *esir₂* `חֵמָר`, *sila₃* `קב`, *ma-na* `מנה`, *giĝ₄* `שקל`, *ki-buru₁₄*-type words (none here), *engar zid* (the formula).
- *{ĝiš}ninda₂* (the seeder funnel, H&P's `מַשְׁפֵּךְ`, H&P open decision 11) **does not occur** in the FI composite: the seeder-plough is *apin* throughout (41–53); the funnel is mentioned only in the notes.

## Open decisions
1. **Line 1:** ETCSL and ORACC read a name *Ud-ul-uru* (transliterated `אוּד־אוּל־אוּרוּ`, "the ploughman of distant days"); Civil 1994 reportedly reads "in days of old a farmer (instructed his son)". To be checked against Civil when available.
2. ***na de₅*** = `הוֹרָה` here (§9.3), but `נתן עצה` in the Instructions of Šuruppag (wisdom corpus) and in the flood corpus's citation of the same line – the same Sumerian phrase. Proposal: `הוֹרָה` in all corpora (it keeps Isa 28:26 `יוֹרֶנּוּ` and Prov 4:4 `וַיֹּרֵנִי` visible), or keep the split by genre. **Settled 2026-10-23 (`harmonization--2026-10-23.md` row 13): `נתן עצה` here too.**
3. **Settled by the coordinator (2026-10-22): `כֹּר` [cognate] (line 29).** ***gur*** (capacity): transliterated `גּוּר` by the brief; the Ur-Namma laws have `כור` – the real cognate (Sum. *gur* > Akk. *kurru* > `כֹּר`, 1 Kgs 5:2). Proposal: `כֹּר` corpus-wide, [cognate].
4. **Settled by the coordinator (2026-10-22): `תהילתך – מתוקה היא!` (as Enki and Ninmaḫ 141; §9.5 updated).** ***za₃-mi₂-zu dug₃-ga(-am₃)*** = `תְּהִלָּתְךָ – טוֹבָה הִיא!` (§9.5, here) vs `תהילתך מתוקה!` (Enki and Ninmaḫ 141; Inana's Descent 412). One rendering for the closing formula across corpora.
5. ***ki-ĝar dug₄*** (8, 91) `*למגרש עשה*`: meaning uncertain (ETCSL "establish / mark the limits").
6. **90** *lu₂ še la₂-la₂* = `שוקלי השעורה` (weigh, Akk. *šaqālu*) vs ETCSL "barley carriers"; and "thirty shekels" unexplained.
7. **82–83:** ETCSL reads "gather your helpers and gleaners … and lay down the sheaves"; the grammar also allows "for your weak ones and gleaners … make (them) lie at the sheaves" (*zar-re-eš₂*), which is the Ruth 2–3 parallel. Our Hebrew (`(ל)קטני־האון …`, `לאלומות – הַשְׁכֵּב?!`) keeps both readings open. ORACC also allows a temporal *-zu-ne* in 82 ("when your strength is small"). Civil's reading, reportedly a provision for the gleaners, should be checked.
8. ***samana*** (71) transliterated (`סָמָנָה`); alternatives `חֲלֻדָּה` (rust) or a biblical blight word (rejected: echo).
9. ***lillan*** (70) `מְלִילָה`: the sound resemblance *lillan* / `מְלִילָה` is accidental (noted); `שִׁבֹּלֶת` is the neutral alternative.
10. ***dur₂-dur₂*** (100) `להושיב` (literal "make sit") vs ETCSL "make trample"; ***ud 5-am₃*** (96) "five days" vs ETCSL "a few days"; ***ud šu₂-uš-e*** (88) `בבוא היום` (corpus) vs ETCSL "daily".
11. **34** *na-du-un*: `לא תלך` (warning) – or prohibitive `אל תלך` (the rule *na-* = `אַל`)?
12. **One root for two Sumerian words:** *te-en* `הצטנן` (proverbs; here 108) and *sed₄* D `צינן` (S&W); *tu-lu* `רפה` (here, E&Nm) and *ḫu-nu* `רפה` (S&W); *ḫa-za* `החזיק` and *kalag* `חזק` (both here, 20/34 and 21).
13. ***ḫa-za*** `החזיק` (Inana's Descent) – or `אָחַז` [cognate, if *ḫa-za* is the loan of *aḫāzu*], which S&W uses for *dab₅*?
14. ***bulug*** `מַרְצֵעַ` here (a copper tool) vs EWO `יָתֵד` (a peg); and *gag* `יָתֵד` here (= Inana's Descent) – so EWO's *bulug* and this *gag* share `יתד`.
15. **Uncertain lines** translated with `?` or *italics* and the alternatives in the apparatus: 8, 10–11, 13, 28, 33, 35, 42, 45, 47, 56, 59, 71–72, 79, 83, 91, 97, 101, 106.

## Key findings for the commentary
- **Isa 28:23–29 is the Farmer's Instructions in miniature.** The same sequence of work – ploughing in order to sow (`יַחֲרֹשׁ הַחֹרֵשׁ לִזְרֹעַ`, the seeder-plough, 41), harrowing (`וִישַׂדֵּד`, 32), levelling the surface (`שִׁוָּה פָנֶיהָ` = *sa₂*, 9), placing the seed in rows (`וְשָׂם חִטָּה שׂוֹרָה` = *ĝar*, 46, 49), threshing with sledge, cart-wheel and stick (`חָרוּץ`, `אוֹפַן עֲגָלָה`, `בַמַּטֶּה יֵחָבֶט` ↔ 99, 93, 86), not threshing endlessly (`לֹא לָנֶצַח אָדוֹשׁ` ↔ the rests of 87, 96) – and the same claim at the end: the farmer's knowledge is a god's instruction (`יוֹרֶנּוּ` ↔ *na de₅*, 110; `גַּם זֹאת מֵעִם יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת יָצָאָה` ↔ "the instruction of Ninurta"). The Sumerian text is a school text c. 1800 BCE; Isaiah uses the genre as a parable.
- **Gen 3:17–19, 3:23, 4:2, 9:20: farming as sentence vs farming as teaching.** In Genesis the work of the ground follows the expulsion (`לַעֲבֹד אֶת הָאֲדָמָה`) and is `בְּעִצָּבוֹן`; the first farmers are men (Cain, Noah). Here the farmer is a god (`אִכָּרוֹ הַנֶּאֱמָן שֶׁל אֶנְלִיל`, 111), the work is his instruction, weeds are routine (8), and well-planned work is done "as with a happy heart" (28). *saḫar* (the cognate of `עָפָר`) is only working soil (61–62). [divergence]
- **The gleaners (Lev 19:9; Deut 24:19; Ruth 2–3).** *de₅* = Akk. *laqātu* ~ `לָקַט` [cognate]: the "barley gleaners" (79, 82) stand beside the "small of strength" (82), and the father orders something done "according to their number" and "at / as sheaves" (83) – on one reading, provision for them to lie by the sheaves (Ruth 3:7) – after which "your work will be cared for truly" (84; cf. Deut 24:19's blessing). The gleaner beats out grain with a stick (86 ↔ Ruth 2:17 `וַתַּחְבֹּט`); the barley is winnowed at the threshing floor in the evening and at night, with prayer (103, 108 ↔ Ruth 3:2 `זֹרֶה … הַלָּיְלָה`), and measured (107 ↔ Ruth 3:15).
- **Deut 22:10, 25:4.** The Sumerian team is oxen only, with spare oxen and ploughs (23–25); the oxen of the cart are fed (94); for the threshing oxen (100) no feeding is commanded – [absent] the Torah's `לֹא תַחְסֹם`.
- **Gen 8:22.** Sowing and harvest (49–52, 74–78), the cooling of the day and the night (108), the cold and heat (13: the field "cooks") – the agricultural year as a fixed order, but with no flood and no promise behind it.
- **Gen 1:14, 1:20.** "When the stars of heaven are right" (38) the plough-team goes out: the stars as `מוֹעֲדִים` of the farmer (and in Hesiod's *Works and Days*, a shared motif). The locust is written with the determinative of birds, {mušen} (66) – the Sumerian scribe classes it with the flying creatures by its motion, as Lev 11:20–22 counts the locust among `שֶׁרֶץ הָעוֹף` (cf. the commentary's `עוֹף` as the motion-word).
- **Deut 28:22, 38; Amos 4:9; 1 Kgs 8:37; 1 Sam 6:4–5; Joel 1.** Pests and blight: the rodents (prayer to their god Ninkilim, 65), the "tooth" of the locust (66 ↔ Joel 1:6 `שִׁנָּיו שִׁנֵּי אַרְיֵה`), the *samana* disease (71) – in the Bible punishments, here an agricultural risk (a watering too many) met by ritual and know-how.
- **Lev 23:14; 25:22; 26:10.** "Since there is still flour of old barley, let no one beat out the grain for your bread" (85–86): eating the old until the new, with the new grain not touched early – economic here, cultic (the omer) in Leviticus.
- **[absent]** Mixed sowing (Lev 19:19; Deut 22:9), the sabbatical year and the firstfruits have no counterpart in the text; nor does any "curse" of the ground.
- **The ancient mind.** The field is alive and has moods ("its angry places", 33); the grain is a goddess who "falls" into the furrow (50); a pest has a god who is prayed to (65); four prayers mark the year (65, 88, 103, 108); the threshing floor and the sheaves are given "rest" (87, 96, with the Akkadian loan *ne-ḫa* ~ `נוח`). And a very practical father: quotas and spares, a tenth more yield for a well-timed watering (73), keep an eye on the man who drops the seed (49), don't wait to be told (37), plan for 144 rather than 180 *iku* and the work will be "as with a happy heart" (27–28).
