## Why this text (guidelines §1, both purposes)
- **Genesis:** a god orders the world by assigning every domain (waters, marsh, sea, rain, field, steppe, fold, the crafts) to an appointed deity ↔ Gen 1 (ordering by category; the days, months and years ↔ 1:14; the fish and birds of the marsh ↔ 1:20–22; rain ↔ 2:5); **Deut 32:8** (borders and the allotment of domains to divine beings); Gen 4:17–22 (culture-bringers: farming, herding, building, weaving, metalwork, music); the one who is left without a portion (Inana ↔ Levi, Deut 10:9, Num 18:20).
- **The ancient mind:** the *me*; Enki's character (procreative, clever, generous, self-praising); the Sumerian map (Sumer, Urim, Meluḫḫa, Dilmun, Magan, Elam, the Martu); the temple economy; Inana's restless, unclassifiable power.

**Finding:** in EWO *ĝar* is for structures and placements (shrines, houses, folds, dwellings, the woman's tongue); **living creatures are "given" (*šum₂*) or "multiplied" (*lu*), and gods are "made to stand" (*gub*)**. ## Key findings for the commentary (Enki and the World Order 250–472)
1. **Gen 1:20–22, who fills the waters?** Enki fills (*si*) the **Tigris** with his water-semen (254; Sumerian *a* = "water" and "semen") – the river conceives from the god and brings water, barley and beer (259–260). The marsh is *addressed* (*gu₃ de₂*, not "called"/named) and **given** carp; the reedbed is given reeds (274–275): a divine speech to a water-place followed by fish, as in `וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים יִשְׁרְצוּ הַמַּיִם` – but the fish are distributed, not brought forth, and **nothing is blessed, multiplied or "according to its kind"** ([absent] in all twelve appointments). The sea is "filled" by no one: its goddess Nanše is appointed over it (306–308) and is its tax-collector, sending "good" fish and "sweet" birds to Enlil (418–421). The only "filling" in the text goes to **Enlil's temple** (261, 299, 368: the refrain).
2. **Fish and birds together**, again: the net-god's three tools (279–281: throw-net – fish; snare – "what is born"; cord-net – bird) and Nanše's "fish good and good, birds sweet and sweet" (420). As in Bird and Fish, the two are neighbours of one marsh economy, not two ends of an axis; their place is the gods' table (↔ `כַּדָּגִים … וְכַצִּפֳּרִים`, קהלת ט:יב).
3. **The sea is a terrifying shrine, not a chaos-monster** (285–298): Enki "places a shrine in the sea"; its waves and *melam* are fearsome and the Anuna dare not approach – the awe of holiness (↔ Ezek 28:2 `מוֹשַׁב אֱלֹהִים יָשַׁבְתִּי בְּלֵב יַמִּים`; Ps 93:4). Against Gen 1:21's *tanninim* (and Tiāmat) this is [divergence]: the sea is an ordered domain from the start. Its "vastness" (*niĝ₂-daĝal*, 307) is Ps 104:25's `רְחַב יָדָיִם`.
4. **Gen 2:5 rain:** "he addressed the rain of heaven" (309; *šeĝ₁₄ an-na* = `מְטַר הַשָּׁמַיִם`, Deut 11:11) and appointed Iškur, the storm-god who "rides the great storm" (313 ↔ Deut 33:26, Ps 68:5) and is the "bolt" of heaven's interior (314 ↔ Job 38:10; Gen 7:11, 8:2) and its **canal inspector** (315): rain as a heavenly irrigation canal.
5. **Deut 32:8 – borders:** "zones he heaped, pegs he filled" (370; *in dub*, *bulug si*), then "for the Anuna – in the city foundation-platforms he placed, in the fields plots" (371–373). **The verbs differ** from `בְּהַנְחֵל … בְּהַפְרִידוֹ … יַצֵּב`: *dub*, *si*, *ĝar*. The verb of `יַצֵּב` (*gub* "set standing") is the **appointment formula** itself – its object is the god, not the border; and the allotment is of *places to gods*, not of peoples to gods (Deut 32:8–9 `כִּי חֵלֶק יְהוָה עַמּוֹ`). Nisaba repeats 370 as her daily office (416). A heap as border marker ↔ Gen 31:52 (our proposal).
6. **Gen 1:16–18 – the sun:** Utu is appointed "in the circuit of heaven and earth" (380, the only expanded formula) as hero, bull, herald and **judge** (374–379), born of the moon's wife Ningal (the sun is the moon's son: the reverse of `הַמָּאוֹר הַגָּדֹל` first). ↔ Ps 19:6–7 (the hero; `וּתְקוּפָתוֹ` ↔ *niĝin₂*).
7. **Gen 4 – culture as office, not invention:** the farmer Enkimdu "man of dyke and canal" (324 ↔ `אִישׁ הָאֲדָמָה`, 9:20; `עֹבֵד אֲדָמָה`, 4:2) and the shepherd Dumuzid (366 ↔ Abel, `רֹעֵה צֹאן`; Jabal, 4:20) are appointed one after the other; the builder Mušdama's vaults reach "the midst of heaven like Tiranna, the rainbow" (346 ↔ 11:4, 9:13); Kulla of the bricks (339 ↔ 11:3); Ninmug the **metalworker of the Land** (409 ↔ Tubal-cain, 4:22); Inana's **drums** of lament and joy (447–448 ↔ Jubal, 4:21; Miriam's drum). In Genesis the arts are human inventions in Cain's line; here every one is a goddess's or god's office.
8. **Levi and the daughters of Zelophehad – Inana without a portion** (387–423): after the twelfth appointment Inana "alone is left behind from the rites (*ĝarza₂* = *parṣu*)" – the reverse of Levi, who has no land *because* the cult is his portion (Deut 10:9; Num 18:20) [divergence]. Her "I am a woman – what have you done to my being alone?" (393/422) and Enki's "**what have I diminished for you? what shall I add to you?**" (*lal* / *taḫ*, 425–437) are the vocabulary of the allotment of an inheritance to women (Num 27:4 `לָמָּה יִגָּרַע`, 36:3 `וְנִגְרְעָה … וְנוֹסַף`; our proposal); the measuring rope is Nisaba's (414 ↔ Ps 16:6, Deut 32:9 `חֶבֶל נַחֲלָתוֹ`), and Inana does not even know how to hang a rope in a well (450).
9. **Deut 32:39 / Isa 45:7 – the opposites:** "what is not to be destroyed you destroyed, what is not to be placed you placed" (446): *gul* / *sig₁₀*, **not "create"** (ETCSL's false echo). The opposites are not light/dark or death/life by one god, but *the improper*: Inana's office is to break the order (↔ Jer 1:10 `וְלַהֲרוֹס לִבְנוֹת`, [equivalent] for *gul* = `הרס`).
10. **"Put words in the mouth" with *ĝar*** (433 "the woman's tongue in the mouth you placed") ↔ Num 23:5, Exod 4:15 – the reserved `שׂם` pays off; and heads heaped "like dust", placed "like seed" (445 ↔ 2 Kgs 10:8 `שִׂימוּ אֹתָם שְׁנֵי צִבֻּרִים`; Gen 3:19 `עָפָר`).
11. **"Good" (*dug₃*)** is said of the beer from the river (259), the shrine (298), the bread-goddess Ezina (333), the steppe (351) and the fish (420) – of things to eat and places, never as a god's verdict on his work ([divergence] ↔ `וַיַּרְא אֱלֹהִים כִּי טוֹב`).
12. **ETCSL's false echoes corrected:** 361 "created" (*du₃* "built"), 446 "create" (*sig₁₀* "put"), 274/309/326 "called" (*gu₃ de₂* "addressed"), 273 "placed in charge" (*gub* "set standing").

### The ancient mind (for the reader; see the intro)
Enki orders the world **sexually** (the Tigris as bride, 252–260; the brick-clay "penetrated like fine oil", 336; the steppe animals made to mate, 353) and **administratively** (gods as canal inspectors, farmers of Enlil, tax-collectors, scribes). The landscape and economy of southern Mesopotamia are the map of creation: rivers, marsh, lagoon and sea, a little rain, irrigated fields, steppe, folds, brick cities, weaving houses – and all abundance flows to Enlil's temple in Nippur. The school is visible in the lists (three barleys, the midwife's kit, the goldsmith's tools). The humour: the great goddess of love and war, "lady of the great *me*" (364), cries that she has nothing, enviously listing her sisters' little tools; Enki answers with a counter-question and a catalogue of her uncontainable powers (reversal of right and left, of men and women, of straight and tangled threads; war omens; heaps of heads), ending with a father's tease (449–450).

## Key findings for the commentary (Enki and the World Order 1–249)
1. **Gen 1:14 – Enki is the calendar.** "Counting the days, bringing the months into their houses, to complete the years; the completed years – to give a decision to the assembly; the decision-seeking of the day – to put in order" (17–19; again 44–45: "the month into its house he brought … its count reached"). Days, months and years in one line, as in `וְהָיוּ לְאֹתֹת וּלְמוֹעֲדִים וּלְיָמִים וְשָׁנִים` – but **no luminary, no `רָקִיעַ`, no "separating"**: the god counts time himself, like a temple administrator, and the year is "completed" and judged in the assembly [divergence]. The moon's "house" is where it disappears at the end of the month. Job 39:1–2 has the same sequence as EWO 12–17: the births of ibexes and does in the hills that only God sees (`הֲיָדַעְתָּ עֵת לֶדֶת יַעֲלֵי-סָלַע`), then `תִּסְפֹּר יְרָחִים` (our proposal).
2. **Gen 1:20–22 – fish and birds together, again, but as the god's scenery.** At Enki's house in the Abzu "the *suḫur* carp among the honey-plants extends its beard, the *eštub* carp among the small reeds extends its tail **to him**, the birds in their nests return chirps **with him**" (97–99; repeated 163–165, where the fish "make a debate" for him). Water-creatures and birds in one scene, as in 1:20, but neither made, nor blessed, nor multiplied [divergence; absent]. The bird is named by its kind (*buru₅*, *mušen*; *gamgam* "bird of heaven", 187 ↔ `עוֹף הַשָּׁמַיִם`), **never by its flight** (*dal* does not occur in 1–249). The *agargara* "fish-spawn" (the closest Sumerian word to `שֶׁרֶץ`) receive a place from the god in Dilmun (240), and the god eats the fish and dates (240–241; cf. Gen 1:29).
3. **Gen 1:21 – the *tanninim* are Enki's titles and his choir.** "Exalted great dragon (*ušum-gal*) standing in Eridu" (5) is an honorific of the god himself; the fifty *laḫama* of the Engur (Akk. *laḫmu*, in Enūma eliš a monster of Tiāmat's army) "speak affectionately" to him (186). The beings of the deep are a household, not chaos [divergence].
4. **Blessing vs fate.** Every act of "blessing" is *nam tar* "decree a fate" (43, 75, 95, 191, 211, 220). Inside the fates the *ḫe₂-* wishes come closest to Gen 1:22, 28: **"House Sumer – may your cow-pens be built, may your cows multiply (*lu*)! may your sheepfolds be set up, may your sheep be myriads (*šar₂*)!"** (205–206) – but the wish is spoken **to the land about its herds**, exactly like `וּבֵרַךְ … שְׁגַר אֲלָפֶיךָ וְעַשְׁתְּרֹת צֹאנֶךָ` (Deut 7:13) and Rebekah's `הֲיִי לְאַלְפֵי רְבָבָה` (Gen 24:60), not to the animals (`וַיְבָרֶךְ אֹתָם … פְּרוּ וּרְבוּ`). Fertility also follows the god's **going forth** (52–58: "at his going forth to the seeded people – true seed shall come out; … true lambs, calves, kids shall be born") ↔ `בָּרוּךְ … שְׁגַר אֲלָפֶיךָ … בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה בְּצֵאתֶךָ` (Deut 28:4–6) and `אֱלֹהִים בְּצֵאתְךָ … גֶּשֶׁם נְדָבוֹת` (Ps 68:8–10). For the land animals (1:24–25), which Genesis does not bless, EWO has fertility without a blessing word. "With Enlil, where the eye is on the lands, I decree good (*dug₃*) fates" (75): eye and "good" in one line, but the good is decreed in advance, not seen afterwards (↔ 1:31) [divergence].
5. **Creation by word?** "Your mouth – when it opens – a thing multiplies and multiplies, abundance it made adhere to the earth" (21); "by my speaking cow-pens are built, folds enclosed" (89); "by my word heaps are gathered" (93) ↔ `כִּי הוּא אָמַר וַיֶּהִי` (Ps 33:9), 1:3. But the word is not a command to something that does not yet exist: it is a fertile mouth among existing things [equivalent/divergence].
6. **Gen 10, Deut 32:8 – the map of the nations.** The journey (117–133, 188–249) is a Sumerian world map, from the centre outwards: Sumer ("from the rising of the day to its setting" it gives the *me* to the people, 193 ↔ Mal 1:11), Ur (`אוּר`, Gen 11:28), Meluḫḫa "the black land" (the Indus; in 1st-millennium texts Nubia = `כּוּשׁ`, Gen 10:6, 2:13; our proposal), Magan (Oman) and Dilmun (Bahrain; "purified", given its goddess Ninsikila, fish and dates – the "eastern" pure land, ↔ 2:8 and the flood story's Dilmun "where Utu rises"), Elam (`עֵילָם`, Gen 10:22, 14:1) and Marḫaši (their houses destroyed, their silver and lapis brought to Nibru), and the Martu nomads "who have no city, no house" (`הָאֱמֹרִי`, Gen 10:16), to whom Enki gives animals (↔ Jabal `אֲבִי יֹשֵׁב אֹהֶל וּמִקְנֶה`, Gen 4:20; Jer 35:7). Earlier, Enki "made the people dwell in their dwellings, made them cling to their herdsman, made them firm" (46–51) ↔ `בְּהַנְחֵל עֶלְיוֹן גּוֹיִם` (Deut 32:8), Ps 68:7. **Differences:** the map is of fates (each land's nature and products), not of descent; the "nations" are trading partners, an enemy and nomads; the order is centre–periphery, with all wealth flowing to Nibru (130 = 247).
7. **Gen 11:4 – the temple that touches heaven, as praise.** "May your *giguna* adhere to heaven! May your true house raise a hand to heaven!" (207–208); "Sanctuary of Ur – may (its) neck rise to heaven!" (218); "Enlil pronounced your exalted name" (216 ↔ `אֲשֶׁר פִּי יְהוָה יִקֳּבֶנּוּ`, Isa 62:2). The Babel motifs – tower to heaven, a name – are here the god's wishes for his land, and the name is given, not made [divergence].
8. **Elsewhere:** the *me* "placed in my hand" (65, *ĝar*) and the fates "given in my hand" (76) – Enki's authority is delegated; the seal-keeper of heaven and earth (72 ↔ Joseph, Gen 41:42); the mother-goddess "called me a good name" (78 ↔ Eve, Gen 4:25; Eccl 7:1); a shrine named with a good name (94 ↔ Gen 28:19); "the place my heart chose" (109 ↔ Deut 12:5); rain from heaven above and the carp-flood from below (90–91 ↔ Gen 49:25 `בִּרְכֹת שָׁמַיִם מֵעָל בִּרְכֹת תְּהוֹם רֹבֶצֶת תָּחַת`); ships bringing gold, silver and peacocks (128–130, 229–231 ↔ 1 Kgs 10:22, 9:28); "your silver – gold!" (231 ↔ Isa 60:17); the cosmic tree and its shade (4, 6, 166–167 ↔ Ezek 31:3–6); linen-clad priests (143 ↔ Ezek 44:17); Sumerian loanwords: *ma₂-laḫ₅* > `מַלָּח` (182), *gu-za* > `כִּסֵּא` (222), *na-gada* > `נוֹקֵד` (47) [cognate]; "the king to whom Enlil gave strength destroyed their houses, [demolished] their walls" (244–245 ↔ 1 Sam 2:10; Ezek 26:4 `וְשִׁחֲתוּ חֹמוֹת … וְהָרְסוּ`).

### The ancient mind in 1–249 (for the reader; see the intro)
The hymn shows a god who is at once nature (the bull, the *mes* tree, the dragon, the shade, the vine), administrator (calendar, seal, judge, fate-decreer) and patron of human life (the young man's vigour, the girl's grace, the shepherd's song, the churning cowherd). Its **self-praise** is a recognised genre of royal and divine speech – and it unwittingly reveals the hierarchy: Enki's *me* and fates were "placed in his hand" by Enlil, and all the wealth of the world goes to Nibru. The **cult** is concrete: exorcists of Eridu, linen-clad priests, treasurers (*enkum*, *ninkum*), purification of the river and the shrine with juniper, chants and incantations, the barge with its captain, sailor, stroke-callers and punt-pole – the god's statue really travelled by boat. The **world** is a merchant's and a soldier's map: the Gulf trade (cedar, timber, gold, silver, carnelian, peacocks, tin-bronze), admiration for the far "black land", generosity to the Martu nomads, and war and plunder for Elam – the enemy that would one day destroy Ur. The **humour** is gentle: the fish of Eridu "debate" for the god and wave their tails to him.
