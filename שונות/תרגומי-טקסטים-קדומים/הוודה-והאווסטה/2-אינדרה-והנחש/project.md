### The text on its own terms
- The poet "proclaims" one deed of Indra as if it were all of them (1a; Jamison); the killing formula repeats until the
  enemy is named (1–5).
- Soma before battle: the god's drunkenness is strength (3), the enemy's is folly (6a).
- The serpent is a cobra with spread hood (5a), handless and footless (7a), and in the end mouthless (6d).
- Six forms of "lie" (5–10): the one who lay on the waters lies dead under them.
- The waters are captive cows and the Dāsa's wives (2c, 11ab); freeing them is freeing the cows of the Vala myth.
- Vṛtra's mother Dānu dies over her son "like a cow with her calf" (9): pity, even for the enemy.
- Indra turns into a horse's tail hair to slip between the fangs (12ab).
- After the killing comes fear: Indra flees across ninety-nine rivers (14). The victor is not almighty.
- The hymn ends in kingship over all that moves and rests, a wheel's rim around its spokes (15).

### Key parallels
- [equivalent] 1c ↔ `אַתָּה בָקַעְתָּ מַעְיָן וָנָחַל` (תהילים עד:טו): the god kills the water monster and opens the
  waters (the dossier's parallel).
- [divergence] 4a ↔ `וַיִּבְרָא אֱלֹהִים אֶת הַתַּנִּינִם הַגְּדֹלִים` (בראשית א:כא): the firstborn of serpents, killed; in
  Genesis the sea creatures are created, not fought.
- [equivalent] 4c ↔ `אַתָּה הֲכִינוֹתָ מָאוֹר וָשָׁמֶשׁ` (תהילים עד:טז): sun and sky as the fruit of victory.
- [equivalent] 7a ↔ `עַל גְּחֹנְךָ תֵלֵךְ` (בראשית ג:יד): the legless serpent, here by nature, not as a curse.
- [divergence] 9d ↔ `אֹתוֹ וְאֶת בְּנוֹ לֹא תִשְׁחֲטוּ בְּיוֹם אֶחָד` (ויקרא כב:כח): mother and young killed together.
- [divergence] 13a ↔ `וַיַּרְעֵם בַּשָּׁמַיִם יְהוָה` (תהילים יח:יד): thunder, lightning and hail as the monster's weapons,
  which fail.
- [absent] 14 ↔ `יְהוָה יִמְלֹךְ לְעֹלָם וָעֶד` (שמות טו:יח): no fear or flight of the victor in the Bible.
- [equivalent] 15a ↔ `וַיֵּשֶׁב יְהוָה מֶלֶךְ לְעוֹלָם` (תהילים כט:י): victory over the waters ends in kingship.
