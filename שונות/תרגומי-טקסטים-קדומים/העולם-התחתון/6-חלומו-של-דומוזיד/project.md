### Key findings: the ancient mind
1. **A dream, its reading and its fulfilment in the same words.** The dream (26–40) is told image by image; Ĝeštinana quotes each image back (44–69: the same words, "for you" for "for me") and gives its meaning; and the end of the poem (250–260) carries out the dream in the dream's own words, down to its last line: the story's last line (260) *is* the dream's last line (40), `הגביע – שוכב; דּוּמוּזִי אינו חי; הדיר – של משב בנוי`. Dream-reading here is a literate, female craft: Dumuzid calls for "my scribe who knows clay, my singer who knows song, my young one who knows the heart of words, my old woman who knows the heart of the dream" (21–24). The interpretation also reorders the images: the goats and rams (37–38) are read last (66–69), because they are the sister herself in mourning – the reading ends in a promise of lament, which she keeps (242–244).
2. **The sister and the friend: one sign.** Offered "the river and its water, the field and its barley" (131–132 / 142–143, Inana's Descent's own bribe lines), the sister "did not stretch out her hand" and the friend "stretched out his hand": the two scenes differ by *nu-* alone. Even the *galla* state the rule – "a sister who reveals her brother's place – since distant days, who has seen (such)?" (138–139). The friend's betrayal is worded as a denial ("he did indeed drop his head in the grass; his place – I do not know!", 144–150), the reverse of the oath he swore in the sister's words (103–109). The poem's moral is spoken twice: "In the city – my sister kept me alive, my friend killed me" (153) and the proverb of the abandoned child (154–155, *ḫe₂-* against *na-*).
3. **Utu: kinship as a legal claim, tears as a gift.** Three times the bound man prays in the same words (164–173, 191–199, 226–234): Utu is "my wife's brother, I am your sister's husband"; he lists his services to Inana's house (fodder to Eanna, wedding gifts to Unug, kisses, play on her knees); Utu "took his tears" – a gift accepted, where the *galla* "accept no gift" (114) – and "like a man of mercy, did mercy" (175). His hands and feet are "set as gazelle hands and feet": a gazelle in this poem, a snake in Inana's Descent (373–374) and in some manuscripts here (197–202, 236–237) – two traditions of one escape.
4. **The *galla*.** They are "mixed men" from five cities, each pair mocked by a proverb (110–128); they know no grass or water, eat no offered flour and drink no libation, take no gift (the list of Inana's Descent 297–305B, copied); yet they offer bribes. The capture is a hunt – they surround, drain the pool, twist cords, knot nets (156–163) – and it is told three times word for word: the repetition *is* the trap. They read faces: the old woman's frightened eye and the sister's mourning betray what neither said (213–215, 246–248).
5. **Hospitality against death.** The old woman Belili gives the fugitive water poured out and flour heaped up (207–209) – exactly what the *galla* "do not drink" and "do not eat" (112–113). Her title "old woman" (*um-ma*) is the sister's own title at 24; in another tradition Belili *is* Dumuzid's sister.
6. **The sheepfold "built of wind".** The poem's last image is not Dumuzid's death but the empty fold: churn on its side, no milk poured, cup on its side, "the fold – built of wind" (*lil₂*: wind and phantom). The flight ends where it began: the last refuge is "the pure fold, my sister's fold" (234), and Arali, the steppe of the herds where he hides (94, 150–151), is also a name of the netherworld.

### Key findings: the Bible (apparatus only; `renderings.md` §4 of the project keeps the words out of the `>` lines)
1. **Dream and interpretation, Joseph and Daniel.** The dreamer "spreads out" (*bur₂*) his dream before the interpreter (25, 42–43): *bur₂* is Akk. *pašāru* "loosen; interpret", the cognate of Aram. `פְּשַׁר` (Dan 2:4; Eccl 8:1 `פֵּשֶׁר דָּבָר`, "who knows the meaning of a thing" ↔ "who knows the heart of the dream", 24). The Hebrew `פרש` keeps the Sumerian double sense ("spread" / "explain") without Genesis's `פָּתַר`. The shepherd-dreamer whose dream is told to his family (Gen 37:2, 5, 10) is the closest biblical figure; but Joseph is betrayed by his brothers, Dumuzid by his friend, and saved (for a while) by his sister. The waking formula (17) ↔ Gen 41:7 `וַיִּיקַץ פַּרְעֹה וְהִנֵּה חֲלוֹם` [divergence: "rose", not "awoke"]. A dream told to a companion who answers with its meaning at once: Judg 7:13–14.
2. **The gazelle's feet.** "Set my hands as gazelle hands, my feet as gazelle feet" (170–171) ↔ `מְשַׁוֶּה רַגְלַי כָּאַיָּלוֹת` (Ps 18:34), `וַיָּשֶׂם רַגְלַי כָּאַיָּלוֹת` (Hab 3:19) – the god who gives the fugitive a deer's feet; and `הִנָּצֵל כִּצְבִי מִיָּד` (Prov 6:5). Shared motif, not a source; ETCSL's "change into" hides the verb "set, place" (*sig₁₀*), close to Hab 3:19's `וַיָּשֶׂם`.
3. **Cords and nets of death.** The *galla* "surrounded him … cord … net" (156–157) ↔ `חֶבְלֵי שְׁאוֹל סְבָבוּנִי; קִדְּמוּנִי מוֹקְשֵׁי מָוֶת` (Ps 18:6); Ezek 12:13. The Psalm's metaphor is here a narrated scene.
4. **The friend's betrayal and the bribe:** Ps 41:10, 55:13–14; Mic 7:5–6 (where the household betrays – here kin is loyal and the friend betrays: [divergence]); Exod 23:8; Num 22:18 (the bribe refused).
5. **The fugitive fed by a woman:** 1 Kgs 17:10–11 (Elijah asks the widow for water, then bread); a woman hides fugitives and does not reveal them: 2 Sam 17:19.
6. **The dream's images in biblical idiom:** the quenched coal = the end of a house (2 Sam 14:7, 31/52); vessels hanging on a peg that falls (Isa 22:25, 33/56); the predator taking a lamb from the flock (1 Sam 17:34, 35); the empty fold and stall (Hab 3:17, 39/260); "lift the eyes" (Gen 13:14, 78).
7. **Mourning:** nature asked to mourn (Jer 9:9 `בְכִי וָנֶהִי`; Amos 1:2 `וְאָבְלוּ נְאוֹת הָרֹעִים`); the sister as mourner (Jer 22:18 `הוֹי אָחִי וְהוֹי אָחוֹת`); laceration (Lev 19:28; Jer 48:37) – the sister promises (69) and does (242–244) what the Torah forbids; the woman going round the streets for the beloved (Song 3:2, 245).
8. **Tammuz.** The closing rubric "a *šir-kalkal* (very precious song) of the dead Dumuzid" (261) places the poem in the cult of the dead Dumuzi that Ezek 8:14 knows (`מְבַכּוֹת אֶת הַתַּמּוּז`) – apparatus only (3, 261); the name is always `דּוּמוּזִי`.
9. **Corrected rank-3 readings** (each checked against the transliteration): "woke up" (17: *zig₃* "rose"); "holy" (*kug* "pure", throughout); "food" (166: *u₂* "grass"); "liver" (74: *bar* "outside, body"); "change into" (170–237: *sig₁₀* "set"); "dances" (169: *e-ne di dug₄* "play"); "my singer" (22: the refrain has "my sister"); 125 assigned by ETCSL to the men of Urim (the structure gives it to Unug); 249 "let us go" (the verb is singular); 232–233 emended by ETCSL to the 1st person (the composite has the 3rd).

### Open decisions (numbered, with my rulings)
1. **tum₂ / de₆ / tum₃** (19–24, 173, 179, 199, 204, 234, 238) = `נָשָׂא`, following Inana 283 (`"שאו!"`) and Lugalbanda and the Anzud Bird 125 (`את חיי אליך נשאתי`). But Inana 371 already gives *gur₃* `נושא` too (and DD 166 copies it): two Sumerian verbs share `נשא` in the corpus. Ruling: followed both precedents; the lead may move *gur₃* to another verb (both Inana and DD would change).
2. **pad₃** split: "find" = `מָצָא` (82, 145–149, 152, 180); "disclose (the place)" = `גִּלָּה` (88, 91–95, 103–107, 138). Inana 395 renders the same idiom *ki … pad₃* "find for you" (`אמצא לך`). Ruling: the split, because the plot turns on *disclosing*.
3. **bur₂** = `פָּרַשׂ` (25, 42–43, 161, 241); 82 *bur₂-bur₂* "loosening" = `הַתָּרָה`. Ruling: one Hebrew where the sense is "spread" (dream, net, cry), a split at 82.
4. **saĝ u₂-a … šub** = `רֹאשׁ בָּעֵשֶׂב הִפִּיל` (transitive, with the head as object); the corpus has *šub* on a corpse `הִשְׁלִיךְ` and intransitive `נָפַל` (57 here). Ruling: `הפיל` (the root of `נפל`), recorded as a third sense.
5. **ze₂-er** (*zir*): Inana's "remove" `הֵסִירוּ`; here "slip away from" = `הִשְׁתַּמֵּט` (172, 178, 203). Ruling: a sense split, noted at 172.
6. **sig "put"** (ORACC one lemma): with *bara-* of the cup "taken down" `הוּרַד` (33, 56, 257); *sig₁₀* "set (as gazelle hands)" `הֵנִיחַ`. Ruling: split by sense.
7. **ma-ra-** in the dream lines (27–38): ORACC parses the 2nd person ("for you") in the dream as in the interpretation. Ruling: `לי` in the dream (ETCSL "for me"), `לך` in the interpretation; `verify` lists 28/46 and 29/48 as "same source, different Hebrew" – justified and noted at 27.
8. **ud ul-e** (15–16) = `בימים רחוקים` (with 139 `מימים רחוקים`); an alternative "late in the day" is noted.
9. **ra** = `מָחַץ` (corpus: *ra* = *maḫāṣu*), also in *izi ra* "strike with fire" = set on fire (59, 251, 253) and *niĝ₂ ra* "throw things" (160): literal, with notes.
10. **šu-du₃ / a₂-la₂** = `כַּבְלֵי־יָד` / `כַּבְלֵי־זְרוֹעַ` (new), *la₂* there = `נִכְבַּל`; `אֲזִיקִים` stays *ĝiš-šu*'s (Gudea; 81). Lugalbanda's ḪAR-GAM is `כבלים` (another text): the lead may wish to check.
11. **dala₂** "thorn" `קוֹץ` (120) against GEN MT.B49 *dala₂* "pin" `סִיכָה`: a sense split.
12. **sig₉** "silence" (53) = `דְּמָמָה` (Lament for Ur 86) and **niĝ₂-me-ĝar** (18) = `דְּמָמָה` (LC 362): two Sumerian words already share one Hebrew in the corpora; kept, flagged for the lead.
13. **232–233** keep the composite's 3rd person (`את ידיו – לידי צבי הניח`) inside the prayer; ETCSL emends. Ruling: follow the source, note it.
14. **ama-Ĝeštinana** (83) = `הָאֵם גֶּשְׁתִּין־אַנָּה` (the creation corpus's precedent, *ama* as a title), not a separate name.
15. **an-za-am** = `גָּבִיעַ`, the Sumerian original of Akk. *assammu*, which OB Gilgameš renders `גביעים`: one Hebrew across the two languages.
16. **ĝidru** (4) of a shepherd's staff keeps the corpus's `שַׁרְבִיט` (the shepherd-king).
17. **Gender in 103–106**: `אל תגלה` (to the friend) against 91–94 `אל תגלי` (to the sister): the Sumerian verb is unmarked; `verify` lists 92/104 and 93/105 – justified.

### Draft for the clean translation's introduction (Hebrew)
"חלומו של דּוּמוּזִי" הוא שיר שומרי בן כמאתיים ושישים שורות, שנשתמר בעשרות לוחות מן התקופה הבבלית הקדומה (המאה ה־18 לפנה"ס, בעיקר מניפור ומאור), ונזכר כבר בקטלוגים הספרותיים של אותה תקופה.
השיר הוא המשכה של "ירידת אִינַנָּה אל הארץ": אִינַנָּה, שעלתה מן הארץ התחתונה, מסרה את בעלה הרועה דּוּמוּזִי לשֵׁדֵי־גַּלָּה במקומה; כאן מסופר הדבר מצדו שלו.
דּוּמוּזִי חולם חלום מבשר רע, ואחותו גֶּשְׁתִּין־אַנָּה, "היודעת את לב החלום", פורשת אותו תמונה אחר תמונה; הוא מסתתר, אחותו מסרבת לשוחד ואינה מגלה את מקומו, ורֵעוֹ מקבל את השוחד ומגלה.
שלוש פעמים הוא נתפס, ושלוש פעמים אוּתוּ, אל השמש ואחי אשתו, שם את ידיו ורגליו כידי צבי – עד שהוא נתפס בדיר אחותו, והחלום מתקיים מלה במלה.
השיר נחתם בכינוי "שיר יקר־יקר על דּוּמוּזִי המת": זהו שיר של האבל השנתי על דּוּמוּזִי, האל שבאכדית נקרא Duʾūzu – "התמוז" שהנשים מבכות ביחזקאל ח:יד.
