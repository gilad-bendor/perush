# Parallels index: the netherworld corpus

The parallels of the two projects – the Descent of Ištar (Akkadian, `IS`) and Inana's Descent (Sumerian, `IN`) – merged by Bible verse. The table below is generated by `build_parallels_index.py` from the **הקבלה** bullets of the annotated files (do not edit it by hand); the key findings above it are written by the coordinator. Tags: [cognate] – the same word by origin; [equivalent] – the same image, idea or formula in other words; [divergence] – the same material, turned another way; [absent] – what one might expect and does not find (guidelines §6.4).

## Key findings

**For the Bible**
1. **Ezekiel 8:14 and its myth.** `וְהִנֵּה שָׁם הַנָּשִׁים יֹשְׁבוֹת מְבַכּוֹת אֶת הַתַּמּוּז` (יחזקאל ח:יד): the Sumerian poem tells how the weeping for Dumuzi began (IN 384: Inana weeps bitter tears for her husband; IN 407–409: half a year he, half a year his sister), and the Akkadian poem ends with the rite itself – the washing, the anointing, the red garment, the flute, and "the wailing men and wailing women" (IS 127–138, *bakkāʾū u bakkâtu*, the cognate of `מְבַכּוֹת` in the same intensive pattern). Between the two texts the reader sees the passage from myth to cult that the prophet saw at the north gate of the Temple.
2. **The land of no return and the rule of Job.** `כֵּן יוֹרֵד שְׁאוֹל לֹא יַעֲלֶה` (איוב ז:ט) is the law of both poems in the same verbs (IS 85 *urid ul īlâ*; IN 194, 287); `וְאֹרַח לֹא-אָשׁוּב אֶהֱלֹךְ` (איוב טז:כב) = IS 6 (= Gilgameš VII 186) and IN 83–84 (*kur nu-gi₄*, the road "whose goer does not return"). The plots are the exceptions – and the exception has a price.
3. **The substitute.** IN 289 "one head in place of her head" and IS 118b, 126 *napṭiru / ipṭiru* "ransom" ↔ `וְאֶתֵּן אָדָם תַּחְתֶּיךָ` (ישעיהו מג:ד), `נֶפֶשׁ תַּחַת נָפֶשׁ` (שמות כא:כג); and in **divergence** `אָח לֹא פָדֹה יִפְדֶּה אִישׁ … וְיֵקַר פִּדְיוֹן נַפְשָׁם` (תהלים מט:ח–ט): for the psalm there is no ransom from death, for the poems there is – another person, Dumuzi.
4. **Love and death.** `כִּי עַזָּה כַמָּוֶת אַהֲבָה קָשָׁה כִשְׁאוֹל קִנְאָה` (שיר השירים ח:ו) is acted out: the goddess of love hands her husband to death with the very "eye of death, word of anger, cry of guilt" that killed her (IN 168–170 = 354–356); then she seeks him like the woman of the Song (IN 390–393 ↔ שיר השירים ג:א; ו:א), under the apple tree (IN 347 ↔ ח:ה; ב:ג).
5. **Dust and clay, the food of the dead** (IS 8 = Gilgameš VII 188; IS 33, Ereškigal eats clay for bread) ↔ `וְעָפָר תֹּאכַל כָּל יְמֵי חַיֶּיךָ` (בראשית ג:יד), `כִּי עָפָר אַתָּה וְאֶל עָפָר תָּשׁוּב` (בראשית ג:יט); and the mourners "sit in the dust" (IN 308, 331, 341 ↔ ישעיהו מז:א; איוב ב:יב–יג).
6. **The mourning rites the Torah forbids** – laceration of the face and body, the single garment (IN 34–39, 176–181, 316–321; IS 81 *guddud appašu*, if *gadādu* "lacerate" is right) ↔ `וְשֶׂרֶט לָנֶפֶשׁ לֹא תִתְּנוּ בִּבְשַׂרְכֶם` (ויקרא יט:כח), `לֹא תִתְגֹּדְדוּ` (דברים יד:א), `וְלֹא יִתְגֹּדַד וְלֹא יִקָּרֵחַ לָהֶם` (ירמיהו טז:ו): in the Sumerian poem they are the virtue of the faithful vizier, and they save a goddess.
7. **The raising of the dead.** `אֱלֹהִים רָאִיתִי עֹלִים מִן הָאָרֶץ` (שמואל א כח:יג) ↔ Ištar's threat "I will raise the dead to eat the living" (IS 19–20 = Gilgameš VI 99–100) and the dead who come up to smell the incense (IS 138); `בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁלִישִׁי יְקִמֵנוּ` (הושע ו:ב), `יִחְיוּ מֵתֶיךָ נְבֵלָתִי יְקוּמוּן` (ישעיהו כו:יט) ↔ the corpse that stands up after three days and three nights (IN 173, 281). The water and the plant of life (IS 114, 118; IN 224–225, 252, 280) are **[divergence]** to the tree of life (בראשית ב:ט; ג:כב): they revive a corpse.
8. **The request that may not be made.** `הִקְשִׁיתָ לִשְׁאוֹל` (מלכים ב ב:י) ↔ IN 191–193 (*al dug₄*, "the *me* that may not be asked") and IS 102 (*erišta ša lā erēši*): the same idea in both languages, one Hebrew (`שָׁאַל`) – and in IN 408–409 the land itself is the one that "asks".
9. **The morning star brought down.** Isa 14 (`הֵילֵל בֶּן שָׁחַר`, `אָרִים כִּסְאִי`, `אַךְ אֶל שְׁאוֹל תּוּרָד`) ↔ the goddess of the morning star who sits on her sister's throne and is brought down (IN 165–172; IS 65): an equivalent of plot, not of words; and the netherworld that "trembles" at the coming of the great (IS 41, 64 *raʾābu* ↔ `שְׁאוֹל מִתַּחַת רָגְזָה לְךָ`, ישעיהו יד:ט).
10. **[absent]** The texts never say "naked", and the translation never does (the biblical stripping of the woman – Hos 2:5, Ezek 16:39 – is a punishment; here it is the law of the place); the cult figures (*assinnu*, *kurgarra*, *gala-tur*, *nu-gig*) are never called `קָדֵשׁ` / `קְדֵשָׁה` (דברים כג:יח), and `שְׁאוֹל`, `תַּמּוּז`, `אוֹב` stay in the apparatus.

**The ancient mind**
1. **The netherworld is a house with laws, not a place of punishment**: gates, a gatekeeper, a vizier, a queen, judges – and *parṣū* / *me*, rules that bind a goddess as they bind every dead person. Enlil and Nanna refuse in identical words because the law is the same; Ištar and Inana are stripped "by the rites of the land".
2. **Death has a mother.** Ereškigal weeps for the young who came before their time (IS 34–36) and lies in pain "on account of her children" (IN 230); she is won not by force but by sympathy (IN 236–239, 263–266) and by a creature outside the order of the sexes (IS 91–92; IN 222–223).
3. **Fertility depends on the goddess**: when Ištar is below, the bull, the ass and the man stop (IS 77–80, 87–90) – sexuality is a divine power, not a private act.
4. **Loyalty and mourning save, indifference condemns**: the mourners in dust are spared, the husband on the seat in the great garment is given (IN 306–358) – the same two verbs (sit, dress) carry the verdict.
5. **The small crosses where the great cannot**: flies and wind at the door (IN 228–229), a fly that finds Dumuzi (IN 394–403), a cult performer made of dirt from a fingernail.
6. **Myth becomes rite**: half a year below and half above (IN 407–409), the yearly mourning with the wailers and the dead who come up to smell the incense (IS 127–138).
