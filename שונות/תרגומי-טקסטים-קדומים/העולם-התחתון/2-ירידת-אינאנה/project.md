## Decisions (beyond `../renderings.md`)
1. **One Hebrew for one formula across the two languages** (`../renderings.md` §2): *kur* `הָאָרֶץ` (= *erṣetu*); *ki* with *an* `אֶרֶץ` without the article; *ki-gal* `הָאָרֶץ הַגְּדוֹלָה`; *ĝarza* `פֻּלְחָן`; *al dug₄* / *al di* `שָׁאַל` (passive `יִשָּׁאֵל`, never the plene infinitive); *a* / *u₂ nam-til₃* `מֵי` / `עֵשֶׂב הַחַיִּים`; *ĝeštug₂ gub* `הִצִּיבָה אֶת אָזְנָהּ` (line 1 and its echo in 175).
2. **Command / execution passages are letter-for-letter copies** with only person and tense changed: 34–40 → 176–182 → 316–325; 41–47 → 183–189, 197–203, 210–216; 48 → 195, 209; 191–194 = 205–208; 227–253 → 254–281; 297–305 = 361–367; 306–310 = 329–333 = 339–343; 168–170 = 354–356; 30–31 → 174A–B, 312–313; 72 → 175B, 315. The only differences are those of the Sumerian (e.g. 260 *šaĝan-na* without *-gin₇*; 270 `אגזור` for 243 `ייגזר`).
3. **The stripping** is described as the text describes it: `הֵסִירוּ מֵעָלֶיהָ` (*lu₂ ba-da-an-ze₂-er*); no "naked" word anywhere (`../renderings.md` §6).
4. *gam* = `הִכְרִיעַ` (ETCSL "kill"); *i-bi₂ uš₂* = `עֵין הַמָּוֶת` (ETCSL "the look of death"); *ḫi-li* = `חֵן` (ETCSL "wig"); *ur₄* by sense (2026-10-21 harmonization, `../renderings.md` §9–§10): `אָסַף` for the *me* (15, 103; = Enki and the World Order 65), `קָטַף` for Ereškigal's hair (235, 262).
5. **The two cult figures and the constables are transliterated**: `כּוּרְגַּרָּה`, `גָּלָה־טוּר`, `שֵׁדֵי־גַּלָּה` (always vocalized; never with the article, which would give `הגלה` "exile"); `נוּגִיג` (221) for *nu-gig*, since `קְדֵשָׁה` is reserved.
6. *u₂* in the *galla* list (297, 361) = `עֵשֶׂב` (ETCSL "food"): the demons "know no plant, no water" – the very words of the plant and water of life; footnoted.
7. *tug₂-mu-dur₇-ra* = `בֶּגֶד־אֵבֶל` (Agade 88), *mu-dur₇* alone = `טִנֹּפֶת` (222–223): the wordplay is explained in the notes (the saviours are made of the stuff the mourners wear).
8. 241 / 268: *dug₄-ga-na-ab-ze₂-en* read as an imperative plural (`דַּבְּרוּ!`), with ETCSL's "I tell you" in the note; 268's unanalysed verb copies it, marked uncertain.
9. Enki's answer (218–221): *kuš₂* = `יָגַע` (laments), the same word as the messengers' "you are weary, our lady" (237): Enki's care passes through his creatures to Ereškigal.
10. Names always vocalized: אִינַנָּה, אֶרֶשְׁכִּיגַל, דּוּמוּזִי, אוּתוּ, שַׁרָא, אוּמָּה, אוּר, נֵטִי, and the transliterated titles above.

## Open decisions (for the user)
1. **230 *ama gan-e nam dumu-ne-ne-še₃*:** "the mother who gives birth, on account of her children" – Ereškigal in labour (Jer 31:14 Rachel; Isa 21:3) or in mourning for dead children? The translation keeps the ambiguity (`בעבור ילדיה`); the notes give both.
2. **233 / 260 *nu-um-gid₂*:** `(בגד) אינו מתוח` (*gid₂* "stretch", as 23) – or ETCSL's "not full like a *šagan* vessel" (her breasts).
3. **241 / 268:** `דַּבְּרוּ!` (imperative, as 248, 250) or Ereškigal's "I speak to you from my heart …" (ETCSL).
4. **297 / 361 *u₂*:** `עֵשֶׂב` (the echo of the plant of life) or `מָזוֹן` "food" (the sense).
5. **301 / 364 *nu-sig₉*:** `אינם שוכבים` (by sense) or a literal "do not place (themselves)".
6. **347 / 348 *{ĝiš}ḫašḫur gul-la*:** `*הגדול*` (ETCSL, reading *gu-la*) or "the destroyed apple tree" (ORACC *gul* "destroy").
7. **409 *bur₂*:** `תשוחרר` (modern but clear) or a biblical alternative (Exod 21:2 `יֵצֵא לַחָפְשִׁי` – an echo the text may not want).
8. **Cross-corpus:** *kur* = `הָאָרֶץ` here vs `הַר` / `אֶרֶץ` in the other Sumerian corpora; *ḫar-ra-an* = `אֹרַח` (Gilgameš) vs the laments' `דֶּרֶךְ`; *ur₄* = `קָטַף`. **Settled 2026-10-21** (the lead's harmonization): *kur* by sense (`הָאָרֶץ` stays here); *ḫar-ra-an* by sense (`אֹרַח` stays); *ur₄* split by sense – `אָסַף` for the *me* (15, 103), `קָטַף` for the hair (235, 262).

## Key findings
**For the Bible**
- **Ezek 8:14** `וְהִנֵּה שָׁם הַנָּשִׁים יֹשְׁבוֹת מְבַכּוֹת אֶת הַתַּמּוּז` ↔ 384 *kug {d}inana-ke₄ mu-ud-na-ni er₂ gig i₃-še₈-še₈* "holy Inana weeps bitter tears for her husband": the mourning of the goddess for Dumuzi, and the half-year (407–409), are the myth behind the rite Ezekiel saw a millennium and more later; the Akkadian poem (`../1-ירידת-אישתר/` 127–138) is its cult form.
- **Isa 43:3–4** `וְאֶתֵּן אָדָם תַּחְתֶּיךָ` and **Exod 21:23** `נֶפֶשׁ תַּחַת נָפֶשׁ` ↔ 289 *saĝ-dili saĝ-ĝa₂-na ḫa-ba-ab-šum₂-mu* "one head in place of her head": the law of the substitute; **[divergence]** Ps 49:9 `וְיֵקַר פִּדְיוֹן נַפְשָׁם` – no ransom from death; here there is one, a person.
- **Job 7:9–10** (`כֵּן יוֹרֵד שְׁאוֹל לֹא יַעֲלֶה`) ↔ 194 "who, having reached that place, would ask to come up?" and 287 "who comes up from the netherworld unscathed (*silim*, `בשלום`)?"; **[divergence]** 1 Sam 2:6 `מוֹרִיד שְׁאוֹל וַיָּעַל`.
- **Hos 6:2** `בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁלִישִׁי יְקִמֵנוּ` and **Jonah 2:1** (three days and three nights) ↔ 173 and 281: after three days and nights the hanging corpse stands up; **Isa 26:19** `יִחְיוּ מֵתֶיךָ נְבֵלָתִי יְקוּמוּן`, **Ezek 37:10** `וַיַּעַמְדוּ עַל רַגְלֵיהֶם`.
- **Song 8:6** `כִּי עַזָּה כַמָּוֶת אַהֲבָה קָשָׁה כִשְׁאוֹל קִנְאָה` ↔ 354–358: the goddess of love gives her husband to death with the very "eye of death" that killed her (168–170 = 354–356). **Song 8:5, 2:3** (the apple tree) ↔ 347–348; **Song 3:1, 6:1** (the woman who seeks her beloved and asks the women) ↔ 390–393.
- **Jer 31:14** (Rachel weeping for her children) and **Isa 21:3** (`כְּצִירֵי יוֹלֵדָה`) ↔ 230 Ereškigal, "the mother who gives birth, on account of her children"; **Jer 4:19, Lam 1:20** (`מֵעַי`) ↔ 236 "oh, my inside!".
- **Lev 19:28, Deut 14:1, Jer 16:6** ↔ 37–39, 179–181, 319–321 (laceration of eyes, nose, ears, thighs; a single garment): the mourning rites the Torah forbids are Ninšubur's virtue, done "for" a goddess not yet dead.
- **Isa 47:1, Jonah 3:6, Job 2:12–13** (sitting in the dust, sackcloth) ↔ 308, 331, 341; **[divergence] Jonah 3:6** ↔ 349: Dumuzi does the opposite of the king of Nineveh – rises to the seat, puts on the great garment.
- **Gen 2:7** ↔ 222–223 **[divergence]**: a creature fashioned from dirt, for a mission to the dead; **Gen 3:22** ↔ 224 **[divergence]**: the plant of life revives a corpse and is given by the god himself.
- **Gen 3:14, Mic 7:17** ↔ 373–378 **[divergence]**: to become a snake (hands and feet "given to the snake") is Dumuzi's rescue.
- **Num 11:5** (fish, leeks, garlic) ↔ 305A–B: the *galla* do not eat the food of ordinary people.
- **Jer 9:20** `כִּי עָלָה מָוֶת בְּחַלּוֹנֵינוּ … לְהַכְרִית עוֹלָל מִחוּץ` and **Joel 2:16, Deut 24:5** ↔ 297–305: death that takes the wife from the husband's lap, the child from the knee, the bride from her father-in-law's house.
- **Job 18:14** `וְתַצְעִדֵהוּ לְמֶלֶךְ בַּלָּהוֹת` and **Ps 91:5–6** ↔ 291–294: the constables of the netherworld as personified powers.
- **2 Kgs 20:5, Ps 56:9** ↔ 376: the god "takes" the tears.
- **Ps 147:1** `כִּי-נָעִים נָאוָה תְהִלָּה` ↔ 412 "your praise is sweet" – sung to the queen of the dead.
- **2 Kgs 2:10** `הִקְשִׁיתָ לִשְׁאוֹל` ↔ 191–193 (*al dug₄*, "the *me* that may not be asked") = Ištar 102: one Hebrew for both (`שָׁאַל`), never the plene infinitive.
- **[absent]** Deut 23:18: *nu-gig* (221) = Akk. *qadištu*, the word behind `קְדֵשָׁה`, but the Sumerian title is untranslatable and is transliterated.

**The ancient mind**
- **The netherworld has laws, not malice**: Enlil and Nanna refuse with the same words because the law is the same – whoever takes the *me* of the netherworld stays there (193). Even Inana's return obeys the law: no one comes up "unscathed", a head must be given for a head.
- **Compassion is the key that opens the land of no return**: Enki does not fight Ereškigal; his creatures repeat her cry and share her pain ("you are weary, our lady – oh, your inside!") and receive the corpse as the reward of sympathy. Ereškigal is a mother in pain, not a monster.
- **Loyalty and mourning save, indifference condemns**: three mourners in dust and the mourning garment are spared; the husband in the great garment on the seat is handed over. The scene is told through two verbs (sit, dress) and their objects.
- **The goddess is both victim and judge**: the eye, the word and the cry that killed her (168–170) are hers at 354–356; the goddess of love is also the giver of death (Song 8:6 in reverse).
- **Smallness passes where power fails**: flies at the door, a wind at the pivot (228–229), a fly that finds Dumuzi (394–403): the creatures of dirt and the smallest animals cross the boundary the great gods cannot.
- **A cosmic compromise**: half a year for Dumuzi, half for his sister (407–409) – the yearly mourning is born of a family arrangement; and the poem ends praising Ereškigal, whose law has held.
