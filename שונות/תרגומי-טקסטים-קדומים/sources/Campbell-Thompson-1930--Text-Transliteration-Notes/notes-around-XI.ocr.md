There are several methods in vogue in the Near East of making bread; the commonest 
shape is that of the flat disk of thin bread varying in size, but usually about a foot in diameter. 
This is probably one of the earliest forms of bread, and it is shown on the Hittite Sculptures, 
where two kings (?) are sharing a feast with this bread piled up in front of them (for the picture, 
see Garstang, Land of the Hittites, pl. xxv); the rounds of bread are to-day piled up in a limp 
flat circular tray-basket (called sufrah). In S. Mesopotamia the oven (¢annur), either in 
bakers’ shops or on the decks of the larger boats, is roughly dome-shaped, either open at the 
top, or closed, having a circular orifice of 18” diameter at one side. The fuel is put in 
from the top, the fire flares up within, and the walls of the oven are thus heated. The baker 
then kneads the flour (¢aZ) in a green glazed bowl (madjen), or a copper bowl (called locally 
injanah), and after the kneading, the leaven (Zamrah, simple dough from yesterday's kneading) 
is added. If no leaven has been retained from yesterday, the dough is left unleavened. The 
baker then makes small cakes (sazak) of this dough, which he pats out to a thin disk about 
10” diameter, which he slaps from hand to hand, and this he plasters on the inner hot wall of 
the oven, where it is baked in about a minute. Ovens exactly like these were discovered by 
Dr. H. R. Hall in his excavations at Ur in 1919.

272. Gilgamish follows the custom of the pearl-divers of Bahrain, long celebrated for its 
pearl-fisheries. In 1916 it fell to my lot in Basrah to interrogate some pearl-merchants who 
wished to return to Lingah, and they told me that the diver descends with a clip on his nose, 
and with one foot in the loop of a rope to which is attached a heavy stone, weighing about 
four Stambul okes at one end, the other end being held by a friend above. The basket for 
collecting the pearls is slung about the diver’s neck, and he has also another rope attached to 
him, which is also held by his friend above, by which he signals to be hauled up. I was told
