## Line numbering
Thompson's English hexameters do **not** run one-to-one with Akkadian lines. The marginal numbers give the Akkadian line count of his own 1930 edition, which is close to the older Haupt/Thompson numbering. Compared with George (BGE 2003 / eBL):
- Same at the start: Thompson 1–~95 ≈ George 1–~95.
- Behind by ~1–3 lines in the 140s–150s: Nisir at Thompson 140 vs George 141–146 (Nimuš); dove at Thompson 145 vs George 148.
- Behind by ~8–10 lines by 190–200: "mouth of the rivers" at Thompson 195 vs George 205–206.
- Misprint on sacred-texts: "[150.]" after [175.] should be [180.].

## Assessment
Thompson re-collated the tablets himself, and the brackets mark restorations and additions exactly. The English hexameter is stilted, archaising "Bible English" (thee/thou, "hath"). The names use old readings: Shurippak, Ubara-Tutu, Ninurta, Nisir (now Nimuš), Puzur-Amurri (George reads Puzur-Enlil, from the logogram ᵈKUR.GAL), Irragal. It is outdated philologically (pre-1930 lexicon, gaps now filled by Assur, Babylon and Nimrud manuscripts). It is still useful as a historical reading and as a literal, bracket-marked witness to how the text was understood c. 1930.
