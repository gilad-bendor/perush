# Berossus (Bērōssos), *Babyloniaka* — the Flood of Xisouthros (Greek tradition)

Berossus, priest of Bel at Babylon, wrote the *Babyloniaka* (c. 290–278 BC) for Antiochus I. Lost; the flood story survives via Alexander Polyhistor → (a) Eusebius' *Chronicle* (Greek lost; **Armenian translation** survives) and (b) George Syncellus, *Ecloga chronographica* (Greek, c. 800 AD, partly via Eusebius/Annianus). A shorter parallel version comes from **Abydenus** (via Eusebius *Praep. Ev.* IX 12 and Syncellus).

Fragment numbering: **Müller FHG II, Berosus fr. 7** (Syncellus p. 30 ed. Paris; Euseb. Arm.) = **Jacoby FGrH 680 F4** (a: Syncellus, b: Euseb. Arm.) = Verbrugghe–Wickersham pp. 49–50; Abydenus = FHG IV p. 280 fr. 1–3 = FGrH 685 F2–3.

## Assessment
For a Hebrew translation of the Greek flood fragment, the base text should be **FHG fr. 7 (Syncellus) as in DFHG** (saved, clean polytonic Greek, with Müller's notes on variants of Scaliger and Eusebius), checked against Jacoby FGrH 680 F4 (restricted) where possible. The Armenian Eusebius version (Karst German, Müller's Latin) differs in details (e.g. boat 15 stadia long in Eus. vs 5 in Syncellus; "pedestrian journey"), which a translation should note.
