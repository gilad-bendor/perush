## Content
Eleven *kirugu* ("songs"), each closed by a *kirugu* rubric line and followed by a one- or two-line *ĝišgiĝal* ("antiphon"): (1) the gods abandon their cities and sanctuaries (1–37); (2) the refrain "O city, bitter is the lament" over Urim and the shrines of Sumer (38–74); (3–4) Ningal's lament: the storm, her flight, her pleas to An and Enlil, the irrevocable decision (75–170); (5) Enlil's storm (171–205); (6) the dead in the streets, the Elamites and Šimaški, Ningal leaves "like a bird" (206–252); (7) Ningal's lament over her house, her people led away, "a strange city built in place of my city" (253–329); (8) the poet addresses Ningal: "how do you exist?", the silent cult, "return!" (330–388); (9–10) the storm that knows no mother (389–418); (11) the prayer to Nanna for the restored city (419–438). Much of the text, especially Ningal's speeches, is in **Emesal** (the "fine tongue" of goddesses and of the *gala* lamentation priests).
