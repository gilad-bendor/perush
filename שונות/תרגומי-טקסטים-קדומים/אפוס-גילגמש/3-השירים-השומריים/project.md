**The ancient mind (for the הערות):** the fear of an anonymous death and the cure of a name; the hero who thinks of death in his prime (HA 20–33); the god of justice (Utu) as the patron of a just expedition; the seven warriors as the god's gift; the guardian's auras as clothes he can take off; the trickery and the pity; the gods' anger at killing; the goddess refused; the king's two councils; the debt of life; the dream of the divine assembly; the place of a dead king among the dead; the household who go with him; the tomb under the river.

### Open decisions
1. **Magan and the `גן` gate** (above): a licence for *ma₂-gan* is needed; until then `verify HA` reports 113.
2. **Plural *ur-saĝ* = `לוחמים`** (36, 58A): the singular stays `גיבור` (GEN), but the plural `גיבורים … הם` would reproduce Gen 6:4 `הֵמָּה הַגִּבֹּרִים … אַנְשֵׁי הַשֵּׁם` in a poem about the name, and the gate reserves `גבורים` (the plene `גיבורים` would slip past it). A split by number; HB (14 *ur-saĝ*) should follow. Accept, or prefer another plural?
3. ***ur₂* = `חיק`** throughout HA (Utu to his mother's lap, 80; "the child of the lap", 134; the captive to his mother's lap, 162, 173; and "the lap of the ramah" = its foot, 83, 148 …), against GEN's `שֹׁרֶשׁ` (a tree's roots): the Sumerian plays on one word for the mother's lap and the mountain's foot. A sense split, recorded.
4. ***la₂***: `מתח` (25 the neck, 28 "the tall man does not stretch to heaven", 67 terror, 78 the shadow) and `כפת` (152b, binding arms). Sense split.
5. ***gub***: `הציב` with ear / foot (1, 54, 56, 95; GEN), `העמיד` with name (6–7; §23.3), `עמד` intransitive (83, 88, 132, 166). Three Hebrew for one verb, by construction, as fixed by the corpus.
6. ***dim₂* `אֵעָשֶׂה`** in 91 (*ak* = `עשה` elsewhere): `ייבנה לי` (Akk. *banû*) is unintelligible there. ***zi … pad₃*** "swear (lit. find the life of)" = `נשבע` in 157 (*pad₃* = `מצא` elsewhere): idiom by sense.
7. ***umun₂*** (119) and ***uš₂*** (103A) both `דם` (umun₂ italic): ORACC and ETCSL read *umun₂* "blood"; no better word.
8. **Uncertain words left in italics** (decide when an edition is available): *muš-šag₄-tur₃* `*נחש־הרחם*` (38), *muš-saĝ-kal* `*נחש־הראש*` (41), *ma₂-ur₃* `*מגררות־הספינות*` (44, 60), *ĝi₆-edin* `*חשכת־הערבה*` (56), *ma₂-da-la₂* `*ספינת־הגרר*` (107, 115), 115's *zi-šag₄-ĝal₂* line, *u₃-luḫ … lum-lum sud-sud* `*שופע הפרי, ארוך הנצר*` (130, 164; ETCSL reads a sceptre), *gar₃ ĝeštin* `*פְּקַעַת־הגפן*` (150), *zid₂-eša* `קמח־*אֵשָׁה*` (148O), the stones *du₈-ši-a*, *nir₇* transliterated (148TT; not `בְּדֹלַח`, Gen 2:12).
9. **27 *nam-ba-ak-e*** read as a statement ("so it will be done to me"), not a prohibitive: the context and *ḫe₂-me-a* decide; the note gives the other reading.
10. ***inga-*** rendered by context: `עוד` (the "Nth time" formula), `כבר` (122), not rendered in the praise lines 133–134 / 168–169 (emphatic).

### Key findings
**The ancient mind.** The poem's question is death and its answer is a name: Gilgameš sees one anonymous corpse floating past the wall (23–26), knows that "the tall man does not stretch to heaven" (28), and goes to "set up his name" – and, where no name stands, the gods' name (5–7): the expedition is also an act of piety. Utu accepts not the kids and the sceptre but the **tears** as the gift (34). The guardian's **terrors are property** – cast (126), handed out one by one in exchange for gifts (145 …), and finally, after his death, his **auras distributed** by Enlil to the field, the river, the reeds, the lion, the palace, the forests and the prison goddess (193–199): everything fearsome in the world is an inheritance from the dead guardian. The Sumerian keeps two words apart (*ni₂ te* "terror" given by Ḫuwawa with *ba* "allot"; *me-lem₄* "aura" given by Enlil with *šum₂* "give"), which ETCSL's English blurs. The **trick** is told with humour (a king of Kiš offered as "my big sister", shoes for big and small feet, mountain stones brought to the mountain) and with unease: the captive weeps, says he never knew mother or father (155), and Enlil asks "Why did you do this?" – Ḫuwawa should have eaten their bread (187–192). The **mothers** run through the poem: the bachelors leave because they have no house (50–53), their mothers wait in the square (84), Gilgameš swears by his mother, Ḫuwawa praises it, Gilgameš wants the captive returned "to his mother's lap" (162), and Ḫuwawa's curse – "you will not return to the city of your mother who bore you" (125) – is turned by Enkidu against Gilgameš (174). Wordplay kept in the Hebrew: *ur₂* `חיק` (mother's lap = the mountain's foot); *la₂* `מתח` (the neck stretched over the wall, the tall man who does not stretch to heaven, the evening shadow stretched over the mountain); *dim₂-ma* `טַעַם` (the mountain's "decision" is Utu's, 11; the lofty king "has no sense", 170, with *sukud* "tall" from 28); *kud* `כרת` (the Mountain of Cedar-cutting, the branches, the neck); *nu₂* `שכב` (the sleeping king who does not rise, 74–77; the felled cedars "laid down", 148); *kur₉* `בוא` (enter the mountain, 5 = enter the flesh, 144).
**The Bible** (all shared motifs, not sources; the full list in the index):
- **The muster and its exemptions** (50–53) ↔ Deut 20:5–8 [equivalent/divergence]: the men with a house go home – here because the expedition takes only those who have nothing to lose.
- **"Two are better than one … the threefold cord"** (107–111) ↔ Qoh 4:9–12 [equivalent]: the closest wording – a threefold thing that no one cuts – after a sequence on two men together; the Hebrew `משולשת` is the natural word, and the parallel is real.
- **The kiss that is a blow** (151) ↔ 2 Sam 20:9–10, Joab and Amasa [equivalent].
- **The king who spares a captive king and the rebuke** (159–174) ↔ 1 Kgs 20:31–42, Ahab and Ben-Hadad (`וְהָיְתָה נַפְשְׁךָ תַּחַת נַפְשׁוֹ` ~ "you will not return to your mother's city") and 1 Sam 15:33, Agag [equivalent/divergence]: in the Bible the prophet speaks for God; here the slave, and the god (Enlil) is angry at the killing.
- **A marriage offer as a ruse before killing** (139–149) ↔ Gen 34:13–16 [divergence] (our proposal).
- **The oath "by the life of"** *zi* (90 …) ↔ `חֵי פַרְעֹה` (Gen 42:15) [equivalent]; heaven and earth as witnesses (157) ↔ Deut 30:19.
- **The name** (5–7) ↔ Gen 11:4 `וְנַעֲשֶׂה לָּנוּ שֵׁם` [equivalent/divergence], 2 Sam 8:13, 2 Sam 18:18 (Absalom's pillar "to keep my name in remembrance"); **the cedar-cutter king** (61–64, 148) ↔ Isa 37:24, 14:8 [equivalent].
- **The saying 28–29 in its three forms**: Sumerian statement (tied to the plot: "the mountain" he will enter); OB III 140 question ("who has gone up to heaven?", same conclusion: make a name); Dialogue of Pessimism 83–84 question with the opposite conclusion; Deut 30:12 / Prov 30:4 have the question's form (`מי יעלה … השמימה`), i.e. the Akkadian shape, not the Sumerian. The Akkadian changed *sukud* "tall" → *arku* "long", *la₂* "stretch" → *elû* "go up", *kur* "the mountain" → *erṣetu* "the earth", *šuš* "cover" → *gamāru* "encompass"; only "to heaven" and "wide" (*daĝal* = *rapšu*) survive – and only they are the same Hebrew.
- **Gen 3:24** (the guardian's flame) ↔ the auras (193): tagged [equivalent] as a motif only; the Hebrew avoids `להט` everywhere (40).
- **Corrections of ETCSL that matter**: 23 "people are dying" is one man (*lu₂ ba-uš₂*); 26 "corpses … make the river almost overflow" is one corpse floating in water; 101 "face" is *igi* "eye"; 174B "wig" is *ḫi-li* "allure" (the same ETCSL slip as in Inana's Descent); 186 "he spoke angrily" is "his heart was seized at Gilgameš's word"; 126 ETCSL's passive is ORACC's active (Ḫuwawa casts the terror).

### Key findings
**What B changes against A – the ancient mind of a second telling:**
- **The opening**: B begins with the praise lines that A gives to Ḫuwawa and to Enkidu in mid-poem (HA 130–132, 164–167): the catalogue title *i₃-a lum-lum* is A's flattery made into B's hymnic incipit.
- **The lament goes down**: where A says "the tall man does not stretch to heaven, the wide man does not cover the mountain" (HA 28–29), B speaks of "the great city, the land of submission, that lets no man go" and says "the long one does not block the land (*kur*), the wide one does not cover the land" (11–13): the limit is below, the netherworld, not above. **The Akkadian saying draws on both traditions**: DP 83 *ayyu arku* "who is long" = B's *sud* (not A's *sukud* "tall"); DP 84 *erṣeta ugammeru* "encompass the earth/netherworld" = B's *kur* as netherworld; but DP 83 *ana šamê elû* "to heaven" = A's *an-še₃*. The Hebrew shows it: `ארוך`, `הארץ`, `רחב` are B's and DP's words; `אל השמים` is A's and DP's.
- The man in the water is still "a man" (*lu₂*), not "a corpse" (8; HA 26); the liver (*ur₅*), not the heart, is struck (5, 9).
- **Personal religion**: B adds "my god Enki, lord Nudimmud" to the oath (16, 87, 100, 110), and Ḫuwawa throws it back: "you swore by your mother and father, by your god Enki" (137–139).
- **The seven are stars**, said in so many words (45–46), who know the road to Aratta, like merchants and like doves in the mountain clefts (47–49) – the knowledge of the road, not force.
- **The muster** sends home "who has a wife … who has a son" (53–55) – closer to Deut 20:7 and 24:5 than A's "house" and "mother".
- **Gilgameš wakes himself** (83–84; in A, Enkidu wakes him).
- **The guardian's power is *me-lem₄* "aura"** in B (72, 130), *ni₂ te* "terror" in A (HA 67, 145–149) – kept apart in the Hebrew (`זוהר` / `אימה`, §23.6).
- **The gifts are shoes "made" for him** (*dim₂*, 102–103, 112–113), small and big together; **the blow is to the ear, without the feigned kiss** (131; HA 150–151).
- **Ḫuwawa's accusation** (137–141): "you laid down a lie, you cut an oath" – B makes the broken oath explicit, where A has only "hero, … falsehood" (HA 152B). Gilgameš's pity follows the accusation (142).
- **The reason for mercy is use** (144–146): Ḫuwawa will be a guide who "knows" the stations of the road, and carry all Gilgameš's things; Enkidu's answer: he will blur the mountain paths, and "we will not return to the city, the mother who bore (us)" (157–158; A: "you").
- **The orphan of the rock** (164–166): "my mother who bore me – a cave in the mountain; my father who begot me – a cave in the range; Utu, you made me dwell alone in the mountain" – A's "I never knew mother or father … you raised me" (HA 155–156) turned into an image, and Utu no longer a foster father but the one who set him there alone.
- **Variants of A that are B's main text**: HA 24 (2 mss. *šag₄ ḫul*) = HB 6; HA 43 (1 ms. *a₂-bi*) = HB 44; HA 152 (1 ms. *saĝ-ki … guru₅-uš*) = HB 132; HA 152a–b (2 mss.) = HB 133–134; HA 174A–B (1 ms.) = HB 152–154. The two versions were not sealed off from each other: copyists of A borrowed B's lines.
**The Bible** (shared motifs): Deut 20:7, 24:5 (the husband exempted, 53); Num 10:31 (the stranger who knows the road as guide, 145); Josh 9:15, 19 (an oath to the deceived/deceiver that binds the stronger, 137); "cutting" an oath ~ `כרת ברית` (137); Num 30:3 (the oath "that goes out of the mouth", 139); Song 2:14, Jer 48:28 (the dove in the rock clefts, 49); Judg 5:20 (the stars, 45); Jer 2:27 (parents who are rock and wood, 164); Lam 3:28 (to sit alone, 166); Job 7:9 (no return from the land of the dead, 11).

### Key findings
**The ancient mind.**
- **The goddess holds the king; she does not woo him.** Inana's speech (B7–B11) is a refrain of possession – "my wild bull, my man – I will not release you!" – and what she wants is the king in her house, judging "in my Eana, in my holy *ĝipar*": the king's sacred office as judge in the goddess's temple. Gilgameš's answer (B13–B18, U3.B1–B4) is a refusal of confinement – "let no one wrap my heroic arm in a garment" – and a wish to go out to the mountains for wild bulls and sheep, silver and carnelian. The Akkadian SB VI turns this into a marriage proposal and a catalogue of Ištar's lovers; the Sumerian has neither.
- **An is a father who gives in.** He refuses twice with a herdsman's reasons (the bull will foul the water and scatter dung; it has no pasture on earth – "its pasture is on the horizon", B47); his third answer is "I *cannot* give" (B49, *-da-*), and the threat of a cry that will "come near" heaven and earth makes fear "come near" him (B51–B52, the *teĝ₃* pun). The SB keeps the father and the request in the same words (`אבי, את שור השמים – תנה לי`) but makes the threat the raising of the dead (VI 99) and the condition seven years of chaff for Uruk (VI 103–111).
- **The Bull is an animal, gigantic.** It eats grass and drinks water like any bull – a league of river at a gulp, "its heart is not filled" (B59) – cuts the date palms and bends them to its mouth (B61), and "fills" Uruk by standing in it (B62). Its death is told in two farm images: it falls "like rain, like the harvest" (MT.D48).
- **The comedy of the minstrel.** While the city is devoured the king drinks and calls for a song; the minstrel answers in the Bull's own words: "you – eat grass, drink water; and I – what is this to me?" (MT.D8–D9; the third manuscript of B67A: "in this matter – nothing of yours"). ETCSL's "you may eat, and you may drink" drops the grass and the water, and the joke with them; the Hebrew keeps it because *u₂* = `עשב` throughout.
- **The kill as a sacrifice and a feast for the poor.** The threat and its execution "as he said" (MT.D28–D32 → MT.D54–D58) divide the body like a slaughtered animal's: the hide to the street and the flayer, the guts to the square, the meat in baskets to the widows' sons of the city, the horns as oil flasks for Inana in Eana. The divine meat feeds the orphans; the horns go back to the goddess who sent the bull; and the poem ends in her praise, "for the death of the Bull of Heaven – holy Inana, your praise is sweet" (MT.D59): defeat turned into offering.
- **"There being no master cook"** (MT.D49): the king takes the knife himself because no cook is there – ETCSL's "just as if he were a master chef" reverses a negation (*nu-me-a*, as *me₃ nu-me-a* "there being no battle", B3). The SB's `כטבח אמיץ ומיומן` (VI 145) may rest on the same misreading of the Sumerian line.
- **The haunch is a blow, not a gesture.** In the Sumerian the king strikes Inana with the *zag* (side / right side = Akk. *imittu*) and makes her fly off "like a dove", and destroys the wall she watched from (MT.D50); then he weeps at the bull's head (MT.D51). The SB moves the act to Enkidu, who throws the *imittu* before Ištar, and gives the weeping to Ištar and her women (VI 155–159).
- **The SB's Bull of Heaven scene draws on the Sumerian in detail**: Enkidu "behind the bull" seizing its tail (B89 and its variant = VI 141–142), the dung (B35 *šurum* ~ VI 127), the guts (B80 → VI 157, the threat moved from the bull to Ištar), the horns as oil vessels (B83 → VI 162–165, dedicated to Lugalbanda instead of Inana), Ištar on the wall (B84 → VI 151).
- **A shared hymnic unit**: the praise "Ho, fruitful one …" followed by "fear not" (B91–B97 = HA 130–135; MT.D39–D41) – a ready-made block of praise for Gilgameš that the poets set into different poems (also HB 1–3).
- **The oldest manuscript** (U3, Ur III): the king in the marsh, bending the reeds with his oar and "covering them with water" – a king as a man of the marshes; and already Gilgameš's refusal of Inana's "gift" (U3.B1–B4).
**The Bible** (all shared motifs, not sources; the full list in the index):
- **Behemoth** (Job 40:15, 23): the giant beast that "eats grass like an ox" and "trusts that the Jordan will burst into its mouth" – the closest biblical figure to the Bull of Heaven grazing Uruk and drinking its river (B47, B58). With it the insatiable (Qoh 1:7; Prov 30:16, B59) and the ox licking up the field (Num 22:4, B57, B60).
- **The woman who seizes the man who refuses her** (Gen 39:7, 12): [divergence] – Potiphar's wife wants him in her bed, Inana wants the king in her temple as judge; the Hebrew avoids `שִׁכְבָה` / `תָּפַשׂ` for it. Song 3:4 `אֲחַזְתִּיו וְלֹא אַרְפֶּנּוּ` and Gen 32:27 `לֹא אֲשַׁלֵּחֲךָ` give the gesture of the hold (*šu bar* = *wuššuru*).
- **Meat for the widow and the orphan** (Deut 14:29): the king's distribution of the bull's meat to the widows' sons (B82, MT.D30, D56) – in the Torah a fixed duty, here a victor's largesse.
- **The cook and the haunch** (1 Sam 9:23–24; Lev 7:32 `שׁוֹק הַיָּמִין`): the right haunch as the special portion of a slaughtered animal – in MT.D49–D50 the king is his own cook and the haunch is thrown at the goddess.
- **The horn of oil** (1 Sam 16:1; 1 Kgs 1:39): the bull's horns as oil vessels for the temple (B83, MT.D32, D58).
- **The unbroken calf** (Deut 21:3; Num 19:2; Jer 31:17): *amar ĝiš nu-zu* "a calf that has not known wood" (B88) – the yoke as "wood" in Sumerian, as "yoke" in Hebrew.
- **"Fear not"** before battle (Num 21:34; Isa 41:10): B97, after the praise (= HA 135).
- **Feasting with music while the city falls** (Isa 22:13; Amos 6:5–6): the king's banquet and the minstrel's mockery (MT.D5–D9) – without a prophet's rebuke: the scene is comic.
- **The woman watching from above** (2 Sam 6:16; Judg 5:28): Inana on the wall (B84). **The dove that flies off** (Ps 55:7; Hos 7:11): MT.D50.
- **Corrections of ETCSL that matter**: MT.D49 "as if" for "there being no" (a reversed negation); MT.D8 the dropped "grass" and "water"; B39–B40 an added "if"; B48 an added "only"; B62–B63 "submerged" for *si* "filled"; B63 "aura" for *ni₂* "terror"; B81 / MT.D31 "carcass" for *kuš* "hide"; B13 a first-person "I shall certainly not" for a third-person prohibitive.

### Key findings
**The ancient mind.**
- **One question, two councils, two answers.** Gilgameš puts "the word" (*inim*) before the elders and before the young men in the same lines – the wells to be finished, "to the house of Kiš let us not bend the neck" – and both councils repeat his wells word for word: the elders conclude "let us bend the neck, let us not strike" (14), the young men answer with a proverb about the burden of service – "standing and standing, sitting and sitting, walking beside the king's son, holding the donkey's bridle – who has the breath for it?" (25–28) – and refuse. The city's labour (wells, escorts, caravans) is the issue: submission means corvée.
- **The king follows the young men, trusting Inana (16), and is right.** The poem praises the city as "the work of the gods", its wall "set on the ground by An" (30–36): the young men's argument is the city's sacredness and the enemy's weakness.
- **The comedy of the siege**: Aga asks a captured envoy "Slave! that man (on the wall) – is he your king?" twice (69, 91); the beaten envoy describes his king in a conditional prophecy – "were he my king … would he not cast down myriads …, capture Aga in the midst of his army?" (71–81) – which comes true "as he said" in the very words (94–99). The battle itself is never told; Enkidu goes out alone (88).
- **The king's body is his power**: the furious brow, the bison eyes, the lapis beard, the fine fingers (72–75), and his aura (*me-lem₄*) "covering the old and the young of Kulaba" (85).
- **The debt of life**: the victor calls his captive by the titles of his own superiors (overseer, officer, governor, general, 102–103) and repays a past kindness – "Aga gave me breath, Aga gave me life; Aga took the fugitive to his lap, filled the fleeing bird with barley" (104–106) – "before Utu", the god of justice, and sends him home (112–113). Unlike Ḫuwawa (HA 159–179), the spared captive lives.
- **The poem plays with the neck**: "bend the neck" (*gu₂ ĝar*, submission, 8–29) against "stretching the neck over the wall" (*gu₂ la₂*, 66, 89 – the line of HA 25, where Gilgameš saw a corpse in the river): the man who will not bow shows his neck to the enemy.
- **The council motif reaches the Akkadian epic**: OB III 179–200 (and SB II–III) set Gilgameš before "the elders of Uruk" (who warn "you are young, Gilgameš, your heart carried you", OB III 191) and the young men before the cedar expedition – the pairing of AG, in a new story, where the elders' warning is not refuted.
**The Bible** (shared motifs, not sources):
- **1 Kgs 12:1–16, Rehoboam's two councils – a [divergence] of the deepest kind.** The structure is the same – the king consults the elders, rejects their word, consults the young, follows them – and in both the question is service and its yoke (1 Kgs 12:4 `אָבִיךָ הִקְשָׁה אֶת עֻלֵּנוּ` ↔ the young men's proverb of service, AG 25–28; the elders' "be a servant to this people today", 12:7 ↔ the elders of Uruk's "let us bend the neck", AG 14); in both the young men answer with a saying (12:10 `קָטָנִּי עָבָה מִמָּתְנֵי אָבִי` ↔ AG 25–28). The values are reversed: in Kings the elders counsel service *to one's own people* and are right, the young counsel a heavier yoke and the kingdom splits; in AG the elders counsel service *to a foreign king* and are wrong, the young men refuse the yoke and the city is saved. The Hebrew avoids `הַיְלָדִים`, `נוֹעָצִים`, `עֲצַת`: the young men are `בחורים`, the counsel is `דבר`.
- **The neck in the yoke** (Jer 27:11–12 `הָבִיאוּ אֶת צַוְּארֵיכֶם בְּעֹל מֶלֶךְ בָּבֶל … וִחְיוּ`): Jeremiah's counsel is the elders of Uruk's – submit and live.
- **The besieger's envoy and the men on the wall** (2 Kgs 18:17–36): a great king's envoys come to a smaller city to demand submission; the king trusts his god (18:5 ↔ AG 16 "trusting Inana"); the dialogue is held before "the people on the wall" (18:26–27). Here the city's envoy goes out, and the king himself appears on the wall.
- **The wells** (Gen 26:15, 18): wells as the labour of generations and as the object of a quarrel between peoples; their mouths "filled with dust" as an act of war (Gen 26:15 ↔ AG 79, 97; 2 Kgs 3:19).
- **The debt of life repaid** (1 Sam 24:18–19, 1 Sam 26:24; 1 Kgs 2:7; Josh 2:12): sparing or releasing an enemy as the return for a past kindness, before God (AG 112 "before Utu"); **the fugitive harboured by a foreign king** (1 Sam 27:2, David with Achish; 1 Kgs 11:40) and **the fugitive as a wandering bird** (Prov 27:8; Ps 11:1) – AG 105–106.
- **The victor releasing the captured king** (1 Kgs 20:31–34): Ahab releases Ben-Hadad by a treaty and is rebuked by a prophet (20:42); Gilgameš releases Aga by gratitude, and the poem praises him.
- **"Who will go?"** (Isa 6:8; 1 Sam 17:8): the volunteer for a dangerous mission (AG 53–57).
- **The king known by his appearance** (1 Sam 10:23–24; 16:12): AG 69–75.
- **Myriads** (1 Sam 18:7): the king measured by the multitudes he strikes (AG 76).
- **Corrections of ETCSL that matter**: 29 "you old men … we young men" adds nouns the Sumerian lacks; 23 "never before have you submitted" for a prohibitive; 32 "created" for *ak* "made"; 17, 41 "advice" for *inim* "word"; 86 "he armed" against the ergative subject (the young men arm themselves); 112 a first-person passive for a third-person active.

### Key findings
**The ancient mind (Nibru).** The poem mourns a man who has not yet died: the refrain "he has lain down and will not rise again" runs over his titles while he still lies sick, "held by the bolt of Namtar" – the demon whose name is "fate". In his sleep the gods both judge and console him: kingship was carried to him as his fate, life for ever was not (E14); his death was fixed "with the cutting of your umbilical cord" (E18: *gi-dur* is literally "the reed cord" – the cord was cut with a reed); and six lines say what has already "reached" him (*sa₂ dug₄*, ḫamṭu – ETCSL's "awaits you" hides that it is told as done): the dark day, the lonely place, the wave, the battle without escape. The same verb was used of his own deed – he "reached" Ziusudra (D6): the one who reached the survivor of the flood is himself overtaken. The comfort is rank and memory: Sisig the dream-god will light the dark for him, no torch is lit at the ghosts' festival without him (E10–E11), youths wrestle before his statue (E8–E9; the feats of strength he was "perfect" in, A7). The "other Nibru version" gives the burial: his household are "laid down in their places" with him – *nu₂*, the verb of the refrain, now causative (N2.7) – and he weighs out gifts to each god of the netherworld by name (N2.9–N2.28), down to the primeval ancestors of Enlil and the dead priests. Mourning is scratching the nose and tearing hair (I7–I8, N2.33), and the people "put no property" (N2.35).
**The Bible** (shared motifs, not sources):
- **Job 14:12** `וְאִישׁ שָׁכַב וְלֹא יָקוּם` ↔ the refrain [equivalent]: the same two verbs (*nu₂* / *zi* = `שכב` / `קום`), the same negation; and Job 14:7 `וְיֹנַקְתּוֹ לֹא תֶחְדָּל` ↔ *isimu₂* `יונקת` (N2.40, MT.K10): Job gives the tree a shoot and denies man rising; the poem gives the dead king offspring and a name.
- **Qoh 9:12** `כַּדָּגִים שֶׁנֶּאֱחָזִים בִּמְצוֹדָה רָעָה וְכַצִּפֳּרִים הָאֲחֻזוֹת בַּפָּח` ↔ G12–G14 [equivalent]: bird, fish and the one who "holds" (*dab₅* = `אחז`, the Bible's verb) as the image of death's hour – the closest wording in the poem. **Qoh 8:8** `וְאֵין מִשְׁלַחַת בַּמִּלְחָמָה` ↔ E22 "the battle from which there is no escape" [equivalent].
- **Ezek 16:4** `לֹא כָרַּת שָׁרֵּךְ` ↔ E18 [equivalent], the same verb (*kud* = `כרת`): the cutting of the cord on the day of birth.
- **Lev 19:28** `וְשֶׂרֶט לָנֶפֶשׁ` ↔ I7, N2.33 [divergence] (the same act and verb, *ḫur* = `שׂרט`, forbidden there); **Deut 26:14** `וְלֹא נָתַתִּי מִמֶּנּוּ לְמֵת` ↔ the gifts and the ghosts' festival [divergence].
- **Gen 23:16** `וַיִּשְׁקֹל אַבְרָהָם` ↔ N2.9 [divergence] (decision 36); **Isa 14:9** ↔ F8 "will go to you" [equivalent]; **Jonah 2:7** `הָאָרֶץ בְּרִחֶיהָ בַעֲדִי` ↔ A17, Namtar's bolt [equivalent]; **1 Sam 28:13** `עֹלִים מִן הָאָרֶץ` ↔ G15 [divergence]; **Ps 21:5** (life asked and given "for ever") ↔ E14 [divergence]; **Gen 3:22** ↔ E14 [equivalent].
- **Corrections of ETCSL that matter**: E19–E24 "awaits you" is a perfect "has reached you"; C7 "you will be accounted a god" is "he is counted *with* the gods" (third person, *diĝir-da*); B4 "for six days" is "on the sixth day"; E25 "in anger" is not in the text; N2.7's passive is active ("he laid down"); G14 "spread his net … catch you" is "dragged the net … holds"; H8 "the earth cracked dry" is "he split the ground"; N2.9 "set out" is "weighed out" (*la₂*).

### Key findings
**The ancient mind (Me-Turan).** Told twice, the dream is the core: the assembly's list of deeds includes reaching Ziusudra and restoring the *me* "effaced" by the flood (F15–F18) – the hero who brought back what the flood destroyed; and Enki's answer turns the flood into the moment the gods fixed human mortality: "since you made me swear by the life of heaven and earth – that since then no man shall live" (F34–F35, F127–F128). Ziusudra was the exception, and the oath closes the door behind him; the question "will he not escape, because of his mother?" is answered with rank, not life: general of the netherworld, chief of the ghosts, a judge whose word is "heavy" like Ningišzida's and Dumuzi's (F38–F41). The poem plays on its verbs: **one verb, *ḫalam* `כחד`, for the effaced *me* (F16), the flood that was to efface the seed of mankind (F31), and the names that, once spoken, are not effaced (K8)**; one verb, *bur₂* `פשר`, for loosening the knotted heart "like palm-fibre" (F153) and solving the dream (H8–H9); *zi* / *zig₃* "rise" for the king who will not rise (A1), who rises from sleep (F88), the city that rises to bury him (H10–H15), and the river water that "rises" from its bed (H20); *ḫur* for scratching the face in mourning and drawing the tomb's plan (H6). The burial is a royal work of the whole people – "the levy of Unug was a flood, the levy of Kulaba a cloud" (H14–H15) – the Euphrates opened and diverted, a stone tomb with gold beams built in its bed, the household laid in it, and the water let back: "those who seek it – let them not find its place" (H31). The poem ends not with the king but with Ereškigala, mother of Ninazu, praised (K12), after the consolation for everyone: statues made "since distant days", set in the temples, names spoken that are not effaced, and Aruru giving a shoot (*isimu₂*) to carry them (K5–K11).
**The Bible** (shared motifs; the flood story is the one line of real transmission, through Akkadian):
- **The oath after the flood** (F34–F35) ↔ Isa 54:9 and Gen 9:11 [divergence]: the Bible's God swears never again a flood; Enki swore never again an everlasting man. With **Gen 6:3** [equivalent] (the limit on man's days), but placed after the flood, not before it; ETCSL's "for ever" is not on the tablet (kept in brackets).
- **The sole survivor** (F33, F126 `לבדו`) ↔ Gen 7:23 `אַךְ נֹחַ` [equivalent]; **his immortality** ↔ Gen 9:29 `וַיָּמֹת` [absent] and Gen 5:24 (Enoch) [divergence].
