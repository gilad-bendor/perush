### Key findings
**Bible**
1. **Numbers 23:19 in two lines** (ii 17′–18′): "Mankind is treacherous; I am she – the one who says, the one who does." Balaam's oracle has both halves – `לֹא אִישׁ אֵל וִיכַזֵּב` (במדבר כג:יט) and `הַהוּא אָמַר וְלֹא יַעֲשֶׂה` (במדבר כג:יט) – with the same two verbs (*qabû* / `אמר`, *epēšu* / `עשה`). And *anāku šî* "I am she" is Deutero-Isaiah's `אֲנִי הוּא` (Isa 43:10; 46:4; Deut 32:39) in the feminine, in an Assyrian oracle that promises that what is said will be done (Isa 46:11; Ezek 12:25; 17:24). We rendered *ṭullumâ* `בוגדת`, not `כוזבת`, so that the Hebrew does not borrow Num 23:19's word.
2. **Genesis 28:15 in iv 9′**: "[Wherever you g]o, I will guard you" = `וּשְׁמַרְתִּיךָ בְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר תֵּלֵךְ` (בראשית כח:טו) – the promise to Jacob (and Josh 1:9 after "do not fear"); it stands on a restoration (*[bēt talla]kūni*).
3. **Deuteronomy 32:10–11's sequence**: "he surrounded him … guarded him … like an eagle over its young" – the goddess "goes around you and guards your guard" (i 18′–19′), "like a winged bird over its young" twitters and circles (ii 6′–8′), and "between my wings I raised you" (iii 27′). *naṣāru* is the cognate of `נצר` (Deut 32:10 `יִצְּרֶנְהוּ`).
4. **Exodus 14:14** (iii 11′): "You – be silent, Esarhaddon!" after the goddess's war on the rumour-mongers = `יְהוָה יִלָּחֵם לָכֶם; וְאַתֶּם תַּחֲרִשׁוּן` (שמות יד:יד); with ii 2′ ("in your palace, sit tight") the king's part is stillness (Isa 30:15).
5. **"I am your father, your mother"** (iii 26′): one goddess in both parental roles, against Ps 27:10 (God in place of father and mother) and Jer 2:27 (idols called "my father" and "you bore me").
6. **The northern enemies as in the Bible**: the Mannean and the Urarṭian together (iii 13′) as `אֲרָרַט מִנִּי` (Jer 51:27); and since Sennacherib's murderers fled to Ararat (2 Kgs 19:37), "I will seal the Urarṭian's writings" reads as intelligence against the exiled brothers. Our proposal.
7. **Psalm 25:16's pair** "alone and afflicted" (`יָחִיד וְעָנִי`) ↔ *ēdu … ḫablu* (iii 16′), with *ḫabālu* = `חבל` [cognate] (Job 34:31; Aramaic `חֲבָל`, Dan 6:24).
8. **Tribute "brought"** (iii 25′ *ubbala*) = `יוֹבִילוּ … שָׁי` (Ps 68:30): *wabālu* is the cognate of `יבל`; together with `סוּגַר` and `מִדָּה` the whole formula is cognate.
9. **Babylon and balal** (ii 24′): the gods of Esaggil "in a field of *mixed* evil" (*ballu*, from *balālu* = `בלל`) – the root of Gen 11:9's pun, about the same city; the pun is Hebrew only, the coincidence is worth a note.
10. **"The kingship of heaven"** (iii 33′–34′), restored (*šarrūtu ša šamê*) – cf. Dan 4:34 `מֶלֶךְ שְׁמַיָּא` and the Rabbinic `מלכות שמים`.
11. **Isa 31:5** ("like hovering birds, so the LORD will protect Jerusalem") is the closest biblical form of ii 6′; and **Isa 10:14** (Assyria's king: "no one chirped") is answered by the goddess's chirping over him (ii 7′). Our proposal.
12. **Nahum 2:12** – Nineveh as the den where "the lion's cub" walked – against the goddess "like a fine cub in your palace" (ii 9′). Our proposal.
13. **Divine anger and reconciliation**: "the angry gods I will reconcile with Assyria" (iii 20′; the Šamaš Hymn's *zenû* + *salāmu* D) – with Esarhaddon's own "seventy years" of Marduk's anger (Jer 25:11; 29:10) in the background.
14. **Rank-3 corrections:** "righteous" (iv 24′) is *kēnu* "loyal" (would import a moral term); "(lion) cub" is an interpretation (*mīrānu* is first a puppy); "well sheltered" hides "in the shade" (*ṣillu* = `צל`, Ps 91:1, 121:5); "I am one who says" hides *šî* ("she"); "be sent out" is an active plural on the tablet; "and" is added in several asyndetic pairs (i 7′, iii 26′, iii 30′). None manufactured a Bible parallel; "well sheltered" and "one who" hide two.

**The ancient mind**
1. **A deity in every protective form**: a skilled boatman holding the ship in a good harbour (i 16′–17′), a bird circling and chirping over its young (ii 6′–8′), a puppy sniffing out intruders in the palace (ii 9′–10′), a parent who is father and mother (iii 26′), who raised the king between her wings and holds him in the crook of her arm (iii 27′, 30′; iv 22′–23′), a cow with her calf (iv 20′), a sentry awake at day and dawn (ii 4′; iv 5′).
2. **The fear of a court after a parricide**: "mankind is treacherous" (ii 17′); "the disloyal" searched out and handed over (ii 29′–33′); people asking "when, when will the change in the land come?" and refusing to stay in Calah and Nineveh (iii 7′–10′); the envoys of Elam and Mannea screened, the Urarṭian's letters sealed, Mugallu's *igib* cut (iii 12′–15′). The oracle is also political intelligence.
3. **Gods in exile, gods who faint from hunger** (ii 24′–27′): Babylon's gods "in the steppe of evil", languishing; two burnt offerings must be sent at once – a god without temple and offerings weakens. The rebuilding of Babylon (iv 4′) is the background.
4. **Many divine voices**: "we are the goddesses" (i 8′); Ištar and Mullissu speak one "word" (ii 30′); the prophetess Urkittu-šarrat ("Urkittu is queen") and the goddess Urkittu (iv 8′).
5. **The king's part is stillness and praise**: sit tight in your palace (ii 2′), be silent (iii 11′), praise me (ii 21′), gather my words inside you (ii 23′).
6. **The king's mother** is addressed twice in the feminine (i 13′, iv 28′): Naqia asked, and the goddess answered her too.
7. **Continuity as hope**: "as at the first, so be it at the last" (i 17′–18′) – the Assyrian hope is that nothing changes; the enemy is precisely "change" (*nakkuru*, iii 9′; *nakru* "enemy" is the same root).
8. **The prophets**: a man from Assur (Nabû-hussanni, "Nabû, remember me!"), Bayâ again (masculine gentilic, female determinative in SAA 9 1), La-dagil-ili ("he does not see god"), and a woman from Calah (Urkittu-šarrat).

### Open decisions
1. **Section boundaries.** 2.4 is taken as ii 29′–iii 18′ across the break between columns ii and iii (≈ 4 lines), with a second heading at ii 38′; a ruling may be hidden in the break (then iii 1′–18′ would be a separate oracle). Column iv is taken as one section 2.6, but iv 8′ ("[I am Urk]ittu") may open a new oracle. Parpola's printed 2.4–2.6 were not available to check.
2. **ii 19′ *marʾutu hubburtu*** (crux): `בת הוֹמִיָּה – אנוכי`, without `את`, so that both readings stay open (Parpola: the object of ii 20′; or the goddess's self-description).
3. **ii 2′ *kammusāka*** (restored): `[היה כורע?]` keeps *kamsu* = `כורע` (Gilgameš, SAA 9 5); the NA sense "stay put" is in the note. Alternative: `[הישאר?]`.
4. **barāmu = `חתם`** (iii 14′) collides with *kanāku* = `חתם` (EST *kanikūni* `חתום`; Enūma eliš *iknukam-ma* `חתם`); `סתם` is *pehû* (LH). Synonyms ("to seal"); kept `אחתום` with a note. The coordinator may prefer a split.
5. **bêru = `בחר`** [cognate] (iii 5′, 13′) collides with EST's *ḫiāru* "choose" = `יבחרו`. Proposal: `בחר` for *bêru* (the cognate) and another verb for *ḫiāru* if the treaty corpus is revised; or accept the overlap.
6. **ṭullumâ = `בוגדת`** (ii 17′), not `כוזבת` (echo control; the Num 23:19 parallel is in the apparatus).
7. **maqlūtu = `קְלִיָּה`** (ii 25, `שתי קְלִיּוֹת`), keeping the root *qalû* = `קלה`; alternatives `שְׂרֵפָה`, or `עוֹלָה` tagged [equivalent].
8. **šiālu = `*צִפָּה*`** (iv 21′ `*אֲצַפֶּה*`; Parpola "cover"), **ṣipputu = `*כיסוי*`** (iii 21′; alternative *ṣippatu* "orchard"), **sapāku = `*תקף*`** (i 11′), **ušanṣakka = `*אגבירך (עליהם)*`** (ii 12′), **šētuqi = `*להרחיק*`** (iv 1′): uncertain words in italics.
9. **iv 5′ *etkāku*** = `עֵרָה אני` assumes the goddess speaks (the stative does not mark gender).
10. **iv 8′ *naʾʾidāni*** read as singular `הללני`, with T1's decision 3.
11. **Gtn** (*assanamme*, *sitammeā*) not rendered by a separate word.
12. **iii 33′–34′**: *šarrūtu* is LUGAL + restored *-ūtu*; "the king of heaven" (*šar šamê*) would also fit the signs.
13. **ii 23′ *bētānu* = `פנים`** (`בפנימך`): unusual Hebrew; alternative `בקרבך` (but *qerbu* = `קרב` is reserved as a cognate).

### Deviations from the corpus renderings (with reasons)
- **§2.11** *akī ša pānīti lū ina urkīti*: the table leaves the verb open (`כֵּן?`); drafted `כאשר בראשונה – / [יהי ב]אחרונה` (*lū* = `יהי`, like `יהיו` in SAA 9 1 ii 37′), split over i 17′–18′ as on the tablet.
- **§2.12** `בִּנְךָ וּבֶן בִּנְךָ יַעֲשׂוּ מַלְכוּת`: drafted in T1's order, object first (`מלכות לפני נִינוּרְתָּה יעשו.`), as the coordinator asked.
- **§2.3** colophon `מִן פִּי`: ii 28′ has *issu pî ša* PN, rendered `מן פי של` (the extra *ša* kept).
- **§2.5** `מִתְבּוֹנֵן בַּצִּפֳּרִים`: the plural sign is restored, so `מתבונן בציפו[רים]`.
- **§2.8** `זְרִיחַת הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ … שְׁקִיעַת הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ`: an added `(ו)` before the second (ii 36′), since the two stand as one vocative pair.
- **§3 *agappu***: `צִפּוֹר אֲגַף` as fixed; the young of the bird = `בָּנֶיהָ` (*māru*), not `גּוֹזָל` (reserved) or `אֶפְרוֹחַ` (*watmu*).
- Plene forms of vocalized table strings: `אנוכי`, `גידלתיך`, `מבטח אשור`, `עגלי`, `הללני`, `מתוך`; `אֲגַף` / `אֲגַפַּי` and `בִּנְךָ וּבֶן בִּנְךָ` kept vocalized as in T1.
