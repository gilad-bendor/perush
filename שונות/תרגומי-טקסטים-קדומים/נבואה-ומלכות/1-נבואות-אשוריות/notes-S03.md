### Key findings
**Bible**
1. **Deuteronomy 8 in one Assyrian oracle (iii.7–15).** "You say in your heart … you will eat bread and forget this covenant; when you drink this water you will remember me and keep this covenant" has, in order, the elements of Deut 8:10–18: eating (8:10, 12), forgetting (8:11, 14), "you say in your heart" (8:17), "remember … to establish his covenant" (8:18), and of Deut 6:11–12, 31:20 and Hos 13:6 (eat, be sated, forget). The verbs are equivalents, not cognates (*mašû* / `שָׁכַח`, *ḫasāsu* / `זָכַר`), except *akālu* / `אָכַל` and *naṣāru* / `נָצַר` (Deut 33:9; Ps 25:10; Ps 78:7, which has the pair "not forget / keep" exactly). The reversal is the finding: in Deuteronomy the people forget the God who fed them; here the gods forget the goddess who fed them, and she complains of hunger (3.5).
2. **The covenant rite of Exodus 24, in other materials (ii.27–iii.15).** A covenant document is brought, the god's text is read aloud before the party it concerns (`וַיִּקְרָאֵהוּ שָׁפָן לִפְנֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ`, 2 Kgs 22:10, is the same sentence as ii.32), sacrifices are made, a liquid is sprinkled (*zarāqu* = `זָרַק`, cognate: oil here, blood at Sinai, Exod 24:6, 8), and a drinking follows (Exod 24:11). The direction is reversed: the tablet states what the god does for the king.
3. **"I heard your cry" and the covenant (ii.13–14, 27)**: Exod 2:24 "God heard their groaning (`נַאֲקָתָם`) and remembered his covenant"; Exod 3:7; Ps 18:7–15 (the royal psalm: the king's cry, the god hears from his temple, descends with hail and coals of fire) is the closest whole scene to ii.10–23.
4. **The theophany weapons, turned against Assyria in Isaiah**: fire from heaven devouring rebels (Num 16:35; 2 Kgs 1:10), stones and heat rained on them (Exod 9:23–24; Gen 19:24; Ezek 38:22) – and Isa 30:30–31 uses the same arsenal "for Assyria shall be shattered". Our proposal.
5. **The recognition formula after blood in the river** (ii.23–25): Exod 7:17–20 has the same order – "by this you shall know that I am YHWH", the river turned to blood; here "I filled the river with their blood; let them see and praise me, that I am Aššur, lord of the gods". And iv.20–21 repeats it for Ištar (`[בזא]ת תראה [כי אשתר של] ארבאיל אנוכי`, partly restored).
6. **`בְּרִית … שָׂם לִי` (2 Sam 23:5)** is the Hebrew counterpart of *adê šakānu* (iii.15): the deity "sets" a covenant for the king; the same verb (`שָׂם` is our fixed rendering of *šakānu*). Ps 89 has the dynastic covenant too, but conditional (89:31–32).
7. **The deity's reproach for ingratitude and the neglected table** (iii.18–29): Mal 1:6–13 and 3:8 (the despised table, "will a man rob God?"), Isa 43:23–24, Mic 6:3, Hag 1:4, 9. Ps 50:12–13 and Deut 32:38 reject the very idea of a hungry god that Ištar expresses.
8. **Small true cognates made visible**: *ṣīt Šamaš* / `כְּצֵאת הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ` (Judg 5:31, the same phrase for the victor; ii.7); *tamlû* / `מִלּוֹא`; *ḫirṣu* / `חֲרִיץ` (1 Sam 17:18); *ṣarṣaru* / Mishnaic `צַרְצוּר` (m. Kelim 3:2, "its measure is in water"); *sippu* / `סַף`; *kapāpu* / `כָּפַף`; *zaqīpu* / Aram. `זקף` (Ezra 6:11); *zarāqu* / `זָרַק`; *kalû* / `כָּלָא`; *urqu* / `יָרָק`; *sūtu* / `סְאָה` (Gen 18:6: seahs for the divine guests' meal).
9. **Rank-3 corrections:** Parpola's "he shines as brilliantly as the sun" hides *ṣīt Šamaš* "the going out of Šamaš" (= Judg 5:31's `כְּצֵאת הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ`); "(hail)stones and fire of heaven" adds "hail" and "of heaven" (the tablet: "stones, heat"); "Fragrant oil is sprinkled" is "good oil" (the Bible's `שֶׁמֶן טוֹב`) with an active verb; "incense is burnt" is "aromatics go"; "(But when)" (iii.12) and "(knowing)" (ii.25) are additions; "my banquet" has no "my"; "sweet beer" is "good beer" (*ṭābu*); "establish" (iii.32) belongs to iii.34; "covenant" = *adê* (`אֲמָנָה`). Only the first hides a Bible link; none manufactured one.

**The ancient mind**
1. **A covenant that binds the gods.** The gods are invited by Ištar as "my fathers, my brothers", given water from a special jug, and told: "you will forget me; this water will make you remember". The oath is drunk, so that it lives inside the body of the one who swore (cf. EST §72: the oath enters the belly like bread and wine).
2. **The deity who needs to be fed.** Ištar is offended, hungry and thirsty, keeps looking out for the food, lists the menu she wants (bowl-bread, a seah-jug of good beer, greens and stew, a full cup) and wants "to restore her vigour" (*lalû*, the Gilgameš word for pleasure and luxuriance). Mesopotamian gods were fed daily; a god without offerings weakens – and a goddess can say so to the king through a prophet.
3. **Reciprocity, said aloud.** "As if I did nothing for you! Did I not bend the four door-jambs of Assyria, did I not vanquish your enemy, gather your haters like moths? And you – what have you given me?" The relationship of god and king is an exchange of victory for provisions, and the prophet is the goddess's voice in the negotiation.
4. **The civil war of 681–680 seen from the temple.** The traitors who agitated against the king, drove him out and surrounded him; the god who sallies from the gate of heaven, burns, stones and slaughters them; the palace staff who rebelled impaled "by their teeth"; the courtiers of right and left made to stand by "bearing punishment". The oracles are both theology and political justice.
5. **The written word as a cult object.** The "šulmu" is "placed before" Bel-Tarbaṣi, the gods and the Image; the covenant tablet "enters" on a cushion, is anointed with good oil, honoured with sacrifices and aromatics, and read aloud – a text treated like a divine statue.
6. **Two gods, one tablet.** Aššur speaks as a warrior in the masculine and ends "that I am Aššur, lord of the gods"; Ištar speaks as hostess and creditor and ends "that I am Ištar of Arbela". Both want to be seen and acknowledged.

### Open decisions
1. **ii.24 `יִרְאוּ` and the `ירא` gate.** `prophecy_tools.py verify` flags `יראו` ("let them see", *lēmūru* < *amāru*) as the reserved root ירא (*palāḫu*). The Hebrew is right and has a precedent (Gilgameš I 222 `יראו את פניך` for *līmurā*); a gate fix: exempt the form `יראו` (and `תראה`-type forms are already free) when the licence text has *amāru* / *lēmūru* / *līmurū*, e.g. add a negative lookahead or an allow pattern `amāru\[|lēmūru|līmur`. The coordinator decides (we did not edit the tool).
2. **ii.13 *anīna*** = `*אָנָּא*` (uncertain translation). Parpola: "Hear me!". `שמעני` would merge it with *šemû* (ii.14), `ענני` is *apālu* (T2), `הקשיבה` is *šemû* Gtn in Gilgameš.
3. ***etēqu* Š** (i.26 `יעביר`, as LH and Etana) and T1's ***ebēru* Š** (S01 iv 4 `אעבירך`): two verbs, one Hebrew in this corpus.
4. ***ḫatāpu*** (ii.22) = `שחט`, also *šagāšu* (Gilg. XI 72) and *palāqu* (Ludlul V 57) in earlier corpora.
5. ***zarāqu*** (ii.29) = `זָרַק` [cognate], while Gilgameš and Etana use `זרק` for *nasāku* "throw": recommend `זָרַק` for *zarāqu* everywhere (the cultic cognate) and a different verb for *nasāku* if one meets it again.
6. ***sippu*** = `מזוזה` (Gilgameš, wisdom), but LH has `סף`; the cognate `סַף` is noted in iii.20.
7. **ii.16 *naqallulu*** = `*הבזקתי*` (uncertain; SAAo "issue as a fiery glow", others "hover").
8. **iii.24 *kurṣiptu*** = `עָשׁ` (EST already uses `פרפר (?)` for *burdišaḫḫu*).
9. **iii.7–14 addressees**: the gods (ii.35 "gods, my fathers and brothers"; their "cities and districts" are their cult centres) or the Assyrians (as i.27)? The notes lean to the gods.
10. **New words for the user's taste:** *ṣarṣaru* `צַרְצוּר` (Mishnaic, not biblical); *maštītu* `כלי־משתה`; *akussu* `נזיד`; *akālu* (inf.) `אֹכֶל`; *ḫaṣānu* `גונן`; *namāšu* `נסע`.
11. **ii.21 *anqullu*** = `חֹרֶב` (wisdom corpus); unvocalized `חורב` looks like Horeb – vocalized, and noted as a Hebrew-only coincidence.
12. **iv.22 *parriṣu*** (restored) = `כוזב`, kept apart from ii.10 *sarsarru* = `שקרן`; T2 avoided `כוזב` for *ṭullumû* because of Num 23:19 – here the word is restored and the echo is not claimed.
13. **i.9 the prepositions**: *ana šamê u kaqqiri* = `לשמיים וָאָרֶץ` (one `ל`, no article), so that EST's merism `שָׁמַיִם וָאָרֶץ` stays whole and the Akkadian single *ana* is kept; alternative `לַשָּׁמַיִם וְלָאָרֶץ`.
14. ***timmu*** (i.25) = `עמוד`, which Gilgameš V 317 has for *šukû*.
