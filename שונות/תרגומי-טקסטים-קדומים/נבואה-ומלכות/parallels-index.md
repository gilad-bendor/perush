## Key findings for the Torah and the Bible
1. **Genesis 15 spread over one tablet (SAA 9 1).** "Fear not" (`אַל תִּירָא`, *lā tapallaḫ*, 23× in SAA 9), "I am" (*anāku* = `אָנֹכִי` [cognate]), "I am your fine shield" (o.iv.19 *arītka deʾiqtu anāku* = `מָגִנְּךָ הַנָּאֶה אָנֹכִי` ↔ `אַל תִּירָא אַבְרָם אָנֹכִי מָגֵן לָךְ` (בראשית טו:א)), the heir (iv 5, 20; Gen 15:3–4), "the issue of my heart" (v 19), smoke, fire and torches at nightfall (iv 30–32, vi 14–17; Gen 15:17). The vision as *diglu* = `מַחֲזֶה` (SAA 9 11 r.6; Gen 15:1).
2. **The salvation oracle of Deutero-Isaiah, word for word, with the opposite message**: "the latter shall be like the former" (SAA 9 1 ii 37′ `הָאַחֲרוֹנוֹת יִהְיוּ כָּרִאשׁוֹנוֹת`) against Isa 42:9, 43:18; "mankind is treacherous – I am she, the one who says and does" (SAA 9 2 ii 17′–18′; Num 23:19's two verbs, Isa 46:11, and *anāku šî* = Deutero-Isaiah's "I am he", in the feminine); the tired goddess (SAA 9 9 o.15 `יגעה אני`) against Isa 40:28 [divergence].
3. **"I made you trust"** (*takālu* D, `הִבְטַחְתִּיךָ`, SAA 9 1 ii 8′, iv 1) is the verb of the Assyrian envoy in 2 Kgs 18:30; "do not trust in mankind" (ii 27′) ↔ Ps 146:3, Jer 17:5.
4. **The covenant of Aššur (SAA 9 3) and Deuteronomy / Exodus 24.** iii 7–15 has Deuteronomy 8's sequence: "you say in your hearts", eat bread, forget the covenant, drink, remember and keep (*naṣāru* = `נָצַר` [cognate]) it – with the roles reversed (the gods forget the goddess who fed them). ii 27–32: the covenant tablet on a cushion, oil sprinkled (*zarāqu* = `זָרַק` [cognate]), sacrifices, read aloud before the king (*ina pān šarri isassiu* = 2 Kgs 22:10's sentence). ii 14 "I heard your groaning", then the tablet = Exod 2:24.
5. **The goddess as mother and nurse** (SAA 9 1 iii 15′–18′ midwife and wet-nurse, *mušēniqtu* = `מֵינֶקֶת` [cognate]; SAA 9 7 r.6–10 carried on the hip, between the breasts, milk by day and watch by night) ↔ Isa 66:11–13 in nearly the same order, Isa 49:23, 46:3–4; Num 11:12 [divergence: Moses refuses to be the nurse]. The wings and the bird over its young (SAA 9 2 ii 6′–8′, iii 27′; *agappu* = `אֲגַף` [cognate]) ↔ Deut 32:10–11 in the same order.
6. **Prophecy as a profession and a written record**: the colophons "by the mouth of PN" (six of the eight named prophets of SAA 9 1 are women); the scribe records that the prophet repeated a phrase "five, six times" and then "I will finish Elam" (SAA 9 8) ↔ Elisha's "five or six times … until you had finished" (2 Kgs 13:18–19; *gamāru* = `כִּלָּה`); the riddle with the hearer's question (SAA 9 7 r.3–5) ↔ Jer 1:11–13; the oracle of `שָׁלוֹם` ↔ Jer 28:9.
7. **The nations and the king** (SAA 9 7 o.8–13): the kings say "come, let us go to Assurbanipal … let him decide between us" ↔ Isa 2:3–4 / Mic 4:2–3 (*parāsu* = `פָּסַק`, not `שָׁפַט`; "come" restored); the king shows them their borders ↔ Deut 32:8 and Isa 10:13. Biblical names of peoples: Gomer (the Cimmerians), Ararat and Minni (Urarṭu and the Manneans, SAA 9 2 iii 12′–13′, Jer 51:27; our proposal: intelligence on the regicides who fled to Ararat, 2 Kgs 19:37).
8. **Advice to a Prince and 1 Samuel 8, item by item**: the citizens' silver (12), conscription (32 ↔ 8:11), fields changed and given to others (35–36 ↔ 8:14), ox-teams (35 ↔ 8:16), the flock tax last in both lists (37 ↔ 8:17), the eunuchs (`סָרִיס` [cognate], ↔ 8:15). The fodder levy (29) ↔ 1 Kgs 5:8, and the reverse of Deut 17:16. The bribe (*ṭātu* = `שֹׁחַד`) is taken by the scholar (42), the "wise" of Deut 16:19; the text speaks of *dīnu* `דִּין`, never of `מִשְׁפָּט`. The basket of forced labour (*tupšikku* = `סַל`, *zabālu* = `סָבַל` [cognate]) ↔ Ps 81:7; the exemption (*šubarrû*) ↔ Ezra 7:24. The gods leave their sanctuaries (54) with Ezekiel's verb (Ezek 8:6; 10–11), after the scribe-god Nabû (49), as after the scribe of Ezek 9.
9. **What the corpus does not share** [absent]: no covenant law, sin or condition in the oracles to the king (contrast Ps 132:12; 2 Sam 7:14); `בְּרִית`, `שָׁמַר`, `עוֹלָם` never appear (*adê* = `אֲמָנָה`, *naṣāru* = `נָצַר`, *dārû* = `נֶצַח`).
10. **Corrections of the rank-3 translations that change a parallel**: Parpola's "righteous" (*kēnu* "loyal"), "Palace of Succession" (`בֵּית הַיְּרֻשָּׁה`), "well sheltered" (hides `צֵל`, Ps 91:1), "pomegranate" (*armannu* "apricot"; Song 4:3 is not a parallel); Foster's reversed line 6 of the Advice (no negation), his "he" for "they", and his "labor obligations" (48) that hides *adê*.

## The ancient mind (the second purpose)
The gods speak through ecstatic men and women of Arbela, whose names are prayers ("Ištar, do not neglect me!", "I have seen her strength"). The goddess is mother, midwife, nurse, cow with her calf, bird, puppy, boatman and warrior; her body pays for her love (kneeling, nursing, sleepless, worn by sun and rain in the steppe, SAA 9 9). She is offended when her meal is neglected and asks the king "what have you given me?" (SAA 9 3.5). The fears are those of a king after a murder in the palace: poison ("safe bread, safe water"), rumours ("when will the change come?"), shifting loyalties. The Advice shows the other pole: the scholars' claim on the king, the privileges of the old cult cities, and a moral world of measure for measure written into the vocabulary itself.
