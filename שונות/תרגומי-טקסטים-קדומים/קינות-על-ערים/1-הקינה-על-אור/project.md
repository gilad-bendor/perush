## Scope

## Line numbering
ETCSL = ORACC = Kramer 1940 = Samet 2014 (436 lines in the critical count; the rubric lines are counted as lines; the lettered lines are pluses of some manuscripts).

## Why this text (guidelines §1, both purposes)
- **Bible:** the closest Mesopotamian counterpart of Lamentations (the weeping goddess, the deity "like an enemy", the dead in the streets, the silent festivals, "how long?", the strange city); the departure of the gods from their sanctuaries (Ezek 8–11; Ps 78:60; Jer 12:7); a divine decree that cannot be revoked (Num 23:19–20; Isa 55:11); the storm-day (the Day of the Lord); the restored city (Jer 30–31, Isa 54, 60).
- **The ancient mind:** a city's life is its god's presence; the goddess pleads but cannot change the assembly's decision; there is no sin of the city – the decision is simply the gods'; the lament itself is an act addressed to the gods, to soothe their hearts so that the city may be rebuilt.

### Key findings (run 1)
**Bible.**
1. **Lam 2:6 `וַיַּחְמֹס כַּגַּן שֻׂכּוֹ` has its image in LU 122** (the house "pushed over on its side like a garden fence") and **Isa 1:8 `כְּסֻכָּה … כִּמְלוּנָה בְמִקְשָׁה` in LU 127–128** (a hut on the stripped harvest ground, exposed to wind and rain), in one lament, five lines apart; with Isa 38:12 `כְּאֹהֶל רֹעִי` in 131 (the shepherd's fold uprooted).
2. **The irrevocable word, one-to-one:** 149 *gur* (= *târu* = `שוב`) "did not turn back" ↔ Num 23:20 `וְלֹא אֲשִׁיבֶנָּה`, Isa 55:11, Isa 45:23, Jer 4:28 `וְלֹא אָשׁוּב מִמֶּנָּה`; 167 *kur₂* "change" (`שנה`) + 168 *ka-ta e₃-a* (`מוצא פיו`) = **both halves of Ps 89:35 `וּמוֹצָא שְׂפָתַי לֹא אֲשַׁנֶּה`**; and the grammar of 167 (*nu-kur₂-ru-dam* "is not to be changed") = Dan 6:9 `דִּי לָא לְהַשְׁנָיָה` (same root, Aram. שנא). [divergence]: the biblical God who "does not change" also "repents" (Amos 7:3, 6; Exod 32:14): the gods of Ur do not.
3. **The failed intercession, step by step against Moses** (Deut 9:18–26): she weeps and pleads (144–145, `וָאֶתְחַנַּן`), "do not destroy" (146–148, `אַל תַּשְׁחֵת`), prostrates herself (153, `וָאֶתְנַפַּל`) – **a second time** (151 *2-kam-ma-še₃* ↔ `כָּרִאשֹׁנָה`, Deut 9:18) – and is not heard (149–150 ↔ `וַיִּשְׁמַע יְהוָה אֵלַי גַּם בַּפַּעַם הַהִוא`, Deut 9:19). With Abraham (Gen 18:23–32) and Amos 7 the plea succeeds or argues from justice; Ningal never argues guilt or innocence. And she is bound to the city's fate (165–166) – where Jer 15:1 merely rejects the intercessors.
4. **[absent] No sin of Ur** in 1–170: the abandonment (1–34) has no cause; the decision is the assembly's word (139–141, 161–168). In Lamentations the sin is the cause (1:5, 1:8, 1:18). The rank-3 translation's "righteous" (*zid*, 41, 118, 121) and "innocent" (66–67) would import a moral claim the Sumerian does not make (*zid* "true, proper").
5. **Lam 5:2 `נֶהֶפְכָה לְזָרִים … לְנָכְרִים` ↔ LU 69 *šu bal … kur₂*** with the same verb (`הפך`) and the same adjective (*kur₂* = *nakāru* ~ `נכר`); and the rites "made foreign" (68) ↔ Jer 19:4 `וַיְנַכְּרוּ אֶת הַמָּקוֹם הַזֶּה` [divergence: there by human cult, here of itself].
6. **The weeping mother:** the ewe whose lamb is "cut off" (66) is *u₈* = Akk. *laḫru*, the cognate of `רָחֵל` – Jer 31:14 `רָחֵל מְבַכָּה עַל בָּנֶיהָ` (the coordinator's "Jer 31:15" is 31:14 in the Hebrew numbering); with the cow for its calf (102; 1 Sam 6:12) and the cow whose voice is gone (37; Jer 9:9 `וְלֹא שָׁמְעוּ קוֹל מִקְנֶה`).
7. **Day and night, the bed without rest:** 91–100 (day: storm; night: lament; "in my bed of the night … no stillness was laid", *lib*) ↔ Ps 22:3 `וְלַיְלָה וְלֹא-דֻמִיָּה לִי`, Ps 6:7 `יָגַעְתִּי בְּאַנְחָתִי … מִטָּתִי` (with *kuš₂* = `יגע` in 36, 45), Jer 45:3, Job 7:13.
8. **The deity as a bird over the city**: Ningal "beat her wings like a bird of heaven and flew to my city" (105–106) ↔ Isa 31:5 `כְּצִפֳּרִים עָפוֹת כֵּן יָגֵן יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת עַל יְרוּשָׁלִָם` [divergence: there it saves].
9. **The Day/storm:** *ud* "day" = *ud* "storm" (87–100, 136–138); `ביום ההוא` ↔ Zeph 1:15, Ezek 7:7, Lam 1:12 – but a Day without judgement. ETCSL's "created" (138, *dim₂* "fashion") would echo Isa 45:7 `וּבוֹרֵא רָע`: a false echo.
10. **Give him no rest:** 79–80 (so that the lord would toil for his city and not sleep) ↔ Isa 62:6–7 `וְאַל תִּתְּנוּ דֳמִי לוֹ`; against Ps 121:4 (the God who does not sleep).

**The ancient mind.**
1. A city is its god's household and herd: the whole first song is a cattle-pen and a sheepfold left to the wind; the gods are "the wild bull", "the lady of the house"; the citizens are lambs and kids; the house is a garden fence, a field-hut, a fold. Destruction is described as the end of an estate.
2. **All Sumer, one lament:** the first song lists every city from Nibru to the sea-port Gu-aba, including Isin, the seat of the new dynasty under which the poem was composed: the lament for Ur is a lament for the Land.
3. **The goddess between her husband and the assembly:** Ningal cannot act; she can only trouble her husband (79–80), weep before An and Enlil, prostrate herself – twice – and then she is bound to her city's fate. The god of the city (Nanna) is himself "the man of tears" (45–46): the patron is the chief mourner, not the destroyer.
4. **The decision is speech**: the gods "said" (139–140), "commanded" (141, 161–162), "decreed the fate" (163); the assembly sits "to bind the word with the tooth" (152); and An's word "is not to be changed", Enlil's utterance "not to be overturned" (167–168). The storm is how a spoken decision acts in the world.
5. **Emesal on the page:** the goddess's speech and the refrains are in the women's dialect; in 100 the same word for "quiet" appears in both dialects side by side (*mu-uš-la₂* / *ĝiš-la₂*).
6. **The liver**, not the heart, is the seat of mood: the temple was the place where "the liver of the black-headed was pleasant" (115); depression is "a dead liver" (119).
7. **The lament as a rite**: *kirugu*/*ĝišgiĝal* rubrics, refrains repeated 35 and 23 times, the *balaĝ* (85): a text for performance, probably at the demolition/rebuilding of temples, to "cool the heart" of the gods (*šag₄ sed₄*, 150 – the very thing Enlil refused Ningal).

### Open decisions (run 1)
1. **41/73, 44/74 (renderings §3 revised):** `עירו הנאמנה ההרוסה – אנחתה מרה היא.` / `אוּר ההרוסה שלו – אנחתה מרה (היא).` instead of the pre-set `בְּעִירוֹ …` / `בְּאוּר …`: the genitive *-ak* + resumptive *-bi* is an anticipatory genitive ("his destroyed city – its lament"); the `ב־` is ETCSL's "in", not Sumerian. Confirm.
2. **45–46 etc.: `איש הדמעות`** for *mu-lu er₂-re* (pre-set `הַבּוֹכֶה`): the Sumerian is a noun phrase ("the person of tears"), and its *-e* is the ergative of the intransitive-with-agent *mu-un-kuš₂* (ORACC *mu.n*). ### Key findings (run 2)
**Bible.**
1. **The Day of the Lord, word by word.** The storm passage (172–205) has, in the order of a few lines, the fire "before" the coming god and the flame that "blazes" behind (186–187 ↔ Joel 2:3, both verbs, *bar₇* `בער` / *tab₂* `להט`), the darkness at the horizon (185 ↔ Joel 2:2, Amos 5:20), and above all **188–190 ↔ Amos 8:9 `וְהֵבֵאתִי הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ בַּצָּהֳרָיִם וְהַחֲשַׁכְתִּי לָאָרֶץ בְּיוֹם אוֹר`**: "at noon" (*an-bar₇*), "in the land" (*kalam*), "the bright day" (*ud zalag*) he "did not bring out" (Isa 13:10 `חָשַׁךְ הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ בְּצֵאתוֹ`). [divergence] the biblical Day judges sin; Enlil's storm judges nothing – it is the assembly's word (202 "the storm that Enlil spoke"; Lam 3:38).
2. **Lamentations 2 and 4, image by image:** the citizens as potsherds (210 ↔ Lam 4:2 `לְנִבְלֵי חֶרֶשׂ`), the little ones lying "in the bosom of their mothers" (228 ↔ Lam 2:12 `אֶל חֵיק אִמֹּתָם`, *ur₂ ama-ba*), the deity "like an enemy" who "stands" (254 ↔ Lam 2:4–5 `כְּאוֹיֵב נִצָּב`), the foxes in the ruins (269 ↔ Lam 5:18), the holy stones scattered (278 ↔ Lam 4:1), the hand on the treasures (238 ↔ Lam 1:10), the dead in the streets (214 ↔ Lam 2:21), no survivor (326 ↔ Lam 2:22), the dances turned (213, 215 ↔ Lam 5:15).
3. **Isaiah 51:17–21 in LU 214–223:** the sons lying "at the head of every street like an antelope in a net" (Isa 51:20 ↔ LU 219 the gazelle in the trap, 214 the streets) and "drunk, but not with wine" (Isa 51:21; 29:9 ↔ LU 223 "who had not drunk beer"). Jer 9:21 has the corpses "like sheaves behind the reaper" (↔ 215 *zar-re-eš* `כעמרים`).
4. **Zechariah 11 twice:** the temple valued at "thirty shekels" by the destroyers (243 ↔ Zech 11:12–13 `שְׁלֹשִׁים כָּסֶף … אֶדֶר הַיְקָר`; Exod 21:32 the price of a slave; *giĝ₄* = *šiqlu* = `שקל`) and the weapon falling on the ewes "as with a worthless herdsman" (305 ↔ Zech 11:17 `הוֹי רֹעִי הָאֱלִיל … חֶרֶב`; 11:4 `צֹאן הַהֲרֵגָה`).
5. **Deuteronomy 28's exile curses without the word "exile":** carried off by boat to slavery (283 ↔ Deut 28:68), serving foreign gods – the slave-girl carrying a foreign god's standard in a foreign city (284 ↔ Deut 28:64, Jer 16:13), the land "you did not know" (285 ↔ Deut 28:36), building for others (288–291 ↔ Deut 28:30), and **306–307 ↔ Deut 28:65 `לֹא תַרְגִּיעַ וְלֹא יִהְיֶה מָנוֹחַ` / Lam 1:3 `לֹא מָצְאָה מָנוֹחַ`**: "I went out of the city, I cannot rest (*ni₂ dub₂* = *pašāḫu* = `נרגע`); I went out of the house, I find no seat (*pad₃* = `מצא`)". The Sumerian has no exile: *e₃* "go out", *re₇* "go" (235 ↔ Lam 1:5 `הָלְכוּ שְׁבִי`, where Hebrew adds `שבי`), *de₆* "carry" (276–277 ↔ 2 Kgs 20:17 `וְנִשָּׂא … בָּבֶלָה`). ETCSL's "exiled" (306–307) and "carried off" (235) are the translators'.
6. **The goddess as a stranger:** "as if a stranger (*gir₅*) in a foreign (*kur₂*) city" (308 ↔ Exod 2:22 `גֵּר הָיִיתִי בְּאֶרֶץ נָכְרִיָּה`); "my city that is no more – I am not its lady" (286 ↔ Isa 47:5 `לֹא תוֹסִיפִי יִקְרְאוּ לָךְ גְּבֶרֶת מַמְלָכוֹת`); "I am not its man" (287 ↔ Hos 2:4 `וְאָנֹכִי לֹא אִישָׁהּ`); debt-bondage (309–310 ↔ Isa 50:1 `מִי מִנּוֹשַׁי`).
7. **The only question, unanswered:** "devastated – for what?" (*ta-a-aš*, 324–325) ↔ Jer 22:8–9, 16:10, 5:19, Deut 29:23: the biblical `עַל מֶה` is always answered by the people's sin; LU's is not. With 196 (tears do not decree fate ↔ 2 Kgs 20:5, where they do) and 321 (the building was "falsehood" ↔ Jer 7:4, where the trust is), the theology of LU is drawn sharply against the Bible's.
8. **Smaller one-to-one contacts:** Josh 8:28 `וַיְשִׂימֶהָ תֵּל` (245 *du₆ … ĝar*); Ps 74:5–6 axes "together" (242 *teš₂-bi*); Isa 5:6 / 7:25 the field not hoed with the `מַעְדֵּר` (272 *{ĝiš}al*); Joel 1:11 farmers–barley–field (271); Prov 23:5 wealth flying away (275); Ps 84:4 `צִפּוֹר … וּדְרוֹר` (282, divergence); Nah 2:8 `מְתֹפְפֹת עַל לִבְבֵהֶן` (300); Amos 5:2 `נָפְלָה לֹא תוֹסִיף קוּם` (320); Gen 8:9 the dove without a resting place (307, with 236–237).

**The ancient mind.**
1. **The storm is how a decision acts.** Enlil does not send an army; he "turns to the storm", drives away the good one, commands the evil one, hands it to its keeper Kingaluda and adds Gibil, the fire god, "as his helper" (172–179). Even the Elamites appear only as one more instrument (243). The divine world is an administration of forces, each with its office.
2. **The city's body:** walls, gates, festival boulevards, the skipping-rope ground (*ešemen*), canals, reservoirs, granaries and "women's estates", orchards with date syrup, fields of barley, herds with their herdsmen, the fisheries inspector, slave-girls of the temple household, mud-brick walls that melt back into mud when nobody maintains them (317), the debt-house (309–310). The lament lists what made a city a city by listing what is gone.
3. **Care of the dead:** covering the head, bandaging, the new cloth (218–222): what was *not* done is part of the horror.
4. **A goddess is defined by her city:** "my city that is no more – I am not its lady" (286); "I am a torn-down cow-pen, scattered cows" (304). Without a house she has nowhere to sit or stand (294), cannot rest (306), is a stranger (308), a debt-slave without a voice (310). She performs the human gestures of mourning – tearing her hair, beating her breast as a drum, lying in the ruins "like a fallen ox" (299–320).
5. **Contempt for the highlanders:** "men who do not know silver", "who do not know gems" (280–281), "thirty shekels" (243): the urban Sumerian view of the mountain peoples of Elam and Šimaški.
6. **The refrain of the possessions** (275–279 "My possessions!", 261–264, 282–283 "Alas, my city!") makes Ningal's lament a liturgical litany, as the *kirugu* structure suggests: a text for performance by alternating voices.

### Key findings (run 3)
**Bible.**
1. **The only sin of the poem (431).** In 442 lines the word *nam-tag* "sin" (= *arnu* = `עוון`) occurs once, in the eleventh *kirugu*, as "the sin of that man" whom his personal god introduces to Nanna – the formula of personal prayer, not an explanation of the ruin. The [absent] of runs 1–2 (no sin of the city) therefore stands to the end, and the [divergence] from Lamentations (1:5, 1:8, 3:42, 5:7, 5:16) is complete: the Sumerian poem ends by asking forgiveness for an individual, never for the city. And the order "forgive – restore – praise" (431 → 425/437) is the order of Solomon's prayer (1 Kgs 8:30, 34 `וְסָלַחְתָּ … וַהֲשֵׁבֹתָם`).
2. **Isa 55:10 in LU 411.** "That storm – like rain that rains from heaven – may it not return (*gur* = *târu* = `שוב`) to its seat": the image and the verb of `כַּאֲשֶׁר יֵרֵד הַגֶּשֶׁם … מִן הַשָּׁמַיִם וְשָׁמָּה לֹא יָשׁוּב`. With LU 149 (An's word "not turned back" ↔ Isa 55:11, run 1) the two halves of Isa 55:10–11 are in one poem – the irrevocable word and the rain that does not go back up – but reversed: Isaiah's word does not return empty; LU prays that the storm, which is Enlil's word (408), not return at all. Gen 9:11, 15 ("never again a flood") is the nearest biblical "never again" – a covenant, where LU has only a wish.
3. **Deut 33:9 in LU 402–405.** "The storm that does not know mother, father, wife, child, sister, brother, neighbour, friend" = Levi "who said of his father and his mother 'I have not seen him', his brothers he did not recognize, his sons he did not know" (`לֹא יָדָע`, *zu* = `ידע`): the same list and the same verb, with opposite value (Levi's zeal, the storm's ruthlessness; Exod 32:27). With 400 "the storm that has no shame in its eyes" (*teš₂* = *bāštu* ~ `בֹּשֶׁת`) ↔ Deut 28:50 `גּוֹי עַז פָּנִים אֲשֶׁר לֹא יִשָּׂא פָנִים לְזָקֵן`.
4. **Deut 32:14 in LU 361–364:** cattle → ghee (*i₃*, Akk. *šamnu* ~ `שמן`), sheep → milk: `חֶמְאַת בָּקָר וַחֲלֵב צֹאן`, same pairing and order – the daily dairy offering of the moon god.
5. **Lamentations, image by image (331–377):** "how?" (331; Lam 1:1, `איכה` kept in the apparatus), the strange city and strange tears (337, 342; Lam 5:2, Ps 137:1–4), "she dwelt among the lands" (343; Lam 1:3), the priests and festivals (348–357; Lam 1:4, 2:6), the changed appearance (358; Lam 4:8 `חָשַׁךְ מִשְּׁחוֹר תָּאֳרָם`), song turned to weeping (359–360; Lam 5:15, Amos 8:10, Job 30:31 – the instruments themselves), thorns and weeds (367–368; Isa 34:13, Hos 9:6), the city as a child crying to its mother in the street (369–370; Lam 2:12), the outstretched hands (371; Lam 1:17, 2:19), "like an enemy … standing" (374; Lam 2:4–5, Ps 10:1).
6. **"Where are you?" (372)** – *me-a* is a real "where", so Gen 3:9 `אַיֶּכָּה` is an [equivalent] of the question (reversed: the house asks the goddess).
7. **"Return!" without a verb (378–380).** ETCSL and Kramer add "return"; the Sumerian has only "like a bull to your pen, like a sheep to your fold … my lady, to your house!". Jer 31:20 `שׁוּבִי בְּתוּלַת יִשְׂרָאֵל שֻׁבִי אֶל עָרַיִךְ אֵלֶּה` – in the chapter of Rachel weeping (Jer 31:14 ↔ LU 66, run 1) – is the closest biblical call; Lam 5:21 `חַדֵּשׁ יָמֵינוּ כְּקֶדֶם` ↔ 379 "the pen of former days". `שוב` stays out of these lines.
8. **"Enough!" (381):** *muš₃-am₃* = *aḫulap* = `רַב!` ↔ 2 Sam 24:16 `רַב עַתָּה הֶרֶף יָדֶךָ` (the destroyer stopped) and Deut 3:26 `רַב לָךְ` (same words, a refusal).
9. **The curse of the storm (409–416)** has no biblical counterpart [absent]: the Bible never curses the instrument of its own God's judgement. Its images do have counterparts: the door shut against the wrath (414 ↔ Isa 26:20, Gen 7:16, reversed: here the wrath is shut in), "not reckoned" (415 ↔ Ps 32:2), the record removed from the god's archive (416 ↔ Exod 32:32, Ps 69:29, Isa 43:25).
10. **"Until the end of days" (418):** *ud-da eĝer-bi* "the back of the days" ~ Akk. *aḫrât ūmī* ~ `אחרית הימים` (Gen 49:1; Isa 2:2).
11. **The searching gaze (434)** ↔ Jer 17:10, Ps 7:10, 1 Sam 16:7 – but the verb is *igi bar* "look", not *barû*, so `בחן` stays in the apparatus; "pure before you" (435–436) ↔ Lev 16:30 `לִפְנֵי יְהוָה תִּטְהָרוּ`, Ps 51:12 `לֵב טָהוֹר`; praise in the rebuilt city (437) ↔ Ps 147:2, 12.

**The ancient mind.**
1. **A goddess without a house cannot "live" or "grow"** (331–349): the refrain asks it literally, and the canals and roads answer with weeds that do "grow" (*mu₂*, 367–368).
2. **The temple staff by office** (348–357): *gudug* (anointed priest, entering before the god), *en* (the high priestess of Nanna living in the *ĝipar*, chosen by omen; Enḫeduana), the purification man, *išib* (incantation/purification priest), *lumaḫ* (linen-clad priest of the *giguna*), *aua*; drums *šem* and *ala*, the *tigi*; the people's bath before the feast. The cult was a daily household service; its silence is the city's death.
3. **The dairy of the moon god** (361–364): ghee and milk carriers bringing produce from the pens to the god's table; Nanna as a herdsman god.
4. **City and goddess as child and mother** (369–380), the bricks that speak "like a human" (372: the temple as a living body).
5. **The storm as a being with an office and a file** (409–416): it has a seat in Enlil's house, a door can be barred on it, it is "reckoned" and has an account (*niĝ₂-kas₇*) hung on a peg – a bureaucracy's way of annulling a decree, like cancelling a debt-document. Ur III's administration, with its hundreds of thousands of account tablets, is the background of the image.
6. **The personal god** (*diĝir lu₂-ulu₃*, 427, 432A) introduces his client with a gift, as in the "presentation scenes" of Ur III seals; the worshipper seizes the god's feet (421), puts his hand to his nose (423), stands (433).
7. **The moon and the star** (426): the restored city "passes before" Nanna like a star before the moon – the astral form of the city's god.
8. **The liturgical close:** *kirugu* 9–10 curse the storm, 11 is a generic prayer ("the man", "the man of prayer") in the main dialect, which any worshipper could say; the lament ends not with mourning but with "praise" (*me-teš₂*, 437).

### Proposals for the corpus renderings (run 3)
1. **Fixed lines / formulas (LU, and for Agade where the same Sumerian occurs):** *šag₄-zu a-gin₇ du₃-mu-un* = `איך ליבך …?`; *za-e a-gin₇ i₃-til₃-le-en* = `את, איך את חיה?`; *i₃-ne-eš₂-gin₇ i₃-e-am₃-mu₂* = `כיצד את צומחת?`; *en₃-tukum-še₃ mu₂* = `עַד אָנָה יצמח?`; *X ki-bi ḫa-ra-ab-gi₄-gi₄* = `את X למקומה ישיב לך` (Agade: the restoration/curse of a city's place); *nam-nin-bi ak-a* = `את גבירותה עשי!`; *uru₂ ki-bi gi₄-a* = `העיר ששבה למקומה`; *ud X nu-zu re* = `הסערה שאינה יודעת X – ההיא!`; *ud … re* = `הסערה … – ההיא!`; *ud ḫul gig dug₄-ga {d}en-lil₂-la₂-ta* = `הסערה הרעה והמרה שנאמרה מאת אנליל`.

3. **The storm (for Agade):** *ud gal an-na* = `הסערה הגדולה של השמים`; *gu₃ dub₂-dub₂* = `המרעידה ומרעידה קול`; *ud gig* = `הסערה המרה`; *zal* = `חלף`; *ra* = `מחץ`; *teš₂-a* = `יחד`; *ki ĝar* = `מקום שָׂם` (= settle, found); *ki-tuš* = `מושב`; *{ĝiš}ig … keše₂* = `הדלת … תיקשר`; *abula ĝi₆ u₃-na-gin₇* = `כשער העיר בלילה`; *šudum* = `חשבון`; *niĝ₂-kas₇* = `רשומה`; *gag* = `מסמר`; *teš₂* "shame" = `בושה`; *u₂-gu de₂* = `העלים`.
