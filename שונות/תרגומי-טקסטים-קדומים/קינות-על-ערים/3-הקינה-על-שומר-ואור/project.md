# Project 3: the Lament for Sumer and Urim (ETCSL 2.2.3)

## Scope
The whole composition: **524 entries** (ETCSL 1–519 with the pluses 80A, 80B, 219A, 361A, 362A, 386A, 407A–C, 412A, 425A, 477A; ETCSL has no lines 120–121, 283, 285–288). Five *kirugu* sections with their *ĝišgiĝal* rubrics (112–114, 284, 357–359, 478–482, 519). Round 6 run 4 (2026-11-07), beside project 1, the Lament for Ur.

The poem: the gods' decree to end the Ur III state ("to overturn the appointed times", 1–57), the storm and the fall of the cities of Sumer one by one (Nibru, Kiš, Uruk, Eridug … Urim), Ibbi-Suen led to Elam, the plea of Nanna-Suen to his father Enlil and Enlil's answer that kingship is given for a term (*bala*), never for ever (364–370), the god's restoration and the closing prayer for the land.

#### Key findings (LSU1)
- **The ancient mind.** The poem opens not with a sin but with a decree: some thirty purpose clauses (1–54) – "to overturn the days, to lose the forms", to wreck pen, field, river, marriage, cradle and oracle – hang on four gods named only in 55, and 56–57 seal it: "Who will overturn it by hand? … Who will set a head against it?" The ruin of Ur III is told as the end of a *bala*, a turn of office (`תְּקוּפָה`), decided in the divine assembly. Then the decree is executed in its own words: what 1–54 announced (silencing the lullaby and the churn, salting rivers, the father not saying "my wife", the offerings of Ur "changed", the people of Nanna "split into fractions") returns in 58–111 and later (95–96, 101–102, 324, 337, 346) as fact.
- **The storm and the city's gods.** *ud* is "day" and "storm" (1–2, 163, 173: Bau "reached her day"); the Gutians and Elamites are "brought down from the mountain" by Enlil (75, 166, 261), the flood of Enlil (76); heaven and earth shake, sun and moon stop (81–84). The second *kirugu* walks through Sumer city by city with three formulas – the god "took a strange road from his beloved dwelling", the goddess "weeps bitter tears", "Alas the destroyed city, my destroyed house!" – and the five-fold *ĝipar* refrain (the *en*-priestess carried to enemy land). Gods are not omnipotent here: Bau "like a human" reaches her day (174), Ninazu sets his weapon in a corner (206), the gatekeepers of Eridu complain that they served day and night for nothing (225–242).
- **The family and the economy.** The decree and its fulfilment are drawn from household and state economy: ration (*šukur₂*), granary, churn, dung, brewery – the centralized Ur III economy seen in its collapse (304: the king grabs a worker's ration).
- **The Bible.** The closest contacts are with Lamentations (the captured king 34, the queen made a slave 273 ↔ Lam 1:1, the empty roads 39 ↔ 1:4, the enemy's gaze 151 ↔ 1:10, the fallen crown 100 ↔ 5:16 but without "for we have sinned") and with Daniel's theology of times and kingdoms (1, 17 ↔ Dan 2:21: God `מְהַשְׁנֵא עִדָּנַיָּא` and `מְהַעְדֵּה מַלְכִין`). **[absent]: sin.** The only *nam-tag* (244) is in a broken line without a clear subject; the "loyal" (*zid*) suffer with the "liars" (110–111). ETCSL's imported echoes corrected: "exile" (375, *gir₅* "stranger"), "glory" (426, *u₆* "wonder"), "looked favourably" (23), "altered the course" (25, *aš₂ bal* "curse").

#### Open decisions (LSU1) – with my rulings
1. **The tool's licence for *nam … kud*.** `lament_tools.py` licenses `ארר` only with *nam … ku₅* or *na-aĝ₂ … kud*; LSU writes *nam ba-an-kud-da-a-ba* (26) and *nam ḫa-ba-da-an-kud* (239) with the sign *kud* (= *ku₅*; ORACC lemma kud). Ruling: keep `אָרַר` (corpus §3: `ארר` = *nam … ku₅*, which is this verb); **the coordinator should widen the RESERVED licence and the FORMULAS regex to `nam\b.*kud`** – then `verify LSU --strict` is clean. (The other corpora already render *nam ba-an-kud* `ארר`: EA 119.)
2. ***bal* = `תְּקוּפָה`** throughout (4, 28, 366, 368, 369, 461–463, 515), *bal nam-lugal-la* `תקופת מלוכה`, *bal da-ri₂* `תקופת נצח`. LU 113 has `תקופת שלטון`: harmonize LU 113 to `תקופה …` (proposal 1 below).
3. **The decree chain**: positive *-ed-e* `לְ` + infinitive, negative `שֶׁלֹּא` + future, a subject before a dash where the infinitive has one (`התקופה הנאה – לשוב ולשוב אל ביתה`). Ruling: kept for 1–54 and wherever *-de₃* recurs (109–111, 215, 263, 498, 502).
4. ***šu la₂*** (18, 152 ×5, 310, 330, 404, 418) = `יד נמתחה` (ETCSL "defiled"), kept apart from *šu pe-el-la₂ dug₄* `יד טמאה נשלחה` (29, 99) and *šu ḫul dug₄* `יד רעה נשלחה` (115), *šu lil₂-la₂ dug₄* `יד המשב נשלחה` (143, 149).
5. ***kaskal* = `מסילה`** (26, 39, 472, 495) because LU's *ḫar-ra-an* = `דרך` and both occur together (26, 39); the kings corpus has *kaskal* `דרך` – a split for the lead.
6. ***ĝiri₃ kur₂ dab₅*** = `דרך נכריה תפס` (11 lines): *dab₅* `תפס` (LU 307), *kur₂* `נכרי` (gated).
7. ***nam-en*** = `אַדְנוּת` (53, 152…, 452, 458); the priest *en* `הָאֵנָה`.

#### Key findings (LSU2)
- **The ancient mind: Nanna's plea and Enlil's answer.** The god of Ur argues with his father as a son with interests: "the first-fruits boat no longer brings first-fruits to you, your offerings no longer reach Nibru" (343–344) – the destruction hurts the destroyer. His requests reverse the decree word for word (*daĝal* 355 ↔ 8; *ḫa-lam* 356 ↔ 27). Enlil answers twice. First (364–370): the assembly's verdict cannot be returned; "to Ur kingship was indeed given – a term of eternity was not given"; "since the Land was founded, who ever saw a term of kingship that stayed at the head?" Second (460–474), after Nanna kneels again: "the city built for you … received your term; to destroy a city and topple its wall – that too is a term" (462), and then the promise: Ur shall be rebuilt, the people bow, Ezina sit at its root, Utu rejoice in its branches. The decree and the promise are spoken in the same formula ("a word spoken by An and Enlil it is", 57 ↔ 474). For an Old Babylonian hearer this was the King List's philosophy and the Isin dynasty's charter: kingship is a *me* that moves; Isin is the next turn, not a usurper; and Ur's god may come home (475–477).
- **The famine and the silenced temple** (293–339, 389–402) are told through the Ur III economy: rations, granaries, brewery, the butcher's knife lying hungry (312), millstones idle (317), the first-fruits barge, foreigners churning milk they do not know (335–336); then the siege: "inside Ur death, outside it death" (399).
- **The final prayer** (493–511) turns the decree chain inside out: "the forms", rivers, rain, speckled barley, roads, marshes, reed-beds, orchards – "may An not change it!" Even the gods can be asked not to change their minds; the opening "to overturn the days" is answered by a plea for stability. The poem ends not in triumph but with "Alas your city, alas your house, alas your people" (518).
- **The Bible.** The strongest structural parallel is with the biblical theology of times and kingdoms: Ur receives kingship but not "a term of eternity" (366) – a [divergence] from the Davidic `עַד עוֹלָם` (2 Sam 7:16, Ps 89), and an [equivalent] of Jer 27:7 (`עַד בֹּא עֵת אַרְצוֹ`) and Daniel (2:21; 4:29). Lamentations: famine and sword (399 ↔ Lam 1:20), the faces of the starved (391 ↔ 4:8; 5:10), the king's ration (304 ↔ 4:5), the gate bars (404 ↔ 2:9), clay pots (406 ↔ 4:2), the enemy's gaze (442 ↔ 1:10); Ps 74 (axes, carvings 382, 420); the punishment turned on the instrument (486–491 ↔ Jer 49:36; 25:12, 25); the returning god and his straightened highways (472, 475 ↔ Isa 40:3; Ezek 43:4). **[absent]: sin, repentance and exile as concepts** – the poem's cause is time, not guilt.

### Draft for the clean translation's introduction (Hebrew)
"הקינה על שׁוּמֶר ואוּר" היא אחת מחמש הקינות השומריות על ערים, שנכתבו במאה ה־20 לפנה"ס, אחרי שעֵילָם ושִׁימַשְׁכִּי כבשו את אוּר (בסביבות 2004 לפנה"ס) והובילו את המלך האחרון של שושלת אור השלישית, אִיבִּי־סוּאֵן, אל אַנְשָׁן. בניגוד לקינה על אור, שהיא בכי של אלה על ביתה, זהו סיפור על גזרה: השיר נפתח בשרשרת ארוכה של שמות פועל – "להפוך את הימים, לאבד את הצורות … להרוס ולהרוס עיר, להרוס ולהרוס בית" – ורק אחריה מתגלים הגוזרים: אַן, אנליל, אנקי ונין־חורסגה.
אחר כך באה הסערה: הגוטים והעילמים "מורדים מן ההר", השמש והירח עומדים, וערי שומר נופלות אחת אחת, בשלוש נוסחאות חוזרות – האל "ממושבו האהוב דרך נכריה תפס", האלה "דמעות מרות היא בוכה", "הוי העיר ההרוסה, ביתי ההרוס!". באור עצמה – רעב: "בתוכה מוות, מחוצה לה מוות".
לב החיבור הוא הדו־שיח בין נַנָּה, אל הירח ואדון אור, לבין אביו אנליל. ננה מתחנן, ואנליל עונה: "לאור אכן ניתנה מלוכה – תקופת נצח לא ניתנה לה". המלה השומרית *bala*, `תְּקוּפָה`, היא "תור" במשרה: המלוכה עוברת מעיר לעיר, וגם החורבן הוא חלק מן התקופה. ובכל זאת אנליל מבטיח שאור תיבנה, וננה שב אל עירו "בראש נשוא".
השיר נחתם בתפילה החוזרת "אַן אַל יְשַׁנֶּה!" על כל מה שעושה ארץ לארץ: הנהרות, הגשם, השעורה, הדרכים, הביצות והפרדסים – ובקריאה האחרונה: "ננה – הוי עירך! הוי ביתך! הוי אנשיך!". אין בשיר חטא של העיר: הסיבה היא הזמן.
התרגום נעשה מן החיבור המשוחזר של ETCSL (2.2.3), עם הניתוח של ORACC, שורה בשורה; סוגריים מרובעים מסמנים השלמה של טקסט שבור, `…` מלה שאינה מובנת, וכוכביות – תרגום לא ודאי. המלה העברית `תקופה` מתרגמת תמיד את *bala*, ו`סערה` / `יום` את *ud*, שהיא בשומרית מלה אחת.
