### Deviations from renderings §10 and the guidelines (decided at the first occurrence, with the reason)
1. ***eṣēru* = `יָצַר`, written `יָצְרוּ` (vocalized)**, not the `צָר` of guidelines §6.7: `את העיר צרו` reads "they
   besieged the city" (צוּר), the very opposite. Same root (the guidelines' "*eṣēru* = יצר"), and it shows the
   cognate of Isa 37:26 / 46:11 / 22:11 (`יָצַר` "planned") and Gen 2:7.
2. ***šibirru* = `מַקֵּל־רוֹעִים`** (§10.4 had `מַקֵּל`? for ET1 to decide): the Sumerian *šibir* already has
   `מקל־רועים` (Enki and the World Order 436), and a Sumerian word takes the Hebrew of its Akkadian equivalent
   (renderings §0.3); the compound also keeps eBL's "shepherd's staff". `שֵׁבֶט` stays free for *šabbiṭu* (Gilgameš XII).
3. ***malāku*: G = `יָעַץ`, Gt = `נִמְלַךְ`**, as in Gilgameš (OB III *imallikū* `יועצים`; VII 1 *imtallikū* `נמלכו`); so
   OBM.2 `יעצו` and I.9 `נמלכו` show the stems' difference. *mitluku* = `נִמְלָךְ` (Gilgameš II 30).
4. ***nadû* in *uššī nadû* = `הניח`** (Gilgameš I 21), not the gated `הִשְׁלִיךְ`.

### Key findings
**The Bible.**
1. **The opening is not a "when not yet" opening.** OBM 7–9 are statives ("not bound", "not set", "not built");
   Foster's "Then no headdress had been …", "Nor yet" import the `טֶרֶם` of Gen 2:5. What *is* shared is the structure:
   a world that lacks one office ("no man to work the ground" / "no king"), filled from above by a god who "forms"
   (*eṣēru* = `יָצַר`, I.1; Gen 2:7) and "sets" (*šakānu* = `שָׂם`, OBM.6; Gen 2:8).
2. **Gen 11 inverted.** In Etana the gods plan the city (*eṣēru* = `יָצַר`, the "planning" of Isa 37:26, 46:11), lay
   its foundations (*uššu*, the source of Aramaic `אֻשַּׁיָּא`, Ezra 5:16 – the same idiom) and set its bricks (*libittu*
   = `לְבֵנָה`, Gen 11:3), and then kingship "comes down" (*warādu* = `יָרַד`). In Gen 11 humans plan and build, and God
   "comes down" (11:5) to stop them. Gen 10:8–10 makes the first post-flood kingdom a human achievement (`הֵחֵל`,
   `גִּבֹּר`), where the Sumerian King List and Etana have kingship lowered from heaven to Kiš (Cush = Kiš only as a
   proposal).
3. **The vocabulary of kingship is shared**: "set a king" (*šarram šakānu* = `שׂוּם מֶלֶךְ`, Deut 17:15, 1 Sam 8:5); the
   kingless "in those days" (SB I.13; Judg 17:6, 21:25); the insignia pair `מִצְנֶפֶת` / `עֲטָרָה` (Ezek 21:31); the
   shepherd's staff (Mic 7:14, Ps 23:4); "a man as their shepherd" (I.6; Num 27:16–17; Ezek 34:23 with the same
   feminine plural suffix); the deity who "seeks" a shepherd-king (I.19–22; 1 Sam 13:14, 16:11, Ps 78:70–71); the god
   who "examines" from his throne in heaven (I.23; Ps 11:4); "the counsel counselled concerning the land" (OBM.2; Isa
   14:26).
4. **A builder without a son.** I.7 calls Etana the people's "builder" (*itinnu*, a play on his name); Gen 4:17's first
   city-builder names the city after his son. Etana's story will turn on the son he does not have (II.140–142 "establish
   a name for me"); the wife's dream (I.a+2–a+7) mentions "his ghost", whose care needs a son (2 Sam 18:18 Absalom).
5. **False echoes avoided** (rank-3 English): "holy day" (*isinnu* = `חַג`), "Then … had not / Nor yet" (statives),
   "creators" (*bānû* "builders", not `בָּרָא`), "right counsel", "four world regions" (I.10: an old restoration),
   "came down from heaven to seek" (I.19, 21: a restoration resting on the abandoned *bēltum*), "with ramparts" (I.18),
   "instead of" (*kīma*, I.a+4–a+5). The reading *[šar]rūtum* (OBM.14, collation) removes Ištar's descent from heaven.

**The ancient mind.**
1. **The city before the king.** The gods plan the city, lay its foundations and bricks (the Igigi, the labouring gods
   of Atra-ḫasīs) and surround it (I.18); the world already has edges, form, people and a festival. Only kingship is
   missing – and it is not a human invention but an object kept "before Anu in heaven" (OBM.11–12): sceptre, tiara,
   turban, shepherd's staff.
2. **Kingship cannot be seized.** "Seven gates are bolted against the aggressive one" (OBM.10): no strongman can take
   it; it must be lowered (OBM.14, the Sumerian King List's idiom). The SB rereads the number as the Seven gods (the
   Sebetti) who bar the gates against "the multitude" and "the settlements" (I.16–17) – the whole of humankind shut out.
3. **The king's two faces**: shepherd and builder (I.6–7) – the leader of the human flock and the builder of the city
   and its temples, the two roles of every Mesopotamian royal inscription; the name Etana explained by *itinnu*.
4. **The goddess chooses**: Ištar/Innina "seeks" a shepherd and a king, as she "loved" Sargon; Enlil inspects the dais
   of heaven – a coronation prepared above before a king is found below. The festival (OBM.5) is "decreed" with the same
   verb as fate: joy is part of humanity's lot.
5. **A wife's dream**: dreams are "shown" by a god (*barû* Š), and women dream and interpret (Ninsun, the women of the
   Mari letters); the dream concerns Etana, "their death?" and "his ghost" – the dead who need a son to pour water for
   them (Gilgameš XII 152).
6. **The scribes**: the incipit *āla īṣirū* is the series' title in the Nineveh colophons (Langdon 1931 read "the city
   they hated"); the SB copyist wrote *kibrāti* by mistake in I.15, carried over from I.10 – writing from memory.

### Open decisions (numbered; the minor ones decided)
1. ***eṣēru* = `יָצְרוּ`** instead of guidelines §6.7's `צָר` (decided; reason above). The coordinator may add to §6.7:
   "`צָר` for drawing images; the full `יָצַר` where `צר` could read צוּר 'besiege'".
2. ***ḫaṭṭu* across corpora (lead):** `שַׁרְבִיט` here (renderings §10.4; Gilgameš VIII 128, 153; Sum. *ĝidru* in the
   laments and Gudea), but Enūma eliš IV 29 and LH (prologue 161, epilogue 3187′, 3356′, 3392′) give *ḫaṭṭu* / GIDRU
   `שֵׁבֶט` – while Gilgameš XII 19, 39 give `שֵׁבֶט` to its cognate *šabbiṭu*. Proposal: *ḫaṭṭu* = `שַׁרְבִיט` everywhere,
   `שֵׁבֶט` only for *šabbiṭu*.
3. ***parakku* (lead):** `כֵּס` (Etana II, here; EA *barag*) vs Enūma eliš `דוכן` (V 115; VI 51, 53, 58, 68, 118). **Settled 2026-10-23** (`../../harmonization--2026-10-23.md` row 5): `דּוּכָן`.
4. ***meānu* `עֲטָרָה`** (§10.4) vs Enki and the World Order 264, Sum. *aga* `עטרת` (= Akk. *agû*, `כֶּתֶר` in Gilgameš):
   EWO's *aga* should probably be `כֶּתֶר` (lead). *kubšu* `מִצְנֶפֶת` = Sum. *saĝšu* `מצנפת` (EA 578) – consistent; but
   Enūma eliš VII 8 has `*מצנפתו*` for *šukūssu* (italic) – a minor split.
5. ***ṣapāru* `*משובץ*`** collides with Gilgameš VIII 170, 185 *raʾzu* `*משובץ*` (both uncertain, both italic). Kept
   (decided): it is the natural Hebrew for a stone set in metal, and both renderings are marked uncertain; alternatives
   tried and rejected: `קבוע` (EA *us₂*; reads "permanent"), `מוטבע` (LH/EST *ṭebû* = `טבע`), `משוקע` (*šabāḫu*).
6. ***barû* Š and *kullumu* both `הֶרְאָה`** (renderings §10.3 sets both; flood: XI 197 `הראיתי חלום`; Gilgameš OB
   `הַרְאֵנִי`): a pre-set double mapping. Left (natural Hebrew for both); ET4 should note it where both occur (coordinator).
7. ***ḫiāṭu* `בָּחַן`** (Gilgameš I 19, 41) vs Enūma eliš *barû* `בוחן` (IV 65, 135; VII 35) and the wisdom corpus
   *bārû* `הַבּוֹחֵן`: an existing collision; I followed Gilgameš (lead).
8. ***ina ūmīšū-ma* `ביום ההוא`** (Gilgameš III 95, VIII 41) vs `בעת ההיא` (Enūma eliš IV 63, Adapa, the Founding of
   Eridu): I followed Gilgameš (lead may harmonize).
9. **OBM.7 *ina šiātim* = `בָּהּ`** (decided): literal "in it/her", leaving open eBL's "at that (time)" and Streck's "in it
   (the land) = here".
10. **OBM.13 *mitlukum* = `נִמְלָךְ`** (decided; Gilgameš II 30): fits both the adjective "circumspect" and the
    infinitive "deliberation"; `הִמָּלְכוּת` was rejected because unvocalized it reads `המלכות` "the kingship" (OBM.14).
11. **I.24 `מבקשת ומבקשת`** (decided): feminine, continuing Ištar's verb (I.20, 22); the subject may be Enlil (I.23).
12. **The formula *X ana šâšū-ma ana Y izzakaršu* = `X אליו, אל Y, דיבר:`** (I.a+2, decided): for ET4 (III b+15,
    b+26, b+32, b+36 → `הנשר אליו, אל אֵתָנָה, דיבר:`). A FORMULAS regex could be added (coordinator).
13. **I.4 brackets** (decided): `האל[ים?` per the ATF (*DINGIR#-[MEŠ?*), although eBL's N brackets the whole word.
14. **OBM.a+1–a+13 and I.a+2–a+7**: whether OB Morgan col. ii tells Etana's marriage, and whether it is the same scene

### Deviations from renderings §10 (decided at the first occurrence, with the reason)
1. ***bâru* = `לָכַד`** (not §10.3's `צָד`): one Hebrew per source word across corpora – the Gilgameš corpus has *bâru*
   = `לָכַד` (OB Ishchali 15' `צפור לכוד`); `צָד` is the cognate of *ṣâdu* / *ṣayyādu* (Gilgameš `צייד`); and the sense
   is "catch, trap (with a net)" (eBL). It also makes the idiom of the net exact: `רשת שַׁמַשׁ תלכדך` (II.47) ↔
   `וְרִשְׁתּוֹ אֲשֶׁר-טָמַן תִּלְכְּדוֹ` (תהלים לה:ח). No FORMULAS regex involves it; only the prose of §10.3 changes
   (`… – הנחש לוכד,`; `רשת שַׁמַשׁ תלכדך`). Note: Gilgameš itself also has *ḫābilu* `לוכד` (I 111, VII 94) – a
   Gilgameš-internal near-collision (participle of `לכד` for *ḫābilu*, finite verbs for *bâru*).
2. ***nērebu* = `מָבוֹא`** (not §10.3's `מַעֲבָר`, which is Gilgameš's *nēberu*, X 79, XI 248); *nērebtu* = `מְבוֹאָה`
   (OBS 2, MA b+3; `מְבוֹאֹת יָם`, יחזקאל כז:ג).
3. ***qerbetu* = `נָוֶה`** (Gilgameš VIII 13 *qerbētu* `הנאות`); OBS a+20 `רשתך – הַנָּוֶה הרחב`.
4. **The animals** (§10.4 left them to ET2): *turāḫu* = `יָעֵל` (= Sum. *dara₂* / *taraḫ*, `יעל` in EA 182 and Agade 23),
   so §10.4's `יָעֵל?` for *armu* moves: *armu* (eBL "mountain goat"; ≠ *arwiu* / *armû* "male gazelle", `צבי` in the
   love songs) = `אַקּוֹ` (pl. `אַקּוֹאִים`); *ditānu* = `*תְּאוֹ*` (pl. `*תְּאוֹאִים*`; = Sum. *alim*, `תְּאוֹ` in Gudea, EWO,
   Agade; §10.4's `תְּאוֹ?` for *turāḫu* would have collided); *sappāru* = `*זֶמֶר*` (pl. `*זְמָרִים*`, always vocalized);
   *mindinu* = `*בַּרְדְּלָס*` (Gilgameš); *nimru* = `נָמֵר`; *rīmu* `רְאֵם`; *serrēmu* `חמור בר` (Gilgameš string,
   without maqaf; §10.4 has `חֲמוֹר־בָּר`); *ṣabītu* `צְבִיָּה`.
5. ***mullû ina qāti***: II.50's restored *[umalli]* is formally D preterite (present would be *umalla*); kept as §10.3's
   present `ימסרהו.`, with a note.

### Key findings
**The Bible.**
1. **The oath of friendship is told as a treaty, and it meets Genesis's oath scenes word for word**: *alka nīpuša
   rūʾūta … anāku u kâši* (II.8–9) = Laban's `לְכָה נִכְרְתָה בְרִית אֲנִי וָאָתָּה` (Gen 31:44); *māmīta itmû
   kilallān* (II.16, 23) = `כִּי שָׁם נִשְׁבְּעוּ שְׁנֵיהֶם` (Gen 21:31); the reciprocal oath (OBS.4) = `וַיִּשָּׁבְעוּ
   אִישׁ לְאָחִיו` (Gen 26:31); the conditional *lemnētā-ma* "if you are evil" (II.12) = `אִם תַּעֲשֵׂה עִמָּנוּ
   רָעָה` (Gen 26:29); "Šamaš's boundary" (II.17, 19, 49) = Laban's heap that neither may cross (Gen 31:52) and the
   cursed mover of the friend's boundary (Deut 19:14, 27:17). The oath ends in curses, like Deut 28.
2. **Isa 14:13 is the eagle's plan**: "I will go up and dwell in heaven" (II.43; OBS.19–20) has the same two verbs, in
   the same order and person, both cognates, and in both the thought is "in the heart" (II.37; OBS.14). Obad 1:4 / Jer
   49:16 add the eagle's nest among the stars and the god who brings it down. Gen 11:4 is a [divergence] (pride and
   ascent, but no tower); **the "tower" of II.2 (*dimtu*) is an uncertain sign in a broken line beside a shrine of
   Adad, not a Gen 11 parallel** (rendered `צְרִיחַ`, `מִגְדָּל` reserved).
3. **The net of the divine judge**: choosing *bâru* = `לָכַד` (Gilgameš) makes II.47 `רשת שַׁמַשׁ תלכדך` the exact idiom
   of Ps 35:8 (net + `לכד`; Ps 9:16, Amos 3:5). Ezekiel 17 is the closest structural parallel to the whole fable: an
   eagle, a `צַפְצָפָה` (17:5), a broken oath (17:13–19) and the god's net (17:20; 12:13). Hos 7:12 (the net that brings
   the birds down from the sky), Ps 139:8 and Amos 9:2–3 (no escape in heaven or earth – and the god commands the
   serpent to bite) match "your net is the wide earth, your trap the distant heaven" (II.69–70) and the sequel (ET3).
4. **The eagle's crime is eating**: the serpent's condition calls breaking the friendship "eating a taboo" (II.13,
   *asakka akālu*) – the idiom of `לֹא תֹאכַל כָּל תּוֹעֵבָה` (Deut 14:3) – and the eagle breaks it by literally
   eating. The bird's-nest law (Deut 22:6–7) has the same verb for the brooding mother (*rabiṣ* / `רֹבֶצֶת`, II.5); the
   eagle does what the law forbids in spirit.
5. **The serpent is the righteous one**: against Gen 3:1 (the cunning serpent among `חַיַּת הַשָּׂדֶה`), here the serpent
   is faithful, generous ("I gave myself to the eagle"), God-fearing ("I honoured your life" = the oath, cf. Ps 15:4),
   and its lament has the structure of a biblical individual lament (Ps 25:2, Jer 18:23, Jer 12:1, Job 16:20, Ps
   41:10). The eagle is the one who "harbours evil against his friend" (Prov 3:29, Zech 8:17).
6. **False echoes avoided** (rank-3 English): Foster's "tower" (II.2), "netherworld" (II.15, MA.c+6, c+9), "curse" for
   *māmītu* (II.22), "desires" (MA.b+13), "No doubt" for *minde* "perhaps" (MA.c+5), "king of the gods" for *šar
   ša[mê]* (MA.c+28), "you" for *nīška* (OBS.a+13), and the restoration "while his young are safe" (II.66), which
   eBL's collation excludes. *mullû ina qāti* is never `מִלֵּא יָד`.

**The ancient mind.**
1. **Friendship as a sworn treaty under the sun-god**: two animals swear "before Šamaš the warrior" and by the earth;
   the curses are those of state treaties – lost paths, closed passes, a roving weapon, Šamaš's trap, and (MA only)
   being "singled out for the slaughterer" and a *gallû* demon riding on one's back. The god who sees everything is the
   guarantor of every oath, and the broken oath itself becomes the trap (*gišparru māmīt šamaš*).
2. **The three versions show a growing cosmos**: OB's net is "the wide pasture" around the settlement (OBS.a+20), SB's
   "the wide earth" and "the distant heaven" (II.69–70): the shepherds' world became the universe. SB also makes the
   hunt reciprocal (eagle and serpent alternate, II.26–33), where OB and MA have the serpent alone hunting – the
   betrayal of a fully mutual friendship.
3. **Intimacy and its betrayal**: MA alone describes the friendship from inside – "they befriended each other, became
   (as) twins, partners, unburdened the troubles of their livers to each other" (MA.b+11–13). The eagle's plan comes
   when his young have grown and left the nest (OBS.15–17).
4. **The wise child and the deaf father**: the littlest fledgling bears Atra-ḫasīs's name ("exceedingly wise"), quotes
   the oath word for word (II.47–50), and is not heard (II.51) – the flood hero's role in a family drama.
5. **A parent's grief**: the serpent comes home carrying meat for his friend (II.54), finds the nest gone, weeps before
   the sun; his lament is a legal plea ("you know, Šamaš") and a cry ("his nest is whole, mine scattered; his
   fledglings safe, my children are not").
6. **SB rewrote *anzillu* "abomination" as *Anzû*** (II.72 vs OBS.a+23): the eagle becomes the monstrous bird who stole
   the Tablet of Destinies – a creature that grasped at the gods' power, as the eagle wanted to "dwell in heaven".

### Open decisions (numbered; the minor ones decided)
1. **II.25 – a tool bug, not a translation problem.** eBL's reconstruction of SB II 25 is `U₄.1.KAM@v.TA.AM₃ | inaṣṣarū |
   b[ūla]` (a `%sb` logogram + normalized words). `tools/ebl_fetch.py` put its normalized half under line 24 as
   "N (variant, other manuscripts)" and left line 25's N empty, so `verify ET` reports "II.25: transliteration differs
   from the base text". The entry carries eBL's real reconstruction. Needs: a fix in `ebl_fetch.py` (keep a
   reconstruction that starts with a non-normalized logogram) and a regenerated `Etana-SB-II/reading-edition.md`, or an
   allow-list entry in `kings_tools.py`. (Coordinator; I did not touch the tools.)
2. ***ḫarrānu* / *urḫu* across corpora (coordinator / user).** Renderings §10.3 has *ḫarrānu* = `דֶּרֶךְ`, *urḫu* =
   `אֹרַח` (and the ✓ formula `לך באורח, חלוף את ההר` for *alik urḫa*); the Gilgameš and wisdom corpora have the
   reverse (*urḫu* = `דֶּרֶךְ`, *ḫarrānu* = `אֹרַח`). The harmonization of 2026-10-21 lists *ḫarrānu* as "kept by sense",
   but *urḫu* is not. In my lines only OBS.1 (`אל ימצא דרך`), which follows §10.3. If harmonized to Gilgameš: OBS.1 →
   `אל ימצא אורח`, and the formula → `לך בדרך, חלוף את ההר` (ET3, II.75 …).
   **Coordinator, 2026-10-22: harmonized to Gilgameš** (*urḫu* = `דֶּרֶךְ`, *ḫarrānu* = `אוֹרַח`, vocalized): OBS.1 changed, the formula is `לך בדרך, חלוף את ההר,`.
3. ***bâru* = `לָכַד`** instead of §10.3's `צָד` (decided; reason above). §10.3's prose strings need updating.
4. ***nērebu* = `מָבוֹא`**, *nērebtu* = `מְבוֹאָה`, instead of §10.3's `מַעֲבָר` (decided).
5. **Animals** (decided): *armu* `אַקּוֹ`, *turāḫu* `יָעֵל`, *ditānu* `*תְּאוֹ*`, *sappāru* `*זֶמֶר*`. Side collision found in
   another corpus: Enki and the World Order renders *taraḫ* (in the boat name *taraḫ abzu*) `אקו־האפסו`, while EA 182
   and the Curse of Agade render *taraḫ* `יעל` – with this decision `אקו` belongs to *armu*; the EWO boat name could
   become `יעל־האפסו` (creation corpus; coordinator/lead).

   – an existing cross-corpus collision (two Akkadian verbs), for the lead.
7. ***nabalkutu*** "cross; turn over" = `חָצָה` for crossing a mountain (renderings §0.3) but `הִתְהַפֵּךְ עַל` for a trap
   that springs over its prey (II.22, 48; MA.b+1) – one lemma, two senses (decided).
8. ***rūʾu* `עָמִית` vs *râʾu* `הִתְרוֹעֵעַ`** (MA.b+11): the Hebrew verb keeps the Semitic root (= `רֵעַ`), the noun (Gilgameš)

10. ***lū* + *tīde*** = `אָכֵן יָדַעְתָּ` (II.68, OBS.a+19; renderings §2 "*lū* + preterite = `אָכֵן`"; Jer 18:23
    `יָדַעְתָּ`). ET3/ET4 should keep it if the phrase recurs.
11. **II.1 `[קורא?]`** – the gated `קרא` licensed by *nabû*; the [equivalent] to Gen 2:19–20 is marked as a cautious
    proposal (the line is broken); the coordinator may drop it.

### Deviations from renderings §10 (decided at the first occurrence, with the reason)
1. ***kutum libbi* = `מִכְסֵה הַלֵּב`** (II.83, 110), not §10.3's `מכסה הקרביים`: `קֶרֶב` (`קְרָבַיִם`) is the
   [cognate] of *qerbu* (MA c+23, II.145, 150, MA d+9, e+10), and *libbu* of the carcass is `לֵב` in every line of the
   scene (MA c+5, c+20; II.77, 84, 91, 100, 111; of the pit II.146, 151, MA d+10). The sacrificial parallel stays
   visible in the apparatus: `אֶת הַחֵלֶב הַמְכַסֶּה אֶת הַקֶּרֶב` (Lev 3:3).
2. ***kubru* = `עֹבִי`** (II.134; Gilgameš VI 127, 136, 142, 161), not §10.3's `מִשְׁמַנֵּי`: one Akkadian word = one
   Hebrew word, and `וּמִשְׁמַנֵּי הָאָרֶץ` (Gen 27:28) would be an echo without basis.
3. ***šubtu* = `מִשְׁכָּן`** (II.78, 92; Gilgameš), *šubta nadû* = `הֵנִיחַ מִשְׁכָּן` (*nadû* "lay" = `הניח`, ET1) –
   Foster's "ambush" is the sense (eBL: jB *šubtu* also "trap"); `מַאֲרָב` belongs to *ušbu* (Gilgameš I 168).
4. **MA d+7 has *etiq šadâm-ma*, not *šadâ ebir*** as §10.3 says (the tablet: *e-tiq KUR-ma*): so `חלוף את ההר`, the SB
   formula; *[mā?]* (restored quotation particle) is not translated (the FORMULAS regex anchors `^לך בדרך`; the quotation
   mark does its work). Also: *urḫu* is written ITI ("month") there – a homophone spelling (*arḫu* / *urḫu*), noted.
5. **II.133 word order:** `יום יום – אֵתָנָה אל שַׁמַשׁ מתחנן:` – the Akkadian has the name first (*etana ūmišam-ma
   imtaḫḫara šamaš*), but the ✓ formula requires `^יום יום`, and the Hebrew keeps the two daily prayers (123, 133) visibly
   parallel.

### Key findings
**The Bible.**
1. **Genesis 37 in a fable.** The eagle's fate follows Joseph's step by step: "come, let us go down" (II.97 – `לְכוּ`,
   Gen 37:20), stripped – plucked (II.86, 120 ↔ 37:23), "thrown into the pit" with the same verb (*nadû* = `הִשְׁלִיךְ`;
   37:22, 24), a pit "without water" where he should die of hunger and thirst (II.87 ↔ 37:24), an anonymous "man" (II.132
   ↔ 37:15), and drawn up out of the pit (OBM c+2, MA e+10, f+7 ↔ 37:28). And Ps 105:17 says it in the god's voice:
   `שָׁלַח לִפְנֵיהֶם אִישׁ` (תהלים קה:יז) – exactly Šamaš's *amēlu ša ašapparakku* (II.132); Gen 45:5 `כִּי לְמִחְיָה
   שְׁלָחַנִי אֱלֹהִים לִפְנֵיכֶם` (בראשית מה:ה).
2. **Isaiah 14 completed.** ET2 found Isa 14:13 in the eagle's plan ("I will go up and dwell in heaven", II.43); ET3's lines
   complete it: the one who said it is "brought down to the depths of the pit" and "cast out" (Isa 14:15, 19) – the
   eagle "cast" in the pit (II.146, 151), "brought low" (OBM b+10 *išpil*; Prov 29:23, Obad 1:4).
3. **The lament psalms of the pit.** The eagle's prayer (II.123–127) has their structure and vocabulary: prayer from the
   pit (Ps 88:5, 7; Lam 3:55), "keep me alive" (Ps 30:4, 119:25), and the vow of praise: "for days of eternity I will make
   your *zikru* heard" (II.127) – *zikru* / `זֵכֶר` [cognate], the same argument as Ps 6:6 and Ps 142:8. MA gives the vow
   to the man instead (e+13).
4. **The barren king's prayer and the "name".** Etana's plea (II.140–142, MA d+4) meets Gen 30 (30:1, 14, 22–23), Isaac's
   and Hannah's repeated prayers (Gen 25:21, 1 Sam 1:7), and above all the "name" that only a son preserves (2 Sam 18:18,
   Deut 25:6, Ruth 4:10); Gen 11:4's name made by building is a [divergence].
5. **The piety the Torah forbids.** Etana's claim on the god lists honouring the gods, *revering the ghosts* and the
   incense of the *šāʾilātu* (female inquirers) – Deut 18:11 forbids the `שֹׁאֵל` (*šāʾilu* [cognate]) and the inquirer of
   the dead, and Deut 26:14 boasts of giving nothing to the dead; 1 Sam 28:6–7 shows a king who did try dreams and a woman
   diviner. His argument "you ate, Šamaš … earth, you drank the blood" is what Ps 50:13 rejects; Isa 1:11 (rams) likewise.
6. **The bargain in the language of marriage.** The eagle offers the serpent a bridegroom's *nudunnû* (II.114) – Gen
   34:12 `מֹהַר וּמַתָּן וְאֶתְּנָה` – and in OB offers Etana "tell me what you ask, I will give it" (OBM c+7), the same
   cohortative; 1 Kgs 3:5 ("ask what I shall give you", in a night dream) is the closest royal parallel.
7. **The serpent's refusal and 1 Kings 20.** "If I release you, how shall I answer Šamaš? Your punishment would turn on me"
   (II.116–118) is the logic of 1 Kgs 20:42: the one who "released" (`שִׁלַּחְתָּ` = *wuššuru*) the man under the god's
   ban dies in his place.
8. **Small words and false echoes.** *kutum libbi* is the caul fat of Lev 3:3 (the god's portion; II.83); *petû* =
   `פָּתַח`, not Gen 3:5's `פָּקַח` (OBM c+9, MA d+3); Foster's "plant of life" (MA f+5: the tablet has "of birth"),
   "lament" (II.73: *unnīnu* "supplication"), "Netherworld" (II.135), "no doubt" (II.100), "king of the wild beasts"
   (*būlu*), "tail feathers" (*nuballu*), "ambush" (*šubtu* "dwelling") – each checked against the Akkadian.

**The ancient mind.**
1. **Šamaš's justice and mercy, separated.** He hears the wronged serpent, prepares the trap himself ("I have bound a wild
   ox for you"), makes the serpent his executioner; he will not save the oath-breaker ("you are under oath – I will not
   visit you") – yet he does not let him die: he "sends a man". He answers two prayers with one answer: a king without a
   son and an eagle without wings need each other.
2. **The serpent is an official, not an avenger.** He refuses mercy for fear of answering to the god "above": the
   punishment he inflicts would fall on him if he relented – the logic of a deputy under oath.
3. **The pious king who has done everything.** Fat sheep, the blood of rams poured on the earth, the gods honoured, the
   ghosts feared, the incense of the dream-women used up – and no son. Piety as a contract with the god, and the son as
   the one who will keep the father's "name" and feed his ghost: without him the king dies twice.
4. **Dreams as a technique.** MA: Etana "lay down and examines (*barû*)" – incubation; the god answers in "the bed of the
   night"; SB has the same answer in direct speech; and the *šāʾilātu* used incense to obtain dreams.
5. **The hunter's world.** A carcass as bait, a pitfall (*šuttatu*), birds of prey coming down in a crowd, the eagle that
   circles and pecks "in front and behind" before going in – exact observation of animals; and the recovery is slow:
   seven months, the eighth (OBM), attempt after attempt (MA f).
6. **Friendship as a renewable treaty.** The eagle repeats to Etana the words with which he once bound himself to the
   serpent (OBM c+6 = II.9) – the second friendship rises out of the pit of the first; MA seals it "for ever" (e+17).
7. **A Hebrew-only pun:** the cohortative "I will give" (`אֶתְּנָה`, II.114, OBM c+7) is spelt like Etana's name in
   Hebrew; kept vocalized, noted, not exploited.

### Deviations from renderings §10 and the briefs (decided at the first occurrence, with the reason)
1. **The flight's question is not *ibrī amur māta mīnâ ibašši*** (renderings §10.3 and the brief quote the literature):
   the tablet has *ibrī naplis-(ma) mātu kī mīni ibašši* in the first flight (31, 35, 39) and *dugul ibrī mātu kī
   ibašši* in the second (b+27, b+33, b+37; MA i+6, i+13) – two verbs, two word orders, *mīni* only in the first. So two
   strings: `רֵעִי, הִסְתַּכֵּל – הארץ, כְּמוֹ מָה הִיא!` (*palāsu* N = `הִסְתַּכֵּל`, ET2) and `צְפֵה, רֵעִי – הארץ, איך
   היא!` (*dagālu* = `צָפָה`, Gilgameš IX 174, Adapa, Theodicy). §10.3's row should be corrected.
2. ***bēru* + three verbs**: *ištēn / šanâ / šalša bēra* + *ullû* (*elû* D) in the first flight and MA = `העלה אותו`
   (the root of *elû* kept, as Š `הֶעֱלָה`, MA e+10); *šušqû* (*šaqû* D) in the second = `הגביה אותו` (§10.3);
   *nasāku* in the drops = `זרק אותו` (Gilgameš XI 293 *issukaššu* `זרקו`; `הִשְׁלִיךְ` is *nadû*'s).
3. ***târu ana* "turn into" = `נֶהְפַּךְ לְ־`** (Gilgameš XI 119, IV 106; Ludlul I 18, 84), not `שָׁב` (licensed by
   *târu*, but in Hebrew "return").
4. ***kappu* of Etana = `כָּנָף`** (III.27, b+19), literal, against Foster's "hands": `כַּף` is *rittu* (Gilgameš VII 170,
   Ludlul V 12); the one Akkadian word for the eagle's wings and the man's "hands" is kept and footnoted.
5. ***ina lēt* = `אֵצֶל`** (III.25), not `צַד` (Gilgameš IX 6 *ana lēt* `אל צד`), because §10.3 gives `צַד` to *idu*
   (III.26, b+28); *lētu* as a noun stays `לְחִי`.
6. ***ṣīt šamši* / *ereb šamši*:** the coordinator's `מִזְרַח שֶׁמֶשׁ` / `מְבוֹא הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ` were not used – neither phrase

   *nērebu* (ET2).
7. ***palgu* = `תְּעָלָה`** (§10.3; = Sum. *pa₅*, Enki and the World Order 324) kept, although Enūma eliš VII 63 has the
   cognate `פֶּלֶג` (open decision 1); the cognate is tagged in MA i+10.
8. ***mūšaru* = `עֲרוּגָה`, *kirû* `גַּן`**: *kirû* does not occur in my lines (the "garden" of Foster's MA i+9 is
   *mūšaru*), so `גַּן` was not used at all; *nukaribbu* = `גַּנָּן` (b+38).
9. **III.32's restored half-line is not translated** (`מְפַכִּ[ים? …]`): eBL's *nārātu? kī? palgî?* is Foster's oral

### Key findings
**The Bible.**
1. **Deuteronomy 32:11 and Exodus 19:4 told as a story.** What the Bible uses as an image of God – the eagle that
   spreads its wings, takes up, carries on its pinion – is here a narrative in three movements: the eagle tells the man
   how to hold on (III.26–29; b+18–b+23), lifts him league after league, and, when he falls, dives and catches him on
   its wings four times (III.44–51 `הנשר נפל – וקיבל אותו בכנפיו`). The verb is the very cognate: *luššīka*
   "let me carry you" (b+17) = `וָאֶשָּׂא אֶתְכֶם עַל כַּנְפֵי נְשָׁרִים` (Exod 19:4) – but there God carries his people to
   himself, here an eagle carries a man to the gods.
2. **Isaiah 14 and Obadiah reversed.** ET2 and ET3 found Isa 14:13–15 in the eagle's boast and fall (II.43, II.146);
   now the eagle lifts (*šaqû* D = `הִגְבִּיהַּ`, b+25; cf. Obad 1:4, Jer 49:16, Job 39:27) and the man says, with the
   same verb as the boast, "I will not go up to heaven" (III.42 ↔ II.43). The ascent in Etana is no hubris: Šamaš sends
   it, Ištar is its goal, a son its purpose. Against Gen 11:4 (`וְרֹאשׁוֹ בַשָּׁמַיִם … וְנַעֲשֶׂה לָּנוּ שֵׁם`) this is the
   clearest [divergence] of the corpus: a "name" (*šumu*) asked of the gods, not made against them.
3. **Genesis 28 and 37–41 in two versions of one dream.** SB: Etana dreams that he and the eagle pass through the gates
   of Anu, Enlil, Ea, then of Sîn, Šamaš, Adad and Ištar, bowing at each, open a sealed house and see Ištar enthroned
   with lions; he wakes in terror (Gen 28:12–17 `שַׁעַר הַשָּׁמָיִם`, `וַיִּיקַץ … וַיִּירָא`); the friend says the dreams are
   plain; and the dream is fulfilled in its own words (b+40–b+45; Gen 41:13). The dreamer bows to the sun, moon and
   Venus gods – the exact reverse of Joseph's second dream (Gen 37:9). MA: two dreams instead, of kingship and plenty –
   plants gathered, heaps of loads in all the land, enemies "evil serpents" bowing beneath the dreamer – interpreted
   as "good" and "at your feet" (h+2–h+22): Joseph's sheaves (37:7), the doubled dream (37:9, 41:32), Joseph's heaps
   (41:49), the royal psalms (Ps 72:9, 110:1). The two versions show the same plot carrying two dream types: an
   ascent-vision and a kingship-dream.
4. **Throne visions.** Ištar on a throne with lions under it (b+11–b+12) meets 1 Kgs 10:19–20 (the lion throne) and
   Isa 6:1 / Ezek 1:26 (the throne seen in heaven); the dreamer's prostration, Ezek 1:28.
5. **The earth from above** (III.31–41, b+27–b+38): Isa 40:22 `וְיֹשְׁבֶיהָ כַּחֲגָבִים`; the vocabulary of the view is
   agricultural – `מַרְבֵּץ` (*tarbaṣu* [cognate], Ezek 25:5, Zeph 2:15), `עֲרוּגָה` (*mūšaru*), `שֹׁקֶת`, `תְּעָלָה`, a
   gardener's dike. (Our proposal:) Ezekiel's eagle parable, which ET2 found in the poplar and the oath, also has the
   garden "beds" (`עֲרֻגוֹת`, Ezek 17:7, 10) and the wing words `כָּנָף` / `אֵבֶר` / `נוֹצָה` (17:3) = *kappu* / *abru* /
   *nāṣu*: Ezek 17 is the closest biblical counterpart of the whole Etana imagery.
6. **Hannah and Leah.** If NinNA1 belongs to Tablet III, Etana "reached his city and his house" and a woman speaks to
   him about the plant of birth, a salve(?), cooking (a+4–a+9): the return home after the prayer (1 Sam 1:19) and the
   plant passed between wife and husband (Gen 30:14–16; `דּוּדָאִים` reserved).
7. **False echoes avoided:** Foster's "sacred bond" (*ellu* "pure", MA h+2), "made understand" (*maḫāru* Š),
   "hands" (*kappu* "wings"), "wearing a tiara" (*ruṣṣunat*, of a powerful voice), "garden" (*mūšaru* "bed"), "brook /
   watery" (the obscure *mēmē*), "rivers … like brooks" (a restoration); `סֻלָּם`, `רָקִיעַ`, `פָּתַר`, `מִגְדָּל` kept out
   of the Hebrew lines; *pašāru* = `פָּשַׁר`.

**The ancient mind.**
1. **The eagle as a royal vehicle.** Akkad-period cylinder seals (24th–22nd centuries BCE) show a man carried up on an
   eagle's back while shepherds and dogs look up: the story is a millennium and a half older than the Nineveh tablets.
2. **The world seen by an irrigation farmer.** From above, the land becomes a garden bed, the sea a sheepfold, a
   trough, the water of a canal, a gardener's dike; the rivers trickle. The ancient imagination measured height in
   double-hours of walking (*bēru*) and the last drop in half-reeds (III.50).
3. **Fear and failure without blame.** At the third league the king who wanted heaven cannot see the land and asks to
   be put down on a path and walk to his city; the eagle does not argue, but lowers him in four drops, catching him
   each time. The first flight fails; the second, after a dream, succeeds.
4. **Dreams as the gods' language, read by a friend.** Not a professional interpreter but the eagle says "your dreams
   are plain" (SB) or "your dream is good" (MA); the "good dream" is a category of Babylonian dream lore; the dreamer
   wakes trembling (as Gudea did); the dream is fulfilled to the letter.
5. **The court of heaven.** The gates follow the gods' rank: the great triad (Anu, Enlil, Ea), then the astral gods
   (Sîn, Šamaš, Ištar) and Adad; at each gate one bows, as at a king's audience; at the end, a sealed storehouse
   (sealings with cylinder seals), a door pushed open, and the goddess enthroned with her lions.
6. **Ištar, giver of birth and of kingship.** The plant is "with Ištar the lady" (III.24): the goddess who sought a
   shepherd for Kiš (I.19–22) holds the means of an heir; the gardener of b+38 belongs to her world (Išullānu,
   Sargon).
7. **Understanding the birds.** SB adds what the older versions take for granted: man and eagle need Šamaš's gift to
   understand each other (III.3–8) – the speech of birds as a divine favour.
8. **The kingship-dream in MA**: the "pure bond above" (*markasu*, the cosmic bond of heaven and earth, cf. Dur-an-ki),
   tribute heaped up, the enemies as "evil serpents" bowing – the fable's serpent turned into the vanquished foe of a
   king.
9. **The lost end.** The Sumerian King List names Etana's son Balīḫ as his successor; Gilgameš VII sees Etana among the
   dead: tradition knew both the son and the death.

### Notes for the clean translation
For the coordinator's introduction to the whole epic (`translation--intro.rtl.md`):
1. **Six fragmentary versions, one story, no end.** OB Morgan and Susa (18th–17th c. BCE), the MA tablets from Aššur
   (13th–11th c.), and SB I–III from Nineveh (7th c.); every version is broken, and the end of the epic is lost in all.
   Labels like `a+1` mark the first preserved line after a gap of unknown length; `[ ]`, `…`, `?` and italics are part
   of the text, not decoration.
2. **The plot in four movements:** kingship comes down from heaven and Kiš has a king but no heir (OBM, SB I); the
   fable of the eagle and the serpent – oath, betrayal, Šamaš's trap, the eagle in the pit (OBS, MA a–c, SB II); the
   childless king's prayer and the rescue of the eagle (SB II, OBM b–c, MA d–f); the dreams and the two flights to
   heaven (SB III, MA g–i). The fable is not a digression: the two needs – a king without a son, an eagle without
   wings – are joined by the god.
3. **Šamaš directs everything**: judge of the oath, maker of the trap, sender of the "man", giver of the speech of
   birds. Ištar holds the plant of birth and sits enthroned with lions at the top of heaven.
4. **Key words kept as the Akkadian has them:** `שֵׁם` (*šumu*) is both "name" and "offspring"; `עשב הלידה` is the "plant
   of birth" (Foster's "plant of life" in MA f+5 is an error); `רֵעִי` (*ibrī*) is the eagle's and Etana's word for each
   other; `בֵּרוּ` is a "double hour" of walking, c. 10 km (three of them c. 30 km); a half-reed (`חֲצִי קָנֶה`) c. 1.5 m.
5. **Repetition is the art.** Identical Akkadian has identical Hebrew: the speech formulas, the oath and its curses,
   the hunt refrain, Šamaš's command and its execution, the plea, the leagues of the flights, the eagle's "look, my
   friend", the drops, and above all the dream and its fulfilment "in the same words" (III b+3–b+8 ↔ b+40–b+45).
   Where a copy differs by a word, the Hebrew differs by that word.
6. **The versions differ in meaningful ways**: SB alone has the speech of birds and the dream of the gods' gates and
   of Ištar; MA alone has two kingship-dreams (Joseph-like), the eagle's identifying Etana and its vow, and gives the
   description of the shrinking earth to Etana; the OB tablets are the shortest and the most direct.
7. **Names are vocalized** where unvocalized Hebrew would mislead: `אֵתָנָה` (not "אֶתְּנָה", "I will give"), `שַׁמַשׁ`
   (the god) vs `הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ` (the sun), `אָנוּ` (not "we"), `סִין`, `אִשְׁתַּר`, `שַׁכַּן`, `אִט` (a ghost).
8. **Biblical words deliberately absent from the Hebrew text** (they belong to the notes): `סֻלָּם`, `מִגְדָּל`,
   `רָקִיעַ`, `עֲקָרָה`, `דּוּדָאִים`, `גּוֹזָל`, `פָּקַח`, `פָּתַר`, `חֵרֶם`, `שְׁאוֹל`; `נֶשֶׁר`, `נָחָשׁ`, `שׁוּחָה`, `בּוֹר`, `רֶשֶׁת`,
   `פַּח`, `חֶרְפָּה`, `נוֹצָה`, `אֵבֶר` appear only for their Akkadian counterparts.
9. **The afterlife of the story**: Akkad-period seals (24th–22nd c.) show the man on the eagle; the Sumerian King List
   calls Etana "the shepherd who went up to heaven" and names his son Balīḫ as successor; in Gilgameš VII Etana sits in
   the house of dust (`יושב אֵתָנָה, יושב שַׁכַּן`).
10. **The main biblical comparisons** (for the reader of the commentary): Gen 11:4 and Isa 14:12–15 (ascent and fall,
    as [divergence]); Exod 19:4 and Deut 32:11 (the carrying eagle); Gen 28:12–17 (the dream of heaven's gate);
    Gen 37 and 40–41 (dreams, doubled dreams, interpretation; the pit); Gen 30 and 1 Sam 1 (the plea for a child, the
    "name"); Ezek 17 (eagle, poplar, oath, net, garden beds); 1 Kgs 10:19–20 (the lion throne).
