# Parallels index: the legends-of-kings corpus

## Key findings (2026-10-21, Round 2 run 6)
1. **The spell of Nudimmud (EA 134–155) is told in the past, not foretold.** The forms decide it: *ḫe₂-* + ḫamṭu
   (146 *ḫe₂-en-na-da-ab-dug₄* "indeed they spoke"), ḫamṭu *mi-ni-in-ĝar-ra* (154 "until he placed") and the copula
   (155 "was one"). So Mittermayer's reading (a past golden age) against ETCSL / Kramer 1968 (a wish or a future).
   The parallel to Gen 11:1–9 is real but precise: **one tongue (*eme* = `לָשׁוֹן`, the word of Gen 10:5, 20, 31, not
   `שָׂפָה` of Gen 11)**; a god who "places a different tongue" (*kur₂* "be different" = `שׁוֹנֶה`, not `בָּלַל`); **no
   scattering, no tower, no sin** – the brick (*šeg₁₂*, `לְבֵנַת כּוּלַבָּה`) is Uruk's pride, not its hubris; and the
   motive is the contest of the lords (*a-da*), not human ambition. The order is reversed: the spell ends where Gen 11
   begins ("the tongue of mankind was one").
2. **The heavy mouth (EA 501–502)** – *ka-ni dugud* (ETCSL, ORACC) ↔ `כְבַד פֶּה` (Exod 4:10) [equivalent; *dugud* =
   *kabtu* = `כבד`]; but Mittermayer reads the same signs *inim i₃-dugud* "the matter was too weighty" (↔ Exod 18:18
   `כִּי כָבֵד מִמְּךָ הַדָּבָר`), and EA 448 *inim dugud* supports her. Both readings meet a mediator: Aaron, Jethro's
   judges – and here clay. With the staff in the envoy's hand (434 ↔ Exod 4:17) and the sign (*ĝiškim* = *ittu* ~
   `אוֹת`, 496 ↔ Exod 4:8), Exod 4's commissioned messenger is assembled in one place (our proposal).
3. **The invention of the letter (EA 500–506)** is told in the narrator's aside "formerly … now, on this day" – the
   form of 1 Sam 9:9 and Ruth 4:7 [equivalent]. The words are "set up" (*gub*), not "written"; "the word was a peg"
   (540, ETCSL – the emic name of cuneiform, Modern Hebrew `כתב היתדות`) is contested by Mittermayer's collation.
4. **The Sargon bitumen is `חֵמָר`, the word of Exod 2:3; the *kupru* claim does not stand.** The tablet has ESIR
   (King: *iddî*) = *iṭṭû*, rendered by the flood corpus's rule `חֵמָר` – the very word of `וַתַּחְמְרָה בַחֵמָר
   וּבַזָּפֶת`; *kupru* (ESIR.UD.A, Gen 6:14 `כֹּפֶר`) is absent. The same verb (*iškunanni* = `וַתָּשֶׂם`), the same
   place (*ina aḫi Puratti* ↔ `עַל שְׂפַת הַיְאֹר`), the same adoption (`וַיְהִי לָהּ לְבֵן` ↔ *ana mārūti*); and the
   divergences – the basket is Akkadian *quppu* (Mishnaic `קֻפָּה`) not Egyptian `תֵּבַת גֹּמֶא`; the mother casts
   the child in (Pharaoh's `תַּשְׁלִיכֻהוּ`, Exod 1:22) instead of placing him at the bank; the finder is a
   water-drawer (*dālû* ~ `דָּלָה`, Exod 2:19) not a princess; "lifted me up" without a name pun (`מְשִׁיתִהוּ`).
5. **Grain, rain and election (EA 308–360, 542–576):** the city of grain feeds the starving mountain (Joseph, Gen 41)
   [equivalent / divergence: a gift-challenge, not a sale]; the storm god's rain makes wheat grow by itself (`סְפִיחַ`,
   Lev 25:5; Judg 5:4); Aratta's people "separated from all people" by Dumuzi (Lev 20:26; Deut 4:34) and survivors of
   the flood, "after the flood had swept over" (Gen 10:32) – election by a god of love, not of covenant.
6. **Uruk is `אוּרוּךְ` throughout** (guidelines §6.4); `אֶרֶךְ` (Gen 10:10) appears only in the notes.
7. **The ancient mind:** the messenger as a profession of memory and formula (whole speeches repeated word for word);
   riddles as diplomacy and as school play built on the lexical lists; kingship as the goddess's love (Inana chooses
   her *en*; Ištar loves the gardener); a contest of cities ended by trade; a founder-king who turns the shame of his
   birth into election, and a royal inscription that dares its successors.
8. **Limits:** the Sargon text rests on King 1907 (Westenholz 1997 and K 7249 unreachable; CDLI offline all day) and
   must be checked against Westenholz before publication; Mittermayer's readings are cited from her commentary and
   introduction (the OCR of her line-by-line text is poor; a reading quoted from it is marked as hers, not ours).
