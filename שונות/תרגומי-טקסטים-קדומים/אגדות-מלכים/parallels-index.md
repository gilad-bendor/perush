# Parallels index: the legends-of-kings corpus

## Key findings (2026-10-21, Round 2 run 6)
1. **The spell of Nudimmud (EA 134–155) is told in the past, not foretold.** The forms decide it: *ḫe₂-* + ḫamṭu
   (146 *ḫe₂-en-na-da-ab-dug₄* "indeed they spoke"), ḫamṭu *mi-ni-in-ĝar-ra* (154 "until he placed") and the copula
   (155 "was one"). So Mittermayer's reading (a past golden age) against ETCSL / Kramer 1968 (a wish or a future).
   The parallel to Gen 11:1–9 is real but precise: **one tongue (*eme* = `לָשׁוֹן`, the word of Gen 10:5, 20, 31, not
   `שָׂפָה` of Gen 11)**; a god who "places a different tongue" (*kur₂* "be different" = `שׁוֹנֶה`, not `בָּלַל`); **no
   scattering, no tower, no sin** – the brick (*šeg₁₂*, `לְבֵנַת כּוּלַבָּה`) is Uruk's pride, not its hubris; and the
   motive is the contest of the lords (*a-da*), not human ambition. The order is reversed: the spell ends where Gen 11
   begins ("the tongue of mankind was one").
2. **The heavy mouth (EA 501–502)** – *ka-ni dugud* (ETCSL, ORACC) ↔ `כְבַד פֶּה` (Exod 4:10) [equivalent; *dugud* =
   *kabtu* = `כבד`]; but Mittermayer reads the same signs *inim i₃-dugud* "the matter was too weighty" (↔ Exod 18:18
   `כִּי כָבֵד מִמְּךָ הַדָּבָר`), and EA 448 *inim dugud* supports her. Both readings meet a mediator: Aaron, Jethro's
   judges – and here clay. With the staff in the envoy's hand (434 ↔ Exod 4:17) and the sign (*ĝiškim* = *ittu* ~
   `אוֹת`, 496 ↔ Exod 4:8), Exod 4's commissioned messenger is assembled in one place (our proposal).
3. **The invention of the letter (EA 500–506)** is told in the narrator's aside "formerly … now, on this day" – the
   form of 1 Sam 9:9 and Ruth 4:7 [equivalent]. The words are "set up" (*gub*), not "written"; "the word was a peg"
   (540, ETCSL – the emic name of cuneiform, Modern Hebrew `כתב היתדות`) is contested by Mittermayer's collation.
4. **The Sargon bitumen is `חֵמָר`, the word of Exod 2:3; the *kupru* claim does not stand.** The tablet has ESIR
   (King: *iddî*) = *iṭṭû*, rendered by the flood corpus's rule `חֵמָר` – the very word of `וַתַּחְמְרָה בַחֵמָר
   וּבַזָּפֶת`; *kupru* (ESIR.UD.A, Gen 6:14 `כֹּפֶר`) is absent. The same verb (*iškunanni* = `וַתָּשֶׂם`), the same
   place (*ina aḫi Puratti* ↔ `עַל שְׂפַת הַיְאֹר`), the same adoption (`וַיְהִי לָהּ לְבֵן` ↔ *ana mārūti*); and the
   divergences – the basket is Akkadian *quppu* (Mishnaic `קֻפָּה`) not Egyptian `תֵּבַת גֹּמֶא`; the mother casts
   the child in (Pharaoh's `תַּשְׁלִיכֻהוּ`, Exod 1:22) instead of placing him at the bank; the finder is a
   water-drawer (*dālû* ~ `דָּלָה`, Exod 2:19) not a princess; "lifted me up" without a name pun (`מְשִׁיתִהוּ`).
5. **Grain, rain and election (EA 308–360, 542–576):** the city of grain feeds the starving mountain (Joseph, Gen 41)
   [equivalent / divergence: a gift-challenge, not a sale]; the storm god's rain makes wheat grow by itself (`סְפִיחַ`,
   Lev 25:5; Judg 5:4); Aratta's people "separated from all people" by Dumuzi (Lev 20:26; Deut 4:34) and survivors of
   the flood, "after the flood had swept over" (Gen 10:32) – election by a god of love, not of covenant.
6. **Uruk is `אוּרוּךְ` throughout** (guidelines §6.4); `אֶרֶךְ` (Gen 10:10) appears only in the notes.
7. **The ancient mind:** the messenger as a profession of memory and formula (whole speeches repeated word for word);
   riddles as diplomacy and as school play built on the lexical lists; kingship as the goddess's love (Inana chooses
   her *en*; Ištar loves the gardener); a contest of cities ended by trade; a founder-king who turns the shame of his
   birth into election, and a royal inscription that dares its successors.
8. **Limits:** the Sargon text rests on King 1907 (Westenholz 1997 and K 7249 unreachable; CDLI offline all day) and
   must be checked against Westenholz before publication; Mittermayer's readings are cited from her commentary and
   introduction (the OCR of her line-by-line text is poor; a reading quoted from it is marked as hers, not ours).


## Key findings (2026-10-22, Round 3 run 2: Etana, `3-אגדת-אתנה/`)
1. **Gen 11 without the tower.** The gods "form" the city (*eṣēru* = `יָצַר`), lay its foundations (*uššu* ~ Aram.
   `אֻשַּׁיָּא`, Ezra 5:16) and its bricks (*libittu* = `לְבֵנָה`, Gen 11:3), and kingship "comes down" from heaven (OBM
   14 `[המל]כות – מן השמים ירדה`; Gen 11:5's `וַיֵּרֶד` is the god): the city and the king are gifts, not hubris.
   SB II 2's "tower" (*dimtu* = `צְרִיחַ`) is one uncertain sign beside a shrine of Adad. The ascent to heaven happens
   – on an eagle – and the "name" is sought (*šuma šuknanni* "establish for me a name" = an heir), but nobody is
   punished: a [divergence] on every point of Gen 11:4, and a reversal of its moral.
2. **The plea for a child and the "name":** `נסח את חרפתי, שִׂים לי שֵׁם!` (II 142 = III 14) ↔ `אָסַף אֱלֹהִים אֶת
   חֶרְפָּתִי` (Gen 30:23), the son as the name that survives (2 Sam 18:18; Deut 25:6); the plant of birth ↔ Rachel's
   mandrakes (Gen 30:14) [divergence]; Etana's daily prayer ↔ Isaac's (Gen 25:21) and Hannah's (1 Sam 1). Etana's piety
   – offerings to Šamaš and to "the earth", honoured ghosts (*eṭemmu* = `אִט`), incense for the female dream-inquirers
   (*šāʾiltu* ~ `שֹׁאֵל`) – is what Deut 18:11 and 26:14 forbid and what Saul tried (1 Sam 28:6–7).
3. **The oath of friends and Šamaš's boundary:** `לפני שַׁמַשׁ הגיבור – שבועה נשבעו שניהם` ↔ `נִשְׁבְּעוּ שְׁנֵיהֶם` (Gen
   21:31), "come, let us … I and you" ↔ Gen 31:44; "whoever passes Šamaš's boundary" (*itû* = `גְּבוּל`) ↔ Laban's heap
   (Gen 31:52) and the neighbour's boundary (Deut 19:14, 27:17). Breaking the oath is "eating a taboo" (*anzillu* =
   `תּוֹעֵבָה`, ↔ Deut 14:3) – and the eagle breaks it by eating.
4. **The eagle: Isaiah 14, Obadiah, Deut 32 and Exod 19 as one story.** "I will go up and dwell in heaven" (II 43,
   `אֶעֱלֶה – ובשמים אֵשֵׁב`) has the two verbs of Isa 14:13 in its order, "in his heart"; the eagle is cast into the pit
   (Isa 14:15); *šaqû* D `הִגְבִּיהַּ` (Obad 1:4); the eagle carries the man on its wings (*naššû* `נָשָׂא` ↔ `וָאֶשָּׂא
   אֶתְכֶם עַל כַּנְפֵי נְשָׁרִים`, Exod 19:4; Deut 32:11) and catches him falling. Ezek 17 (eagle, `צַפְצָפָה`, broken oath,
   the god's net, `עֲרֻגוֹת`) is the closest single biblical counterpart to the whole imagery (our proposal).
5. **The pit (Gen 37):** plucked (= stripped), "cast into the pit" with the same verb (*nadû* = `הִשְׁלִיךְ`; *šuttatu* =
   `שׁוּחָה`), no food or water, an anonymous "man" sent by the god (II 132 ↔ Ps 105:17), drawn up; the psalms of the pit
   (Ps 6:6 – the eagle's vow to make the god's *zikru* heard; Ps 30, 88; Lam 3:55). The net of the divine judge "is
   the wide earth, the distant heaven" (II 69–70 ↔ Ps 139:8; Amos 9:2–3; Hos 7:12; Ezek 12:13).
6. **Dreams:** Etana's dream of the gates of the gods and of Ištar enthroned with lions (III b+1–14) is fulfilled in
   its own words (b+40–45) ↔ Gen 28:12–17 (`שַׁעַר הַשָּׁמָיִם`) and the lion throne (1 Kgs 10:19–20); MA's two
   kingship dreams (heaps "in all the land", enemies bowing "at your feet") are the closest parallel to Joseph's doubled
   dreams (Gen 37:5–11; 41:32); the interpreter is a friend, not a professional.
7. **False echoes avoided:** Foster's "not yet" (OBM 7–9 are statives; Gen 2:5's `טֶרֶם` in the notes only), "tower",
   "Netherworld", "plant of life" (MA f+5 has "plant of birth"), "Ishtar came down from heaven" (resting on an abandoned
   reading of OBM 14), "no doubt" (= "perhaps"); "open my eyes" is *petû* = `פָּתַח`, not Gen 3:5's `פָּקַח`.
8. **The ancient mind:** kingship as an object kept before Anu and lowered to men; the king as shepherd and builder;
   an animal fable written as a treaty with its curses; a child wiser than his father; a parent's grief; Šamaš who keeps
   justice and mercy apart (he will not save the oath-breaker, yet sends help) and answers two needs with one answer;
   a son as the "name" that keeps a man alive after death; the earth seen from above by an irrigation farmer (a
   sheepfold, a garden bed, a trough); a king's fear of height, unblamed. **Limits:** all six versions are broken and
   the end is lost in all; the MA order and the placement of some SB fragments follow eBL.

## Key findings (2026-10-31, Round 5 run 5: the Cuthaean Legend, `4-אגדת-כותה/`)
1. **Asking the gods before battle, and defying them.** *šâlu* is the cognate of `שָׁאַל` in the Bible's own idiom of consulting a god (1 Sam 23:2, 14:37, 28:6); the method is extispicy, which the Bible names once (Ezek 21:26). The king's defiance (SB 80–83, "which lion consulted a diviner?") is the reverse of 1 Sam 15:23: there rebellion is "like divination", here rebellion is the refusal of divination.
2. **The reverse of 1 Sam 15.** After obeying, the omens order mercy and the Morning Star forbids destroying the enemy; the king "did not bring his hand to kill" (*qāta wabālu* `הֵבִיא יָד`; Gen 22:12, 37:22 `שָׁלַח יָד` in the apparatus). Same frame as Saul's war, opposite ethics.
3. **The created host and the flood.** The enemy is the gods' own creation – "built" (`בָּנָה`, not `בָּרָא`), suckled by Tiāmat, "the work (`מְלֶאכֶת`) of Enlil"; tested with a pin ("if blood comes out, they are humans like us"; Num 13:32–33 as the closest frame; Gen 6:4 only in the apparatus). Ea speaks of the gods' decision "to destroy the seed of mankind" and to keep people in being forever (Gen 6:7, 9:11 in one speech; *šubšû* `הֵבִיא לִכְלַל הֲוָיָה`). In MB A Ea alone builds the host and curses it "so that it not destroy mankind"; OB I calls Akkad's ruin "like the flood of water (*abūb mê*) among the former people" (Gen 6:17's `הַמַּבּוּל מַיִם`).
4. **The curses gather the Bible's own:** civil strife in Isa 19:2's order, the famine price in 2 Kgs 7:1's units (*sūtu* `סְאָה`, *šiqlu* `שֶׁקֶל`), Lev 26:20's earth and tree, Deut 28:65–67's wandering without bread or sleep (MB A).
5. **The stela as a king's instruction** (Deut 17:18–20 in form; Isa 7:4, 30:15 in content): be still, do not go out, "in your wife's lap do your work" (Deut 24:5; Qoh 9:9), answer "Yes, my lord!" (`הֵן`), "requite their wrong with kindness" (*riābu* `גָּמַל`; 1 Sam 24:17, Gen 50:20, Prov 25:21–22). Kutha `כּוּתָה` and Nergal (2 Kgs 17:24, 30).
6. **For the ancient mind:** one verb, "touch", tests both the enemy's flesh and the gods' will; the dead need a name on a stela more than a son; non-resistance is political wisdom because the enemy is the gods' work; across a millennium (OB → MB → SB) the enemy turns from historical peoples into a created race, and the king from blaming the god to blaming himself.
