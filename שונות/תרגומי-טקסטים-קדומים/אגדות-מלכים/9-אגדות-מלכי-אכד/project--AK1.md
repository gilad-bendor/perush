## Key findings
**The ancient mind.** (1) In these OB poems the speech before battle is the centre of kingship: the king does not only command – he is encouraged by his men (CH.1–3: "you have made me trust, you have opened my ears"), consults them ("whatever you tell me I will do", FL i16′–17′), and is advised to caution ("trust the wall, keep your body whole", FL ii16). (2) The body as an army: "let your mouth command your heart, your heart your knees" (CH.15–16) – courage is a chain of command inside a man; the merchants of EA 359 say the opposite of their own knees (ST.12). (3) War as festival and as birth: "a festival of men", "the writhing women writhe, the two mothers are bathed in blood" (CH.19–21) – battle is a rite of Ištar, and the winner is "born". (4) The cosmos joins the march: the forest attacks, the sun darkens, the stars "go out against the enemy" (CH.59–64; FL iv10′–11′). (5) The king as beast and god: "I, Sargon, am your raging lion; call my name in the clash" (LI.9″–12″); in EA 359 Sargon sits "like a god" on a golden throne (ST.14′) – the memory of Akkade's deified kings. (6) The edge of the world: the cedar mountain is "the border of his reign" (FL i12′); Sargon goes "to the land of Uta-rapaštim" (CH.58); the far city of the merchants is "seven leagues" of thicket (ST.27–31). (7) Kingship and commerce: EA 359 is a king's war for traders who say "we are not warriors" (ST.18).
**The Bible.** Deut 20 is not in the Akkadian: there is no "soft heart" (`רך הלבב` stays in the apparatus), and the points of contact are divergences – the virtues of the heart instead of its fears (CH.4, 15), the battle as the "festival" a man must not miss instead of the inaugural feast that exempts him (CH.19–24 ↔ Deut 20:5–7), the cut trees boasted of (CH.114 ↔ Deut 20:19), the non-warriors who ask for war (ST.18 ↔ 20:8), the king who speaks while the prayer is left to another (LI.14″ ↔ 20:2). The closer model for EA 359 is the spies' discouraging report (Num 13:28–31: ST.9, 26). Real lexical contacts: *mutu* `מְתִים` (Isa 3:25), *ḫiālu* `חיל` (Isa 13:8), *ṣinnatu* `צִנָּה`? (1 Sam 17:7), *parzillu*, *kakkabu*, *tīlu*, *sipittu*, *madattu* `מִדָּה`, Karkamiš, Amurru; idioms: "open the ear" (Isa 50:5), the herem list (Josh 6:21, 1 Sam 15:3), "before he finished speaking" (Gen 24:15).
**Readings against SEAL's lemmas / English** (each a finding): CH.15–16 *liwaʾʾir* is *wuʾʾuru* D, not *âru*; CH.80 *ušēridam* is *warādu* Š, not *redû*; FL iii14′ *i-me-ra-am* is *imēru* "donkey", not *immeru* "sheep" (SEAL's "sheep" also in CH.66); LI.15″ *libbaš* is *labāšu* (*lilbaš*), not *bâšu*, and has no "heart"; CH.13 SEAL's English does not match its own lemmas; CH.35 SEAL lemma "spear", SEAL English "shield".

## Open decisions
1. **`מלך הקרב` vs `מלך העִמּוּת`.** *tamḫāru* is AN2's `עִמּוּת` (`קרב` = *qablu*, which EA 359 also has: ST.3, 6, 19). The lines read `מֶלֶךְ הָעִמּוּת`; the file title (the coordinator's) keeps the common name "מלך הקרב". For the coordinator: rename the title, or allow the title as a conventional name.
2. ***etēqu* Š (FL i11′).** Hebrew has no causative of `חלף` meaning "lead across", and `הֶעֱבִיר` belongs to *ebēru* Š (ST.19′); rendered `נתן לחלוף` (twice). A row for the corpus renderings?
3. ***raqādu* `רָקַד` [cognate] (ST.30) vs AN's *mēlulu* `רָקַד`.** By the cognate rule the cognate keeps `רקד`; AN's *mēlulu* would move (Nergal and Ereškigal has *mēlulu* `מָחוֹל`). For the lead.
4. ***ēnu* (LI.2′) = `אֵן`** (transliterated, vocalized) because *bēlu* is `אדון` – while EA's Sumerian *en* is `אדון`. A split by language, recorded.
5. ***qurdu* `גְּבוּרָה` / *qurādūtu* `גִּבּוֹרוּת`** (CH.4, 6, 54): two abstracts kept apart; `גבורה` is also in AN III / NA (check their source words at the consistency pass).
6. **Uncertain readings left in italics or untranslated:** CH.2 (*nārika*), CH.13–14, CH.70 (*lītāt itrû*), FL i18′ (*šutātūt*), ST.7, 10/27 (*a da mu mu uš ši ib bi ir*), ST.16, 18 (*šumni izkur*), ST.21, ST.26′.
