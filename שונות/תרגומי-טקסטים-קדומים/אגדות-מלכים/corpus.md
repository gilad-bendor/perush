# Corpus: legends of kings → Hebrew (Gen 11 and Exod 2)

Read `../translation-guidelines.md` first, then this file, then the shared `renderings.md` (binding), then
`translator-brief--kings.md`, then the project's `project.md`.

## Goal (Round 2, run 6 of `../curated-translation-candidates.md`, 2026-10-21)
Translate, complete, two legends that the ancient Near East told about its kings and their beginnings:
1. **Enmerkar and the Lord of Aratta** (ETCSL 1.8.2.3; Sumerian; composed in the Ur III or early Old Babylonian
   period, copied in the OB schools of Nippur and Ur; 641 composite entries). Enmerkar, king of Uruk and "son of Utu",
   asks Inana to make the distant highland city of Aratta send gold, silver and lapis lazuli and its craftsmen to build
   her temple in Uruk and Enki's Abzu in Eridu. A messenger runs back and forth across seven mountain ranges; the two
   kings exchange threats and riddles (the grain carried in nets; a sceptre of no known material; a champion of no
   colour); the messenger's mouth grows "heavy", and Enmerkar invents writing on clay; Iškur's storm brings wild wheat
   to Aratta; the end (damaged) seems to settle the contest by trade. Inside the first message: **the "spell of
   Nudimmud"** (134–155), on a time when all peoples addressed Enlil "in one tongue" until Enki "placed a different
   tongue in their mouth".
2. **The Sargon Birth Legend** (Neo-Assyrian / Neo-Babylonian Akkadian; K 3401 + Sm 2118 and K 4470 from Nineveh, the
   school extract BM 47449 from Babylon; K 7249 not available to us). Sargon of Akkad (c. 2334–2279 BCE) tells in the
   first person how his mother, an *ēnetu* (high priestess?), conceived and bore him in secret, set him in a reed
   basket sealed with bitumen and cast him on the river; Aqqi the water-drawer drew him out, raised him as his son and
   made him a gardener; Ištar loved him and he became king; then the list of his deeds and a challenge to every later
   king. Probably composed under Sargon II of Assyria (722–705 BCE), the namesake who claimed the old king's heritage.

### Two purposes (guidelines §1)
- **Bible comparison.** For Enmerkar: **Gen 11:1–9** (one language, the confusion of tongues, the bricks, the building
  that reaches heaven, the scattering), Gen 10 (the tables of nations "by their tongues"; Erech/Uruk in Nimrod's
  kingdom, 10:10), Zeph 3:9 (`שָׂפָה בְרוּרָה`), Isa 11:6–9 and 65:25 (the peace among the beasts), **Exod 4:10**
  (`כְבַד פֶּה`), the messengers and their formulas (Gen 32:4–6; Num 20:14–17; Judg 11:12–28; 2 Kgs 18–19), the
  writing of the word (Exod 24:12; 32:15–16; Deut 27:8; Isa 8:1; Jer 36; Hab 2:2), the famine and the grain (Gen 41–
  47), the rain from the god (Deut 11:10–17), the contest of kings by riddles (1 Kgs 10:1–13; Judg 14). For Sargon:
  **Exod 2:1–10** (the Levite mother, the hidden child, the papyrus box daubed with bitumen and pitch, the river, the
  child drawn out, adopted as a son and named), Exod 1:22 (the river), 2:16–19 (Moses drawing water), the exposed or
  hidden child (Gen 21:15–19; 2 Kgs 11:2–3), Gen 10:8–12 (Nimrod and Akkad), Isa 20:1 (Sargon II), the boasts of kings
  (Isa 10:8–14; 37:24–25) and the challenge to successors (Deut 17:14–20 inverted).
- **The soul of the ancients**, equal in standing. Kingship and its legitimation (the king chosen in the goddess's
  heart; the king as son of the sun-god; a usurper who tells his own humble birth); rivalry and prestige between a
  river-plain city without stone or metal and a mountain city full of them (the economy of luxury goods; diplomacy by
  messenger and riddle); how they imagined the origin of writing (because a mouth grew tired) and of the many
  languages (a god's act; a golden age, or a hope?); the gods' household (Inana between two lords; Enki's wisdom;
  Nisaba's house of wisdom; Iškur's storm); the messenger's craft (memorizing word for word); the school that copied
  and loved these texts; a king's autobiography as a literary form (*narû*); the foundling, the gardener and the
  goddess's love.

## Shared conventions
- **`renderings.md` binds both projects**; it inherits the temple, lament and creation corpora's Sumerian renderings
  and the flood and law corpora's Akkadian ones (§0 there), and the rule "a Sumerian word takes the Hebrew of its
  Akkadian equivalent" — which here makes the two texts meet (Sum. *bal* "cross" and Akk. *nabalkutu* = `חָצָה`;
  *kur₂* and *nakāru* = `שִׁנָּה`; *eme* and *lišānu* = `לָשׁוֹן`).
- **Repeated lines get identical Hebrew** (command → delivery, the journey, the messenger's greeting, "say to him"):
  `kings_tools.py pars EA FROM TO` lists each line's earlier copies with their Hebrew; `verify` checks the fixed
  formulas and lists identical transliterations with different Hebrew.
- **The annotated file is the only source of the Hebrew**: `kings_tools.py build KEY` generates `translation.rtl.md`.
- **Parallels index:** per file in `project.md` (`### Parallels index (<KEY>)`, KEY = EA1 … EA4, SB; columns exactly
  `| Line | Source | Hebrew rendering | Bible | Tag | Comment |`), merged into `parallels-index.md` by
  `build_parallels_index.py` (Genesis first, then Exodus).
- **How this run was done (2026-10-21):** the cloud session had no sub-agent tool, so the coordinator worked through
  the translator chain itself, strictly in order (EA1 → EA2 → EA3 → EA4, then SB), each part drafted from its data file
  with the earlier parts' Hebrew in front of it, the proposals merged into `renderings.md` §9 after each part, and the
  three review passes done per part.

**Etana (project 3, added 2026-10-22, Round 3 run 2):** the childless king of Kiš, the eagle and the serpent, and the
ascent to heaven for the "plant of birth"; for Gen 11:4 (ascent and "name", without a tower), Gen 30:1, 14–23 (the
plea and the "disgrace"), Gen 37 (the pit), Gen 28:12–17 and 37–41 (dreams), Isa 14:12–15, Obad 1:4, Exod 19:4,
Deut 32:11, Ezek 17 (the eagle). Its renderings: `renderings.md` §10 (fixed before drafting; §10.6 what each part
fixed). It was translated by four sub-agent translators in sequence (ET2 → ET1 → ET3 → ET4; the coordinator merged
and pushed after each), one part at a time (quotas: guidelines §5.1).

**The Uruk cycle (projects 5–7, added 2026-11-07, Round 6 run 3):** with Enmerkar and the Lord of Aratta, the four
Enmerkar–Lugalbanda poems. Reading order: EA (the contest of Uruk and Aratta), LC (the march on Aratta; Lugalbanda's
sickness in the cave, his prayers to the sun, the evening star and the moon; the plants and water of life; fire and
bread; the hunt, the dream and the night offering), LZ (the Anzud bird, the destiny decreed and the gifts refused; the
siege, the lone messenger, Enmerkar's complaint to Inana, the tamarisk and the fish), ES (the boast about Inana's bed;
the sorcerer from Ḫamazu, the cow and the goat that speak, the old woman Saĝburu's five victories at the river; En-
suḫgir-ana yields). Renderings §12 (fixed before drafting; §12.6 what each part fixed). One translator per text,
resumed part by part (LC, LZ in two parts each), one agent at a time; tool keys LC, LZ, ES of `kings_tools.py`.

**Anzû, the Akkade cycle completed, the OB royal epics, Idrimi (projects 8–11, added 2026-11-14, Round 8 run 4):**
gods and kings at war. Anzû: the bird steals the Tablet of Destinies, god after god refuses, the mother sends her son,
the warrior wins back the order of the world and receives the names (Enūma eliš's model). The Akkade legends: Sargon's
speeches before battle and his march beyond the known world, the merchants' plea, the revolt of all the lands against
Narām-Sîn, his alliance with Erra. The royal epics: Zimrī-Lîm's war in Mari's own idiom, Bazi, Gulkišar. Idrimi: the
exile's first-person story. Renderings §13; brief `translator-brief--round8.md`. Reading order: AN (SB I–III, then OB
II–III and NA) → AK (CH, FL, LI, ST; RB, RA, AP, EN) → RE (ZL, BZ, GU) → ID.

## Tools (in this folder)
| Script | What |
|---|---|
| `kings_tools.py verify KEY [--strict]` | Entries against the base line list, transliteration = base text, one `>` per entry, reserved and gated strings (renderings §5), name traps (lines and headings), fixed formulas, translation = `>` lines, identical lines with different Hebrew. |
| `kings_tools.py build KEY` | The clean translation, verified. |
| `kings_tools.py grep KEY\|all '<regex>'`; `lines KEY FROM-TO`; `pars KEY FROM TO` | How a Hebrew string was used so far (all = + the lament, temple and creation corpora's Sumerian texts); the base lines; the earlier copies of a repeated line with their Hebrew. |
| `kings_tools.py conc EA [out]`; `data EA FROM TO out` | ETCSL ↔ ORACC concordance; a translator's line data (ETCSL, ORACC lemma + morph, ETCSL and Mittermayer at the line). |
| `kings_tools.py data ET FROM TO out`; `pars ET FROM TO` | Etana: a translator's line data (the eBL block of each line + its related lines in all six versions with the Hebrew already given); the related lines of a range. `verify ET` licenses the reserved words by the eBL lemma (RESERVED_ET); `build ET` prints the eBL label under each version's heading. |
| `kings_tools.py data LC\|LZ\|ES FROM TO out`; `skel KEY FILE FROM TO`; `pars KEY FROM TO` | The Uruk cycle: ETCSL line, ETCSL's lemmas, ORACC lemma + morph aligned by content (LC, LZ) or each verb's parses from the form index (ES), the near-identical lines of all four poems with their Hebrew; a skeleton; the related lines across the cycle. Gates licensed by the transliteration + ETCSL and ORACC lemmas (RESERVED_CY); FORMULAS_CY. |
| `kings_tools.py data\|pars\|fill\|verify\|build AN` | Anzû: the Etana / Cuthaean eBL machinery (six chapters, textno 10). |
| `kings_tools.py data\|pars\|fill\|verify\|build AK\|RE\|ID` | Projects 9–11: base `base.tsv` per text (SEAL transliteration + SEAL lemmas + SEAL's English line, or CDLI ATF), labels `<text>.<label>`; related lines across all twelve texts; gates RESERVED_R8 licensed by SEAL's lemmas or the folded ATF (renderings §13.4). |
| `build_parallels_index.py` | `parallels-index.md` from the per-file tables (projects 8–11: the parts' `project--<part>.md`). |
