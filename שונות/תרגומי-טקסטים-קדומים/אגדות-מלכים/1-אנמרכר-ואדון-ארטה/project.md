### Key findings (EA1)
- **The spell of Nudimmud (134–155) is told in the past, by its verb forms.** 146 *ḫe₂-en-na-da-ab-dug₄* is *ḫe₂-* +
  the ḫamṭu base *dug₄* (affirmative: "indeed they spoke"), not a precative (that would need marû *e*); 154
  *mi-ni-in-ĝar-ra* is ḫamṭu, *i₃-kur₂* has no future marker, 155 is a copula. ETCSL's "may they all address Enlil …
  Enki … shall change the speech … the speech of mankind is truly one" (Kramer's future reading) is not supported by
  the forms; Mittermayer's past reading ("until Enki placed a different tongue in their mouths, the tongue of
  mankind was one") is. So the Sumerian tells the same sequence as Gen 11:1–9 – one tongue, a god's act, many
  tongues – [equivalent], with three [divergence]s: the verb is *kur₂* "be different" (no `בָּלַל`, no confusion),
  the occasion is "the contest (*a-da*) of lords, princes and kings" (no building, no name, no fear of man), and the
  god is Enki, the god of wisdom, who sets the world in order (no punishment, no scattering). The one tongue is the
  tongue in which the four quarters "spoke to Enlil" – a political-cultic unity, not people speaking to each other.
  The future reading belongs with Zeph 3:9, not with Gen 11.
- **The word is *eme* "tongue" = `לָשׁוֹן`** – the word of Gen 10:5, 20, 31 (`לִלְשֹׁנֹתָם`), not `שָׂפָה` of Gen 11.
- **A world without beasts of prey and without fear (136–140)** ↔ Isa 11:6–8, 65:25; Lev 26:6; Ezek 34:25; Hos 2:20 –
  in the Bible a hope or a covenant blessing; and against Gen 9:2 (`וּמוֹרַאֲכֶם וְחִתְּכֶם`), the fear of man set on the
  animals after the flood.
- **The city-scattering threat (115–116)** "lest I make its people fly from the city like a wild dove from its tree"
  is the counterpart of `פֶּן נָפוּץ` (Gen 11:4) and `וַיָּפֶץ` (11:8): in Babel the builders fear it, here a king
  threatens it. The verb is *dal* "fly", not "scatter".
- **The bricks**: *šeg₁₂ kul-aba₄* "the brick(work) of Kulaba" is Uruk's pride and the measure by which Aratta
  failed Inana (15, 32) – the brick of the plain against the stone and metal of the mountains, where Gen 11:3 makes
  the brick a stand-in for stone.
- **ETCSL's "Before the land of Dilmun yet existed" (12)** and its "before … before" (16–19) import the "not yet"
  formula of creation myths (`טֶרֶם`, Gen 2:5): the Sumerian has plain negations ("was not", "were not doing").
- The house "come down from heaven" (43) is a [divergence] of `וּמִגְדָּל וְרֹאשׁוֹ בַשָּׁמַיִם` (the verb is
  restored; Mittermayer reads "reaching heaven").
- **The ancient mind:** kingship as the goddess's choice and love (the *en* in the *ĝipar*, the kohl for Dumuzi); the
  temple as a living, growing, shining thing; a "time before trade" imagined as the beginning of history; the economy
  of prestige (gold, silver, lapis lazuli, the donkey caravans); diplomacy by threat in the language of curses
  ("like a settlement Enki cursed"); a king who compares himself to a god ("as he destroyed – I too"); the many
  languages as part of Enki's world order.

### Proposals for the corpus renderings (EA1)
- `אז` for every *ud-ba* / *ud-bi-a*; `בימים ההם` for *ud re-a*.
- *kur* before a country name = `ארץ` (`ארץ דִּלְמוּן`, `ארץ אַנְשָׁן`, `ארץ מַרְטוּ`); otherwise `הר`.
- *nam-lu₂-ulu₃* absolute = `האנושות`; + a city name = `אנשי X`. *lu₂-u₁₈-lu* = `האדם`.
- *pa e₃ ak* = `הזהיר` (`מזהירה`, `יזהירו`); *galam* = `בכשרון … עיבד` (2026-10-23 harmonization: `בתחכום … עיבד`); *si sa₂* = `יישר`; *sikil* = `זך` / `זיכה`;
  *mu₂* = `הצמיח`; *de₆* = `נשא`; *tum₂* = `הוביל`; *tum₃* = `הביא`.
- *kug ki-in-dar-ra-gin₇* = `ככסף בסדק־הסלע`; *šeg₁₂ kul-aba₄* = `לְבֵנַת כולבה`; *saĝ-men … ĝal₂* = `כתר־הראש … חבש`.
- *saĝ keše₂* = `ראש קשר`; *na de₅* / *ĝizzal ak*: the 69–70 strings (`"אנמרכר, בוא! עצה אתן לך – את עצתי תקבל!`, since the 2026-10-23 harmonization `… – עצתי תיאחז!` /
  `דבר אומר לך – קשב לו תעשה!`) for 626–627.
- *kiĝ₂-gi₄-a inim zu zag-še tuku* = `שליח – יודע דבר, בעל כוח־עמידה –`.
- The threat strings (115–123) and the demand strings (124–135) are the template for 187–207 and 487–496; the Zubi
  strings (108–113) for 164–169 (past tense).
- *muš* `נחש`, *ĝiri₂* `עקרב`, *kir₄* `צבוע`, *ur-maḫ* `אריה`, *ur-gir₁₅* `כלב`, *ur-bar-ra* `זאב`; *gaba-šu-ĝar* `יריב`;
  *a-da* `תחרות`; *eme* `לשון`; *eme … kur₂* `לשון שונה`; *ka* `פה`.

### Key findings (EA2)
- **The delivery omits the spell.** The messenger delivers the threat and the demand word for word (187–207 =
  115–135), but of the spell of Nudimmud only its name: "say for me the spell of Nudimmud" (207). The text assumes
  the spell is known – or (Mittermayer) it is Enki's "instruction" whose fulfilment comes later (495–496). [absent]
- **The diplomatic formulas are the Bible's:** "Your father, my king, sent me to you" (176) ↔ `בִּנְךָ בֶן הֲדַד מֶלֶךְ
  אֲרָם שְׁלָחַנִי אֵלֶיךָ` (2 Kgs 8:9), `כֹּה אָמַר אָחִיךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל` (Num 20:14); the host's "What is it to me?"
  (178) ↔ `מַה לִּי וָלָךְ` (Judg 11:12; 2 Chr 35:21); "my king says thus" (186) ↔ `כֹּה אָמַר` (Gen 32:5;
  2 Kgs 18:19). The kinship terms ("father", "son") rank kings.
- **The messenger with the word put in his mouth** (296 *inim ka-na ba-an-sig₁₀*) ↔ `וְנָתַתִּי דְבָרַי בְּפִיו`
  (Deut 18:18), `הַדָּבָר אֲשֶׁר יָשִׂים אֱלֹהִים בְּפִי` (Num 22:38): the prophetic office is modelled on the royal
  messenger; here the human model itself, with its craft of exact repetition ("like his own mouth", 296, 302).
- **The good tidings** (215 *inim dug₃*) ↔ `וְאֶל בְּשׂוֹרָה טוֹבָה יָבוֹא` (2 Sam 18:27); the runner on the mountains ↔
  Isa 52:7.
- **Enmerkar born and suckled in the mountains, raised on Aratta's soil** (184, 210–212; Mittermayer reads "Uruk" in
  211): the king of the plain claims to be a son of the mountain he claims.
- **The contest replaces war (253–261):** the lord of Aratta proposes an *a-da-min₃* – the word of the Sumerian
  debate poems – and a proverb of two bulls. The condition of his submission is not defeat but Inana's choice (290:
  "if she casts Aratta from her hand like a dog chasing a carcass").
- **The ancient mind:** the messenger's journey by night and day (with the stars, with Utu); a king at a loss for an
  answer, staring at his feet, sleepless (236–239) – the human comedy of a court; the riddle as diplomacy; the
  piety of Aratta (libations of water and flour, prayer and supplication, 249–250); Inana as the goddess of both
  cities, whose "dance" is battle (289).

  `כִּי כָבֵד מִמְּךָ הַדָּבָר לֹא תוּכַל עֲשֹׂהוּ לְבַדֶּךָ` (Exod 18:18). Either way, the problem is solved by a
  mediator: Aaron as a mouth (Exod 4:14–16), Jethro's division of labour (Exod 18) – and here a lump of clay.
- **Exodus 4 in one place (our proposal):** the staff put in the envoy's hand (434 ↔ Exod 4:17), the sign (*ĝiškim* =
  *ittu*, the cognate of `אוֹת`, 496 ↔ Exod 4:8), the heavy mouth (501 ↔ Exod 4:10) – a cluster of motifs of the
  commissioned messenger.
- **The invention of the letter (500–506)** is an etiology told in the narrator's own voice to the audience:
  *ud-bi-ta* "formerly … (it) was not" / *i₃-ne-eš₂ … ud ne-a* "now, on this day, so it was" ↔ the Bible's narrator's
  asides `כִּי לַנָּבִיא הַיּוֹם יִקָּרֵא לְפָנִים הָרֹאֶה` (1 Sam 9:9) and `וְזֹאת לְפָנִים בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל` (Ruth 4:7)
  [equivalent] – there a custom that has vanished, here one that is born. The verb is *gub* "set up" (`הציב`), not
  *sar* "write"; ETCSL's "wrote" and "tablet" are the English's. Mittermayer reads *kiseb-gin₇* "like a seal" (503):
  seal on clay → word on clay → (in Sargon and Ur-Zababa) the envelope.
- **"The word was a peg" (540)** – ETCSL's *gag* "nail, peg" (the emic name of cuneiform, as Modern Hebrew `כתב
  היתדות`) is contested by Mittermayer's collation (*u₄-ba du₁₁-ga-ni-am₃*): no ancient source calls the script
  "nails". Kept from the base, flagged (EA4.3).
- **Iškur's storm and the wheat that grew by itself (542–553)** ↔ the storm theophany that brings rain (Judg 5:4; Ps
  29:3; 18:8; 98:8) and `סְפִיחַ` (Lev 25:5) [equivalent]: the god intervenes at the moment of the lord of Aratta's
  confusion, and the riddle-war is not decided by wit.
- **A chosen people (566–568):** the people of Aratta "were separated from all people", "brought out from the people
  by Dumuzi" ↔ `וָאַבְדִּל אֶתְכֶם מִן הָעַמִּים לִהְיוֹת לִי` (Lev 20:26), `לָקַחַת לוֹ גוֹי מִקֶּרֶב גּוֹי` (Deut
  4:34) [equivalent] – the election by a god of love (Dumuzi), not of covenant.
- **The flood as the watershed of history (571–576):** "those who stood before the flood", "after the flood had
  swept over" (the Sumerian King List's formula) ↔ `אַחַר הַמַּבּוּל` (Gen 10:32), the survivors (Gen 7:23); the
  water of life sprinkled on them (Num 19:17); and the surprise – Aratta once held Sumer's neck in the yoke (Jer 27:8).
- **The end is trade, not conquest (611–625):** gold, silver and lapis prepared "for exchange" (*bal ak*), the
  *iškaru* quota (Exod 5:8), heaped for Inana in the courtyard of E-ana – the demand of 484–485 fulfilled in Uruk; and
  Inana seats Enmerkar beside her on the dais (594 ↔ Ps 45:10): the sacred marriage is the kingship.
- **The ancient mind:** a message too long for memory; a king who invents a medium because his envoy is overloaded;
  a reader who has never seen writing staring at the clay; the god of rain as the answer to a technological
  triumph; a mountain people who trace their election to Dumuzi and their survival to the flood; a dispute between
  cities ended by a trade agreement.
