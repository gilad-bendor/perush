## Key findings
- **The ancient mind.** The three poems show how a king of the 18th–16th centuries wanted to be sung. Zimrī-Lîm is named by the gods "in the womb", yet he tells his soldiers that "a womb built you, a mother like yours bore me" (ii22–23). He drinks from waterskins with the infantry, shares their trouble, hunts to feed them, and walks before them "like a standard" (iii9–16). He rules with counsel: he consults the *sugāgū* and promises to do "as your counsel" (ii31–36); his *merḫûm* Ašmad answers in a herdsman's images, Subartu "scattered like sheep at pasture" with provisions waiting (ii39–44). Before battle he asks the gods "who had not been called on" and gets an answer "yes"; the *āpilum*, Mari's prophet, gives a sign; the *bārû*s are summoned and the king purifies himself; then he asks Šamaš "Will I bind my enemies today?" (iii27–43). Mercy is his "taboo" (iii2). After the victory he does not boast: he enters before Dagan at Terqa and *asks* "life, prosperity and strength" (iv7–11). The Song of Bazi turns an old king of Mari (the King List's Bazi) into a goat-god of the Bišri mountain: he asks his father where to put his shrine, for all the daises are taken; he strikes the mountain and water comes out; his house, with lapis bricks and serpent doorposts, is a gate of death where "half of the waters are life, half death" (29a), and the song was sung by people going up to the mountain (58–59). The Gulkišar epic gives the Sealand's side against Samsu-ditāna: a king who speaks as Adad, Errakal and Girra, and threatens to crush "eggs" and gather "hatchlings and old men" (10′–11′); his goddess promises him a statue "in the house of stelae" (19).
- **The Bible.** The closest contacts are the royal psalms: the king who "asks life" of his god after victory (Ps 21:5; against Solomon in 1 Kgs 3:11), the god at the king's right (Ps 110:5), the poured-out *imtu* / `חֵמָה` (Ps 79:6; Jer 25:15 with `הִשְׁקָה`), the oracle question and answer (1 Sam 23:2; 30:8), and the prophet as "answerer" (1 Sam 28:6). Deut 7:2's `וְלֹא תְחָנֵּם` has a real cognate in *enēnu* (ZL iii2). Deut 22:6's eggs and hatchlings meet GU 10′–11′ as their exact opposite. The struck mountain giving water (BZ 23–24) and the house from which water goes out (Ezek 47:1) are [equivalent]s; the two waters of life and death (BZ 29a) meet Deut 30:19 as an image, not a text.
- **Readings against SEAL's English:** *āpilum* is Mari's prophet, not "the diviner" (ZL iii35); *kunnum* "established", not "the just one" (ii29–30; `צדיק` would import a moral claim); *šūšerānim* "set right for me", not "liberate" (ii26); *puršumu* "old men", not "piglets" (GU 11′); *kizzu* "he-goat", not "ram" (BZ 2, 35).

## Open decisions
1. **ZL i13, 15 *lītellil*** – *elēlu* Gt "be pure" (SEAL's lemma) or a denominative of *etellu* "pre-eminent" (SEAL's "excel")? Rendered `*יתנשא*`.
2. **ZL iii6 *ballat* = `בלול` [cognate of `בָּלַל`, guidelines §6.7]** – the corpus "never" list has Gen 11's `בָּלַל`; Akkadian *balālu* "mix (a liquid)" is the very cognate, in its Lev 2:5 sense. The line passes only because `בלול` is missing from the gate's word list (see Tool, T1). The lead should decide whether *balālu* licenses `בלל`.
3. **ayyābu vs nakru** – GU uses both; *ayyābu* is the cognate of `אוֹיֵב`, which the corpus gave to *nakru* (CL). Both rendered `אויב` here; by the cognate rule *ayyābu* keeps `אויב` and *nakru* would need another word – for the lead.
4. **zību vs barbaru** – *zību* is the cognate of `זְאֵב`; rendered `תַּן` (GU.17) with *barbaru* `זְאֵב`. By the cognate rule it should be the reverse; left for the lead.
5. ***saḫāru* split by sense** – `פָּנָה` "turn" (AK1; ZL ii40, iii16) / `סָבַב` "go around, surround" (ZL ii10, BZ 20); AN gave `סָבַב` to *lawû*.
6. ***maḫāru* split by sense** – `קִבֵּל` "receive" (AK1) / `עָמַד מוּל` "confront" (GU rev. 3, 24).
7. ***perdu*** transcribed `*פֶּרְדּוּם*` (ZL ii2) because AN III gave `פרדה` to *parû*; ***šiḫāmu*** `*שִׁיחָמִים*`, ***rusû*** `*רוּסוּ*`, ***qāqullu*** `קַקּוּלּוּ` transcribed.
