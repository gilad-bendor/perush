## Why this text (guidelines §1, both purposes)
- **Bible:** Proverbs above all – the father to the son (Prov 1:8; 4:1; 23:22); honour and fear of parents (Ex 20:11 and Lev 19:3: *kubbutu*, *palāḫu*); strong drink (Prov 20:1; 23:20–21, 29–35); bad company (Prov 1:10–15; 13:20; 22:24–25); the guarded mouth (Prov 13:3; 21:23; Ps 141:3); quarrels (Prov 17:14; 26:17, 20–21); the enemy (Prov 24:17; 25:21–22; Ex 23:4–5; 1 Sam 24:18); the weak and the poor (Prov 14:31; 19:17; 22:22); the strange woman (Prov 5–7; 23:27); the slave girl who rules (Prov 30:21–23); the official and the king (Prov 25:6–7; Qoh 8:2–4; 10:4); slander (Lev 19:16; Prov 11:13; 20:19); the promise kept (Deut 23:22–24; Num 30:3; Qoh 5:3–4); "an abomination to the god" (Prov 6:16–19; 11:1, 20; 12:22); "reverence begets favour, prayer absolves" (Prov 1:7; 10:27; 15:8); long life (A+91: Ex 20:11, Deut 5:15, 25:15).
- **The ancient mind:** the household gods and the father and mother as gods (10); the protective spirit that hands a drunkard to demons (19); public life (the crowd, the witness, the dispute); the women of the cult (A+23–A+25); service at court (the prince's seal and treasury); the scribal school ("read from the tablet", A+63, A+85, A+98); the daily duties of piety (offerings, incense, freewill gifts); the colophon names an author or copyist and his father.

- **Drink and the spirit (15–21):** *šikaru* `שֵׁכָר` [cognate]; *kurunnu* `סובא` (SH); *lapānu* `יִדַּל`; *šēdu* `רוּחַ־מָגֵן`, *gallû* `גַּלּוּ`; **19 *ana gallê iturraššu* = `לְגַלּוּ תֵּהָפֵךְ לו`** – G of *târu* "turn into" (as Ludlul I 85 `לגַלּוּ נהפך רֵעִי`), against Foster's "turn him over to demons" (that would need D) and against the brief's "hands the drunkard to the *gallû*". `נהפך` follows Ludlul I 85 (*ana X târu*), `שוב` elsewhere.

### Open decisions for the user
1. **10 "father and mother are the household gods"**: translated as eBL restores; BabLB2's variant *ša āšib [bīti]* ("who dwell in the house") is only in the note.
2. **19**: `לְגַלּוּ תֵּהָפֵךְ לו` (the spirit becomes the demon) vs Foster's "turns him over to the demons". The morphology supports ours; if the coordinator prefers to keep both readings visible, the note has them.
3. **One Hebrew for two verbs:** *atāru* D (A+87 *uttar*) and *waṣābu* (A+90 *uṣṣab*) are both `הוסיף` – inherited (SH 100 and Theodicy 52). Here they stand three lines apart, and A+90 `שלום יוסיף` happens to match Prov 3:2 `וְשָׁלוֹם יוֹסִיפוּ לָךְ` exactly. Alternative for *waṣābu*: `ירבה` (would collide with *mâdu*). Kept; for the coordinator.
4. **One Hebrew for two verbs (inherited):** *enû* "revoke" and *šanû* "change / repeat" are both `שנה` (Theodicy 2, 55; Ludlul). A+94 `אל תשנה` and A+44 `אשנה` stand ten lines apart; `המיר` (Ps 15:4 `וְלֹא יָמִר`) would separate them and show the Psalm parallel.
5. **The root `משל`:** *mašālu* "be like" (7 `משול`) and *bêlu* "rule" (A+18, A+22 `משל`) – the Hebrew root has both senses, the Akkadian two roots; inherited from Ludlul / SH.
6. **The root `בעל`:** *aḫāzu* "marry" (A+23 `תבעל`, A+31 `בועלה`), *mutu* "husband" (A+23 `בעליה`) and *bēlu* "owner" (A+46–A+47, A+101) all use `בעל`. Alternative for *aḫāzu*: the law corpus's `נשא` (blocked here by *našû*, A+26 in the same section).
7. **A+104 the colophon:** both names have the divine determinative; Išme-karāb is known as a god. Human scribe and father, or gods? Translated neutrally.
8. **A+16 *šubši*** with the creation corpus's `הֵבִיא לִכְלַל הֲוָיָה`: heavy in a maxim; the alternative is `צור` / `עשה`, which would break the cross-corpus rule for *šubšû*.
9. **Uncertain lemmas** rendered in italics: 40 *abru* (`*איתנה*` vs "brushwood"), 46 *šutaʾʾuru*, A+8 *ṣurru*, A+9 *tuktanniš*, A+74 *uṣṣû* (`*תְּשַׁלַּח*`), A+99–A+101 *muštakṣib* (`מְגָרֵעַ?`).
10. **A+51 *erbu*** `הכנסה` (income) – eBL's lemma; Foster reads "your visit" (*erēbu*). The Hebrew keeps "income".

### The text on its own terms
- **The sage and the school (1–5, A+5, A+63, A+85, A+98):** the counsels are "Ea's command" (4), spoken by an *ummânu* to his "son"; the "big brother" of A+5 is the senior pupil; the formula "on the basis of your learning, look in the tablet" introduces quoted sayings. The many school tablets and two ancient quotations (a letter from Nineveh, A+86–A+91; BabNBQuo1, A+102) show the text in use for centuries.
- **Parents as household gods (8–11):** the parents are feared (*palāḫu*, the word of religious awe), they bless, their prayers protect, and they are "the gods of the house" – inside the household what the gods are outside, and after death the family dead fed by their children.
- **The protective spirit turns (19):** the drunkard's *šēdu* becomes a *gallû* to him – the same divine power protects or destroys according to a man's conduct (cf. Ludlul I 85: the friend turned into a *gallû*).
- **Public life is a trap (32–41):** the assembly, the quarrel and the lawsuit; being present makes a man a witness and binds him to someone else's case; the quarrel is a hunter's pit behind a screen; the advice is to "slip away" before it starts, and to put it out like a fire if it is yours.
- **Good for evil, for the gods' sake (42–48):** requite the evildoer with favour, keep justice toward the wicked, let your liver shine toward your enemy – "this is pleasing to the gods"; the opposite (binding evil, returning evil) is the *ikkibu* of Marduk and Ninurta. *ikkibu*, once a ritual taboo, has become a moral category.
- **Šamaš the requiter (A+7–A+16, A+73):** kindness to the weak "is good upon Šamaš" and he "requites" with favour; the slanderer has "arrears" in Šamaš's accounts – sin as an unpaid debt, the god of justice as creditor.
- **The household and its women (A+17–A+31):** the slave girl who becomes "heavy" in the house; the wife's rightful rule over the husband's "way"; the women attached to Ištar's cult (*ḫarimtu*, *ištarītu* "spoken for to a god", *kulmašītu*) are not to be married – not because they are impure, but because they lack the reverence and submission a household needs and will squander it. A scribal "variant" (*šanîš*, A+31) preserved beside the main line.
- **The court (A+32–A+57):** the prince's seal on the servant's neck, the treasury open to him alone, the temptation to "lift the eye"; the secret will be uncovered; the prince's bright face turns to anger, and the courtier's punishment is "a word forever" (gossip). The official: honour the predecessor, come with a gift every day, do not abuse the power "to raise and to lower" (the phrase of Marduk's investiture in Enūma eliš).
- **Speech (22–31, A+70–A+77, A+92–A+103):** speech is a possession one "acquires" and cannot get rid of (A+43, A+76); "do not say your liver's words even alone"; the slanderer "eats pieces" of his victim (the idiom passed into Daniel's Aramaic).
- **The daily religion (A+78–A+84):** blessing the personal god every day, sacrifice, the "utterance of the mouth", incense, freewill offering, supplication and "stroking the nose" – and a frank do-ut-des: for a grain, a talent.
- **Piety's rewards (A+86–A+91):** reverence begets favour, sacrifice adds life, supplication "loosens" guilt (sin as a bond), the god-fearer prospers, the goddess-fearer gains well-being, the fearer of the Anunnakkū lengthens his days – the whole divine world, from the personal god to the great gods.
- **Promises and oaths (A+92–A+103):** a word given must be delivered; the elliptic oath "if you promised – (I swear) you will not (fail to) give!"; the one wronged by secret schemes can send against the schemer an oath-curse "that cannot be loosened"; "not giving" is Marduk's abomination – as in the exorcist's confession lists of Šurpu.
- **The colophon (A+104–A+105):** Išme-karāb ("he heard the prayer") son of Enlil-banda – both written with the divine determinative; the text ends where it began, with Ea.

### Proposals for the shared renderings

### Lessons for the guidelines
- **A rank-3 translation can reverse a line's meaning, not only manufacture an echo:** CW 48 "That is *not* acceptable to the gods" has no negation on the tablets; check every negation against the ATF.
- **A stem decides the theology:** CW 19's G *târu ana* ("the spirit becomes the demon") vs a D reading ("hands him over"); parse the stem before believing the English (and the brief).
- **A shared formula changes with its number:** the pre-set `נשא עיניים` became `עינך` where the Akkadian has the singular.
