## Why this text (guidelines §1, both purposes)
- **Bible:** Qohelet above all (2:1–11: the experiments with pleasure and work; 1:2; 2:16–17 the skulls of the great and lowly – the same end; 3:19–20; 6:12 `מִי יוֹדֵעַ מַה טּוֹב לָאָדָם`; 7:26 the woman "more bitter than death, a snare"; 9:2 one fate to the one who sacrifices and the one who does not; 1:4); Job 3 and Jer 20:14–18 (better not to be); Prov 23:27 (the woman a deep pit `שׁוּחָה עֲמֻקָּה`); lending (Ex 22:24; Prov 22:7); sacrifice and the god trained like a dog (Ps 50:8–13; Isa 1:11; Mal 1); `הִנֵּנִי` (Gen 22:1, 11; Isa 6:8) for *annû*; "who is so tall as to reach heaven" (Deut 30:12–13; Prov 30:4); the master and slave (Prov 30:22; Qoh 10:7).
- **The ancient mind:** a satire, a dialogue, or a philosophical poem? – the servant's two answers to every wish; the life of a Babylonian gentleman (palace, chariot, dinner, hunting, house, love, sacrifice, lending, public works); "he who eats well is his own god"; the god trained to follow like a dog; Marduk's basket; the ruin-heaps and the skulls; the joke at the end.
