### Decisions
- **The frame (renderings §11.4), checked against the transliteration of 1–6 (★): kept as pre-set.** *arad* is the vocative (`עֶבֶד`); *mitanguranni* is *magāru* Gtn imperative + ventive + 1cs, "keep complying with me" – `הִתְרַצֵּה לִי` (the corpus has *magāru* = `רצה`: Ludlul I 69, II 33); *annû* is the word *annu* "yes" (eBL lemmatizes it as *annû* I "this": a lemma slip) – `הֵן`, as Ludlul's *anna*; *ē* "no!" + *ul* + present = `לֹא, עֶבֶד, אני – … לא X!` (the *-ma* on the object is shown by a dash); *lā* + present (prohibitive) = `אַל תX, אדוני, אַל תX!`; the imperative answer `X, אדוני, X!`. One deviation in form only: the refusal line has a dash after `אני` (`"לא, עֶבֶד, אני – אל ההיכל לא אדהר!"`), as the Akkadian fronts the emphasized object with *-ma*. Where the infinitive is the object (14 *patānum-ma ul apattan*) the Hebrew has the infinitive absolute (`סָעוֹד לא אֶסְעַד`), as Machinist notes.
- **Vocalization of the frame:** the refrain line `"עֶבֶד, הִתְרַצֵּה לִי!" "הֵן, אֲדוֹנִי, הֵן!"` is vocalized identically in all eleven copies; in the other frame lines only `עֶבֶד` is vocalized (unvocalized it reads "worked"); `אדוני` plain. Quotation marks mark the change of speaker (they open and close where eBL's T does); the houseless man's speech (34–36) is a quotation inside a quotation (single quotes).
- **The open country (17–28), the inversion word by word:** *muttapraššidu* (man, bird) = `נָד` (vocalized); *dayyālu* (dog) = `סובב` (*dâlu* = `סובב`, Gilgameš); *murtappidu* (wild ass) = `משוטט` (*rapādu*, Gilgameš); *akkannu* = `ערוד`. 25–28 repeat the four subjects of 19–22 in the same Hebrew (`האדם הנָּד`, `הכלב הסובב`, `*העורב* הנָּד`, `הערוד המשוטט`). `נודד` was not available (SH 67 *ṣāʾidu*); the Gilgameš ban on `נָד` concerns *rapādu*, not *muttapraššidu* "fugitive".
- **New words fixed here** (all grepped first): *mašāru* (drive a chariot) `דהר` (`נהג` is *warû*, CW; `רכב` *rakābu*, Gilgameš); *dekû* "raise, fetch" `הרם` (`הביא` *wabālu*, `הגיש` *ṭeḫû*; Ludlul's `העיר` is the sense "rouse"); *šīšir* `לֵךְ יָשָׁר` (keeps *ešēru* = `ישר`); *patānu* / *patnu* `סעד` / `סעודה` (collision, see open decisions); *ḫaḫḫūru* `*עורב*` (italic, open); *qinnu* / *qanānu* `קן` / `קנן` (Gilgameš); *nawû* `נווה` [cognate], *narbāṣu* `מרבץ` [cognate]; *samītu* `תחתית (החומה)`; *qayyalu* `קשוב`; *šinipu* `שני שלישים`; *qâdu* `הצית`, *kâšu* I "linger" `התמהמה`; *bēl dabābi* `בעל־דברים` (the idiom of Ex 24:14, chosen as a unit); *sakātu* `שתק`; *pâ epēšu* `פתח את הפה` (Gilgameš's speech formula); *sartu* `מרמה` (`lemma 'sartu'`: no earlier rendering in the corpus; *sarrātu* "lies" stays `כזבים`); *kâṣu* `מופשט־עור`; *napālu* D (eyes) `ניקר`; *qūlu* `דּוּמִיָּה`; *ḫirītu* `חפירה` (*ḫerû* = `חפר`, Gilgameš); *šēlu* `מושחז`; *qīptu* "credit" `פיקדון` (Ludlul I 61's `פקודה` is the sense "office": one Hebrew root, two nouns by sense); *ummânu* "capital, loan" `מַשָּׁאָה` (always vocalized; same root as SH 118's `נושה` for *ummânu* "creditor"); *ḫalāpu* Gt (clothes) `התעטף` (Ludlul's `מכורבל` by sense); *qappatu* `סלסלה` (`סל` is *pisannu*, Ludlul III 104; `קופה` *quppu*; `טנא` rejected: Deut 26); *labīru* `עתיק`; *arkû* / *pānû* (pl.) `אחרונים` / `ראשונים`; *tikku* `מפרקת` (a deviation from Gilgameš `עורף`, which this corpus has for *labānu*, Ludlul II 61); *nasāku* `זרק` (Gilgameš, Etana; `השליך` is *nadû*); *gamāru* D "encompass" `הקיף` (by sense; `גמר` elsewhere, 3 Š `הגמיר`); *Latarak* `לָטַרָךְ`; *lū* (86) `לוּ` [cognate].
- **Copied from the finished texts of this run:** *usātu* `עזרה`, *bēl usāti* / *bēl lemutti* `בעל עזרה` / `בעל רעה` (SH 122, CW A+16); *niqû* `זבח`; *lillu* `כסיל`; *šuttatu* `שוחה` (CW 39, Etana); *būrtu* `בור` (§11.8); *uṭṭatu* `גרגיר` (CW A+83); *ḫubullu* `חוב`; *rubû* `נשיא` (SH); *nadānu* `נתן`; *ḫalāqu* D `איבד` (SH 104); *pāna wabālu* `נשא פנים` (§11.10); *erēšu* `שאל` (CW A+13).
- **Parenthesized words of N** (*umma*, *(kīmi)* in 47, 71, *(u)* in 22, *(ša)* in 41) are not translated (§10.1). *kīmi* is `אם כך` in the answers (40, 63) and `[לאמור]` in 36 (introducing a quotation).
- **Brackets** follow the ATF of all manuscripts, so they sometimes differ from N's: 63 *[idin]*, 65, 66 are preserved in BabaNB1 / AššNA1 and are not bracketed.
- **No `⟨ ⟩` line for 43:** eBL's T adds "<Do not do it master, do not do it>" – the servant's prohibitive line is missing in every manuscript; the Hebrew adds nothing and the note says so.

### Open decisions for the user
1. ***patānu* "dine" = `סעד`** collides with Ludlul I 10, 12 (*kâšu* D "sustain" = `סועד`). Alternatives: `לחם` (`לִלְחוֹם אֶת-מוֹשֵׁל`, Prov 23:1 – the exact situation, but unvocalized it reads "fight"); `אכל ארוחה` (two words in a refrain; *akālu* = `אכל` is in 16). Kept `סעד`.
2. ***ḫaḫḫūru* = `*עורב*`** (italic): eBL's lemma "raven", Machinist "hunting falcon", eBL's T "bird". `עורב` is the flood corpus's word for *āribu* (Gen 8:7), so two Akkadian words share it; alternatives: transliterate `חַחּוּרוּ`, or a hawk word (`נֵץ`).
3. ***ummânu* "loan" = `מַשָּׁאָה`** against §11.2's *ummânu* = `נושה` (the creditor): the sense here is the money given (object of *nadānu*). Same root kept.
4. ***tikku* = `מפרקת`** (a deviation from Gilgameš `עורף`): this corpus has `עורף` for *labānu*.
5. ***muttapraššidu* = `נָד`**: if the user prefers to keep `נד` / `נודד` away from every Cain echo (as Gilgameš does for *rapādu*), the alternative is `הנמלט` (but `נמלט` is the law corpus's *ḫalāqu* "flee").
6. **77 *arkûti u pānûti*:** rendered literally `אחרונים … ראשונים` (later / earlier), which keeps both readings (generations; low / high rank) and shows Qoh 1:11; eBL's "the lowly and great" chooses the second.
7. **13 *šēbi patni ilšu*:** three readings (eBL "he who eats well is his own god"; Machinist "who is sated with the meal of his god"; Mitto "his god too is sated"); the Hebrew follows eBL with `(הוא)` added.

### The text on its own terms
- **The gentleman's life** (all stanzas): chariot and palace (2–9), the regular dinner with hand-washing (11–13), hunting in the steppe with a bird (18–22), building a house and keeping the father's household (30–38), the lawcourt (a+6–a+7, 35), love (47–52), sacrifice to the personal god (54–61), lending barley at interest (63–69), public works "for the land" (71–73): an inventory of what an urban Babylonian of standing did and valued.
- **The court is grace and danger at once** (3–9): the prince "lifts your face", and the same prince sends you on campaign by a road you do not know (8 = a stock phrase: Gilgameš III, a Nimrud proverb tablet).
- **Šamaš at the table** (13): the god of justice "goes with" washed hands; and in the same line the sated man is "his own god" – the personal god of Ludlul and the Theodicy reduced to a full stomach.
- **The steppe** (17–28): man, dog, bird and wild ass are each free and fed – and each mad, toothless, homeless, condemned to the open; the same facts, two speeches. The city-dweller's view of nomads: the roamer's "judgement changes" (25).
- **The houseless man** (33–36) is "two-thirds a fool" – a parody of Gilgameš's "two-thirds god" – and pleads with his creditor in the servant's own formula ("Look, master, look!").
- **Silence** (a+1–a+7): the wisdom virtue of the closed mouth (cf. CW) against the court's reality – who does not speak loses.
- **Crime and punishment** (44–45): death, flaying, blinding, arrest, prison – the Assyrian state's range of punishments, given as a practical, not a moral, argument.
- **Love** (48–52): one of the few Akkadian lines on love as the cure for grief (*qūlu u nissatu*), set beside the stock misogyny of wisdom literature (pit, trap, iron dagger).
- **Religion as commerce** (56–61): sacrifice is credit (*qīptu*) stored with the god; and the god, fed, is trained "like a dog" to follow and to demand rites and Latarak figurines (apotropaic statuettes buried in houses). Both answers assume the same contract.
- **Lending** (63–69): grain loans at interest (barley, ŠE.BAR), the lender's dream of capital that stays while interest grows, and his nightmare of debtors who eat the grain and curse him. "Lending is like love, getting back is like childbirth" is a proverb of the marketplace.
- **Marduk's basket** (73): good deeds are "placed" in the god's palm-leaf basket – stored, it seems, like produce or a deposit; the servant then shows the skulls on the ruin-heaps that cannot tell good from evil (76–78).
- **The literary self-awareness**: 76 quotes the end of Gilgameš ("go up on the wall … and walk around") and 83–84 the saying from Gilgameš and Huwawa; the poem answers the heroic tradition's "therefore let me make a name" with "therefore it is good to die".
- **The servant's two answers to every wish** are the poem's form and its philosophy: every act has a good and a bad description, and speech can always supply both. Whether this is satire (a foolish master, a servile slave who in the end is the only one who knows the master cannot survive without him) or a philosophical poem on the emptiness of all action – the ending (85–86) keeps both open.
- **Scribal touches**: BabaNB1 writes "my lord" with the sign group of the god Bēl ({d}+EN); AššNA1 writes *inanna* "now" with the divine determinative (*i-{d}nanna*); the repeated word written MIN ("ditto") in a+3, 51, 66.

### Proposals for the shared renderings
- §11.4, the frame as drafted: refusal `"לא, עֶבֶד, אני – <object> לא <verb>!"` (with the dash); the refrain vocalized identically every time; `עֶבֶד` always vocalized in the frame.
- *mašāru* `דהר`; *dekû* "fetch, raise" `הרם` (beside Ludlul's `העיר` "rouse"); *patānu* / *patnu* `סעד` / `סעודה` (open: collision with *kâšu* D); *muttapraššidu* `נָד` (vocalized); *dayyālu* `סובב`; *ḫaḫḫūru* `*עורב*` (open); *nawû* `נווה` [cognate], *narbāṣu* `מרבץ` [cognate]; *qayyalu* `קשוב`; *bēl dabābi* `בעל־דברים`; *sakātu* `שתק`; *kâṣu* `מופשט־עור`; *napālu* D (eyes) `ניקר`; *qūlu* `דומייה`; *ḫirītu* `חפירה`; *qīptu* "credit" `פיקדון`; *ummânu* "capital, loan" `מַשָּׁאָה` (vocalized); *qappatu* `סלסלה`; *tīlu* `תל`, *gulgullu* `גולגולת` [cognates]; *labīru* `עתיק`; *tikku* `מפרקת`; *nasāku* `זרק` (as Gilgameš / Etana); *Latarak* `לָטַרָךְ`.
- Always vocalize (add to §8): `מַשָּׁאָה`, `נָד`, `נַחְלָה` (stative "grievous"; unvocalized "inheritance"), `אֵלוֹ` (already), `עֶבֶד` in dialogue frames.

### Lessons for the guidelines
- **A modern translation's social gloss can hide a lexical parallel**: eBL's "the lowly and great" (77) for *arkûti u pānûti* "the later and the earlier" hides Qoh 1:11's `רִאשֹׁנִים … אַחֲרֹנִים`. Check pairs of adjectives against the lemmas, not the English.
- **Look for the Sumerian source of an Akkadian saying in ETCSL** (the bulk zip is local): DP 83–84 is Gilgameš and Huwawa A 28–29, and the neighbouring lines there (neck, wall, corpses in the river) explain the sequence of DP 76–84.
- **eBL's `Parallels:` field links a line to a non-parallel one** (DP 6 → 23 instead of 24): such reported differences are justified in the apparatus, not "fixed".
