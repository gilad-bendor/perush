## Scope
The whole hymn as eBL edits it: **200 labels (1–200)**, one file `annotated-SH.rtl.md`. Sections (eBL gives none): the translator decides them from the content (e.g. 1–20 the light over the world; 21–52 the sun's course and rule above and below; 53–56 the diviners kneel; 57–82 the god who saves: the just, the traveller, the merchant, the prisoner, the sick; 83–100 the net for the wicked and the judge; 101–127 the honest and the cheating trader, lender, weigher; 128–166 who prays to Šamaš and how he answers; 167–175 the strangers and the creatures of the sea; 176–200 the closing epithets and blessings).

## Why this text (guidelines §1, both purposes)
- **Bible:** the weights and measures laws (Lev 19:35–36; Deut 25:13–16 – *aban kīsi* ~ `בְּכִיסְךָ אֶבֶן וָאָבֶן`, *sūtu* ~ `סְאָה`, and the refrain "Šamaš is pleased, he adds life" beside Deut 25:15); Prov 11:1, 16:11, 20:10, 23; Amos 8:5; Mic 6:10–11; Hos 12:8; the bribe (Ex 23:8; Deut 16:19; Ps 15:5); interest (Ex 22:24; Lev 25:36–37; Deut 23:20–21; Ps 15:5; Ezek 18:8); the judge (Deut 1:16–17; 16:18–20); the adulterer (88: Ex 20:13 in the repository's numbering; Prov 6:29); the sun hymn (Ps 19:2–7, 104; Mal 3:20 `שֶׁמֶשׁ צְדָקָה וּמַרְפֵּא בִּכְנָפֶיהָ`; Job 38:12–15: the dawn exposes the wicked); the god who sees everything (Ps 139:7–12; Prov 15:3; Job 34:21–22); those who cry out (Ps 107: travellers, prisoners, the sick, seafarers); the shepherd (Ps 23; Ezek 34); the net of the god (Ezek 12:13; 17:20; Hos 7:12; Ps 9:16).
- **The ancient mind:** the sun god as judge and as the god of divination (53–55, 129, 149–155: the diviner's bowl, the dream interpreter); who prayed to him (the shepherd, the merchant, the fisherman, the robber, the wandering dead); the ethics of trade (the two weights; lending by different standards); the twentieth of the month as his feast (156–158); beer for the god; the gates and bolts of heaven (182–183); Aya, the bride, telling him to rest (200).
