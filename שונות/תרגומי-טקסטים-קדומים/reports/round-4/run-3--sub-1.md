**For the ancient mind**
- A land surveyor's conscience: he measures, registers, sets boundary stones, records the new Nile islands in the
  king's name, calibrates the Udjat measure; his sins are professional and so is the punishment (the cheat's son is not
  enrolled as a scribe, 17.14; one stolen furrow shortens your life; the Moon lassoes the man who "hunts" a field).
- The two men: the "hot" man whose tree is floated away and burned, the "silent" man whose tree flourishes in its own
  garden; burial is the measure of a life. Silence is power (the real crocodile does not roar; whoever "knows how to
  sleep" wins).
- Measures as divine eyes: the Udjat is Horus's healed eye, the oipe the Eye of Re; Thoth's baboon heart is the plummet
  of every market balance – the judgement of the dead in every transaction.
- Taboo becomes ethics (bw.t, a cult taboo, becomes the god's horror of lies); "no one is sinless", in a culture whose
  dead say "I have not sinned" forty-two times.
- Life as a Nile voyage: the tongue is the steering oar, the Lord of All the pilot; the ferry is a moral place (take the
  fare from the rich, let the poor cross free).
- Wealth behaves like a living thing: it does not sleep at your house, it sinks into the Duat or grows wings.
- The book speaks about itself (it pleasures, teaches, purifies); tTurin is a school tablet with "day 4", "day 8" in
  its margins – a pupil's daily stint; one copyist, Senu son of Pamiu ("the cat"), handed down the only complete text.

## Lessons for the guidelines, sources and tools
- **Tools:** (1) An AED/TLA alternative-analysis separator can span several words: a converter must not print it as a
  lacuna; check every `[…]` inside a line against the TLA's German before the first line (tools.md, `egypt_tools.py`).
  (2) A **Hebrew → lemma-ids report** (from the פירוק bullets) catches collisions at once: the ship words collided across
  two translators and were found only at the fourth ship word; the consistency reviewer's scratch script did it for the
  whole text (2,338 words) – worth a `egypt_tools.py lexmap` command, and the same for every corpus tool.
  (3) `verify` should warn on heavily vocalized `>` lines (the ★ plene rule broke again on a first save).
  (4) Short gates (two-letter roots with up to three prefix letters) hit preposition + homograph (`בלחם`); formulas keyed
  on a one-letter word need boundaries that exclude compounds (`ḥr-r-rʾ`).
  (5) `check_bible_quotes.py` reads a bare "ch:v" after a backticked Hebrew word as Genesis.
- **Guidelines §5.2:** (1) The cognate rule across languages needs its scope stated: it protects loanwords and rare
  technical words (`אָרִיס`), not the common Semitic vocabulary that every corpus uses for its own word (§10.7.4 here).
  (2) A pre-set refrain list must be checked against the transliteration of *both* copies (Amenemope's "better" saying
  and its patron refrain were both mis-delimited in the pre-set renderings). (3) "Read the rank-3/5 translation only
  after drafting" also means: write its wording only with the source open. (4) A passage that will be fixed by its first
  copy should be drafted with both copies' contexts open. (5) The pattern worked: two translators, one per half, each
  resumed part by part; three fresh reviewers – the fidelity reviewer found the one systematic error.
- **Sources:** the BM papyrus photographs for the line ends of 27.17–28.1.
