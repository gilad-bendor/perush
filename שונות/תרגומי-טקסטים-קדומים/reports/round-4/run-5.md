## Key findings
**For the Bible**
- **Ex 12:13, 23.**
  - Išum "walks in the night" and "guides the lad and the girl in safety" (21–22); he "is a door: before them (the Seven) he is shut" (27).
  - In Exodus the blood-marked door keeps out the destroyer; here the merciful god himself is the door.
  - The poem was copied onto house amulets. The Aššur amulet Ist-A 153 and the Tarbiṣu amulet IM 95180 are among Tablet I's manuscripts, and Tarbiṣu has 28–30 and 152–176. The amulets did in Assyrian houses what the blood does in Ex 12.
- **Deut 32:24, 33 in cognates.**
  - The seventh of the Seven is loaded with *imat bašmi* (38) = `חֲמַת פֶּתֶן`: both are the Song of Moses' venom words. Erra's weapons smear themselves with *imat mūti* (7).
  - Deut 32:23–25's arsenal belongs to the Seven: the arrows *uṣṣu* `חֵץ` (90) and the sword (12).
  - Išum's "lad and girl in safety" (22) reverses Deut 32:25.
- **Gen 6:1–4.**
  - Anu mates with Earth, and seven warrior gods are born and named (28–29; *ittabi zikiršun* ↔ Gen 5:2), with no sin and no punishment.
  - The seven sages, the gods' craftsmen, were "sent down" to the Apsû and never brought up (147, 162).
- **Deut 31:17–18 as cosmology.**
  - Marduk rose from his seat once, and the flood came: "the seam of heaven and earth" tore (132–137).
  - If he rises again, the waters, the darkness and the netherworld rise (170–177).
  - Divine absence here is a physical unravelling of the world; Gen 9:11 promises the opposite.
- **The plea and the litany.**
  - Išum to the angry Erra: "Why have you plotted evil … will you not turn back?" (102–103) ↔ Gen 50:20 and Ex 32:12 `שׁוּב מֵחֲרוֹן אַפֶּךָ`.
  - "Let them hear and tremble" (62–74) follows Ex 15:14–16 step by step.
- **Smaller contacts:**
  - the sleeping god (16–20; Ps 44:24, 78:65; 1 Kgs 18:27);
  - *ṭēma šakānu* = Aramaic `שָׂם טְעֵם` (31; Ezra 5:13);
  - *kamān tumri* ~ `כַּוָּנִים` (57; Jer 7:18);
  - *tamlû* `מִלּוֹא` (59);
  - the rusted sword "for lack of slaughter" (91; Ezek 21:14–16);
  - *pānī šakānu* (124; 2 Kgs 12:18);
  - "I am the standard" `נֵס` (114; Ex 17:15);
  - the gods "made to crouch" (*ušarbaṣa*) at Marduk's gate (189; Gen 4:7 `רֹבֵץ`, 3:24);
  - the wooden statue made by craftsmen as "the flesh of the gods" (150–162), against the prophets' mockery (Isa 40:20; Jer 10:3, 9).
- **Inside Akkadian literature:**
  - 172 repeats Gilg. XI 107 almost word for word;
  - 187 repeats Enkidu's death-dream (Gilg. VII 183);
  - 175 echoes Ištar's threat to raise the dead;
  - the noise that keeps the gods awake (41, 73, 82) is Atra-ḫasīs's, in the same Hebrew.

**For the ancient mind**
- The war god who cannot rest, caught between a tired body and a heart that wants battle. He makes love with Mami, using the idiom of Enkidu and Šamḫat.
- The Seven are Erra's weapons (44), which is why he speaks to his weapons and to them in one breath.
- The soldiers' ethos (47–59): the city is for old men and babies, and ash-cake is better than palace bread.
- The gods need quiet (82).
- Weapons rot unused (87–91).
- Erra's self-praise makes him the harmful force in every part of the world (109–118).
- The cult statue is the god's body: its "flesh", its craftsmen, the sages who purify it.
- The closing trick: Erra offers to guard the world, so that its king will leave it.

2. ***ummânu*:** the flood corpus and Erra `אֻמָּן`, against Gudea's Sumerian *um-mi-a* `אָמָּן` (temple corpus). One spelling should win.
3. ***šubtu*:** `מוֹשָׁב` here (Enūma eliš), against the Gilgameš formula *šubat irkalla* `משכן אירכלה` in IS / NE. Kept by formula; noted.

5. ***ṣubātu*:** `בגד` here (141, 181), against GEN / Gilg. XII `שִׂמְלָה`.
6. ***emqu*** `חכם` shares the word with *apkallu* `חכם`; ***malku*** `מלך` shares it with *šarru*.
7. ***nādu* "waterskin"** `נאד` [cognate], against the Descent of Ištar's *ḫalziqqu* `נאד` (98–99).
8. **The flood formula *abūba šakānu*** `חולל מבול` (flood corpus) is kept against *šakānu* = `שָׂם` (the creation rule). The rule could say that a formula wins.
9. **Checked with no split:**
