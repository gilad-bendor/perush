## Key findings
**The ancient mind.** The poem sews a Catalogue *ehoie* ("or like her who…", 1–56) onto a heroic fight with "who also
slew Cycnus" (57) – the seam of oral composition left visible. Alcmene's story is a world of blood-guilt and
supplication (the exile received by "custom", δίκη), of an oath with the gods as witnesses, and of Zeus "planting" a
"warder-off of ruin" for gods and men: twins from two fathers in one night, the mortal and the divine. The shield is
the centre: a world of terror first (Phobos, Eris, the Keres drinking blood, Achlys described feature by feature – the
most physical images of death in Archaic Greek), then of peace (a wedding, dances, ploughing, harvest and vintage – the
farmer's year of the Works and Days), and its images move and sound "as if alive"; Perseus hovers on it touching
nothing; Zeus himself marvels. The battle is dated by the cicada and Sirius (as WD 582–588); Ares is wounded by a
mortal with Athena's help (as Diomedes in Iliad 5); and the robber of the pilgrims to Pytho loses even his grave,
washed away by the Anauros at Apollo's command.

**The Bible in the apparatus** (all shared motifs or the LXX's later vocabulary – Archaic Greece did not know Israel):
- the Archaic panoply and Goliath's (1 Sam 17:5–7: `צִנָּה`, `מִצְחַת נְחֹשֶׁת`, `כּוֹבַע`, `שִׁרְיוֹן`) – the same Aegean
  objects; the poem's own pair of shields, ἀσπίς / σάκος, rendered `מָגֵן` / `צִנָּה` as the Bible pairs them
  (1 Kgs 10:16–17);
- ἤλεκτρον (142) = the LXX's word for `חַשְׁמַל` (Ezek 1:4) and κέγχρος (398) = the LXX's `דֹּחַן` (Ezek 4:9): lexical
  identity with the LXX (kind a); ἀλαλητός `תְּרוּעָה` (the LXX's sister word ἀλαλαγμός, Ps 89:16);
- mourning by tearing the cheeks (243; Lev 19:28, Jer 16:6) and dust on the head (269; Josh 7:6);
- the lyre at the feast (202, 281; Gen 4:21, Isa 5:12); the king as "shepherd of the people" (41; 2 Sam 5:2); the gods
  as witnesses of an oath (20; Gen 31:50); βωμός `מִזְבֵּחַ` where the LXX avoids βωμός for God's altar (kind c).
