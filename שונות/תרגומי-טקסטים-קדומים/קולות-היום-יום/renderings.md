# Shared renderings: the everyday-voices corpus (OB love lyrics, Sumerian proverbs, letters)

Binding for every project of this folder (`corpus.md`). Echo tags follow guidelines §6.4: [cognate], [equivalent], [divergence], [absent]. Fixed by the corpus coordinator (Round 2, run 7, 2026-10-21) **before the first line was drafted**, from the earlier corpora (looked up by string and by lemma: `kol_tools.py lookup`, `kol_tools.py grep all`) and a survey of the three base texts (ORACC lemma frequencies; the formulas found in the transliterations). Translators propose additions in their `project.md` ("Proposals"); the coordinator merges them here (§9). The translator of a formula's first occurrence may revise a pre-set rendering of §3 with a reason; after that it binds and the script checks it.

## 2. Grammar
- **Akkadian** (guidelines §6.3; the Gilgameš and wisdom corpora): preterite = past; present = future / present by context; stative = adjective or nominal clause; precative *lu-/li-* = jussive / cohortative (`אֶשְׂבְּעָה`, `יִשְׂבַּע`); the ventive rendered when it means motion toward the speaker (`בּוֹא אֵלַי`); the **dual and the plural of the body** kept (*šaptāki* `שְׂפָתַיִךְ`); **gender of the address kept** (Hebrew distinguishes *-ka* / *-ki* as the Akkadian does: `דֹּדֶיךָ` / `דֹּדַיִךְ` – this is how the dialogues show who speaks).
- **Speakers of the dialogues** (LAOS 4 01, 15, 16, 18): the tablet does not mark them; Wasserman's attribution (He:) / (She:) / (Chorus:) is an interpretation. In the `>` line: no speaker tags; in the apparatus: who speaks, and why (the gender of the verbs and suffixes).
- **Sumerian** (creation corpus, lament corpus): ḫamṭu = past; marû = participle / future; *ḫe₂-* = jussive (or affirmative, by context: say so); *ga-* = cohortative; *na-* + marû = prohibition `אַל`; *nu-* = `לֹא`; the copula *-am₃* = nominal sentence. **Proverbs are timeless**: a ḫamṭu in a proverb is often a gnomic past (the "it happened once" of a fable); render it as a past and say so where it is gnomic.

## 4. Echo control for this corpus (read before drafting)
1. **The Song of Songs is the danger and the prize.** Its vocabulary is the natural Hebrew of love poetry, so a translator who reaches for "the beautiful word" manufactures a parallel at every line. Rule: every Song-word in a `>` line must be licensed by its real Akkadian counterpart (§3.1, §5); the Song's other words go to the apparatus as [equivalent] / [divergence] / [absent]. The real cognates (*dādu* `דּוֹד`, *dišpu* `דְּבַשׁ`, *šamnu* `שֶׁמֶן`, *šaptu* `שָׂפָה`, *šumēlu* `שְׂמֹאל`, *eršu* `עֶרֶשׂ`, *aḫātu* `אָחוֹת`, *kallatu* `כַּלָּה`, *rēʾû* `רוֹעֶה`, *ṣīḫtu* `צְחוֹק`) are kept visible – they are findings.
2. **Proverbs and Qohelet** likewise: `מָשָׁל`, `חָכְמָה`, `כְּסִיל`, `תּוֹעֵבָה`, `צֶדֶק` / `צַדִּיק`, `רָשָׁע`, `הֶבֶל`, `יִרְאַת`, `מוּסָר`, `עֵצָה` are not to be used for convenience; each is licensed or absent (§5). The "better … than" form is used only where the Sumerian has it.
3. **The letters and the patriarchal stories** (family law, the mother, the brothers, the father's house, debt): render the Sumerian legal words with the law corpus's Hebrew (*e₂ ad-da* = `בֵּית אָב` [equivalent ↔ בראשית יב:א; לא:יד]); mark the overlap, do not build it.
4. **ETCSL's and Wasserman's English** are rank 3 and have their own biblicisms ("righteousness", "abomination", "fool", "wise", "beloved", "garden"): check each against the transliteration (★ rule).
